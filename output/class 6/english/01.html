INTRODUCTION

What are Stative Verbs?

Stative verbs describe a state or a condition rather than an action. They tell us how someone feels, what they possess, or what exists.

🔵

The Verb "To Be"

Used in its stative form to describe conditions and the existence of somebody or something.

is · am · are · was · were · will be
🟢

Has / Have / Had

Used to show illness, possession, relationships, and nature.

has · have · had
🟣

"Been"

Describes a past state which also exists in the present (Present Perfect).

Past (was/were) + Present (is/am/are)

= Present Perfect: been

📌 Key Note

Stative verbs do not describe actions — they describe how things are. "She is tired" is a state; "She is running" is an action.

DEEPER READING

Explore Further

Expand each panel to dive deeper into specific concepts.

🔵 Why can't stative verbs use the -ing form?
▼

Stative verbs describe permanent or ongoing states that don't change moment to moment. Using the continuous (-ing) form implies a temporary or in-progress action, which contradicts the nature of a state.

  • ❌ "She is being a doctor." (wrong — it's a state)
  • ✅ "She is a doctor." (correct — it describes her role/state)
  • ❌ "I am having a car." (wrong for possession)
  • ✅ "I have a car." (correct)
🟢 When does "have" become an action verb?
▼

The word "have" can be stative (showing possession) or dynamic (showing an action). The difference lies in meaning:

  • ✅ Stative: "She has a dog." (possession — she owns a dog)
  • ✅ Dynamic: "She is having breakfast." (action — she is eating)
  • ✅ Stative: "He has a fever." (illness/condition)
  • ✅ Dynamic: "They are having a party." (event/action)
🟣 Understanding "Has Been" vs. "Was"
▼

Both can describe past states, but with different implications:

  • "Was" — a state that started and ended in the past.
  • "Has been" — a state that started in the past and still continues, or its effect is still felt now.

Examples:

  • ✅ "She was sick last year." (fully in the past)
  • ✅ "She has been sick for a week." (started in past, still sick now)
⏳ The Mystery of "Had Had"
▼

"Had had" uses the verb "have" in the past perfect tense. It shows that one past event happened before another past event.

  • Structure: had + past participle of "have" = had had
  • ✅ "She had had a difficult time before the success." — The difficult time was before the success (both in the past).
  • ✅ "By the time we moved, they had had a big house for many years." — They owned the house before the moving event.
📝 Contractions Quick Reference
▼

Common contractions used with stative verbs:

  • is not → isn't
  • are not → aren't
  • was not → wasn't
  • were not → weren't
  • will not → won't
  • has not → hasn't
  • have not → haven't
  • had not → hadn't
  • does not → doesn't
  • do not → don't
  • did not → didn't
