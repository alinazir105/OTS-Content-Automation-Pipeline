Learning Objectives

  • Identify and correctly use Present Simple and Present Continuous tenses for various situations.
  • Differentiate between Present Perfect and Present Perfect Continuous, applying them to actions with present relevance.
  • Understand and apply Past Simple, Past Continuous, Past Perfect, and Past Perfect Continuous tenses for past events.
  • Distinguish between "used to," "would," and "be used to" to describe past habits and current familiarity.
  • Formulate sentences using Future Simple (will) and "be going to" for predictions, intentions, and spontaneous decisions.
  • Recognize and correctly use stative verbs, understanding when they cannot be used in continuous forms.

Key Concepts

⏰

Present Simple

The Present Simple tense is used to describe permanent situations, general truths, laws of nature, repeated or habitual actions, and scheduled events in the future. It focuses on facts and routines rather than actions happening at the moment of speaking.

He works in a hospital.Water boils at 100°C.He gets up at seven every day.The plane leaves at 3:30.
🏃

Present Continuous

The Present Continuous tense is used for temporary situations, changing or developing situations, frequently repeated actions expressing annoyance, actions happening at or around the moment of speaking, and planned arrangements in the near future. It emphasizes ongoing actions.

She is working in Paris this week.The world is getting taller and taller.He is always asking stupid questions.The baby is sleeping at the moment.
⏳

Present Perfect

The Present Perfect tense describes recently completed actions with visible results in the present, states that began in the past and continue up to the present, or experiences that happened at an unspecified time in the past. It connects the past to the present.

I have finished my homework (so I can relax now).She has lived in London for five years (and still lives there).Have you ever visited Rome?
⏱️

Present Perfect Continuous

The Present Perfect Continuous tense is used to describe actions that started in the past and are still continuing in the present, or actions that have recently stopped but have a visible result in the present. It emphasizes the duration of the action.

I have been studying for three hours.She has been crying, that's why her eyes are red.They have been building this bridge since last year.
🗓️

Past Simple

The Past Simple tense is used for completed actions that happened at a specific time in the past. It focuses on the action itself and when it occurred.

I visited Paris last year.She finished her work an hour ago.They played football yesterday.
↔️

Past Continuous

The Past Continuous tense is used to describe an action that was ongoing at a specific point in the past, or two actions happening simultaneously in the past. It often sets the scene for another past action.

I was reading a book when he called.While she was cooking, he was watching TV.At 8 PM yesterday, I was having dinner.
⏪

Past Perfect

The Past Perfect tense is used to describe an action that happened before another action or a specific point in the past. It clarifies the sequence of past events.

By the time I arrived, the movie had already started.She had finished her homework before she went out.He realized he had left his keys at home.
⏮️

Past Perfect Continuous

The Past Perfect Continuous tense is used to describe an action that started in the past and continued up to another point in the past, emphasizing the duration of the earlier action.

I had been waiting for an hour when she finally arrived.He was tired because he had been working all day.They had been living in that house for ten years before they moved.
🔄

Used to / Would / Be used to

Used to describes past habits or states that no longer exist. Would describes repeated past actions (but not states). Be used to describes familiarity with something, meaning 'accustomed to'.

I used to play tennis every weekend (but I don't now).Every summer, we would go to the beach (repeated action).I am used to waking up early (I'm accustomed to it).
🔮

Future Simple (will)

The Future Simple with 'will' is used for spontaneous decisions, predictions without strong evidence, promises, offers, and future facts.

I will help you with your homework (offer).It will rain tomorrow (prediction).I think I'll have a coffee (spontaneous decision).
➡️

Be going to

The 'be going to' future is used for plans and intentions, and predictions based on present evidence.

I am going to visit my grandparents next weekend (plan).Look at those dark clouds; it's going to rain (prediction based on evidence).She is going to study medicine at university (intention).
🧠

Stative Verbs

Stative verbs describe states, conditions, or feelings rather than actions. They are generally not used in continuous (progressive) tenses.

I understand the lesson (not 'I am understanding').She loves chocolate (not 'She is loving').This soup tastes delicious (not 'This soup is tasting').

Quick Check

What tense do we use for general truths?
Present Simple
Which tense emphasizes the duration of a past action leading up to another past action?
Past Perfect Continuous
When do we use \'be going to\' instead of \'will\' for future events?
For plans, intentions, or predictions based on present evidence.
Can stative verbs be used in continuous forms?
Generally no, as they describe states rather than actions.

Glossary

Tense
A grammatical category that expresses time reference, indicating when an action or state occurs (past, present, future).
Aspect
A grammatical category that expresses how an action, event, or state denoted by a verb extends over time (e.g., simple, continuous/progressive, perfect).
Stative Verb
A verb that describes a state of being, a condition, or a feeling, rather than an action. Examples include \'know\', \'love\', \'seem\'.
Auxiliary Verb
A verb that helps the main verb to form different tenses, moods, or voices (e.g., \'be\', \'have\', \'do\').

Worked Examples

Example 1: Complete the sentence: \'She _____ (live) in London for five years.\'
  1. Analyze the sentence: \'for five years\' indicates a duration that started in the past and continues to the present.
  2. Identify the appropriate tense: Present Perfect Continuous is suitable for actions that started in the past and are still ongoing, emphasizing duration.
  3. Formulate the verb: \'has been living\'.
Final: She has been living in London for five years.
Why: The phrase \'for five years\' indicates an action that began in the past and continues up to the present moment, making the Present Perfect Continuous the most appropriate tense to emphasize the duration of her residence.
Example 2: Choose between \'used to\' and \'would\': \'When I was a child, I ____
  1. Analyze the sentence: It describes a repeated action in the past (\'every summer\').
  2. Consider \'used to\': It can describe past habits.
  3. Consider \'would\': It can also describe repeated past actions.
  4. Both are grammatically correct here, but \'would\' is often preferred for repeated actions when the context of \'past time\' is already established.
  5. Formulate the verb: \'would visit\' or \'used to visit\'.
Final: When I was a child, I would visit my grandparents every summer. (Also acceptable: \'used to visit\')
Why: \'Would\' is used here to describe a repeated action in the past, similar to \'used to\'. Since \'visit\' is an action verb, both are possible, but \'would\' adds a slightly more narrative feel to the repeated past event.
