THE INFINITIVE /-ING FORM /-ING/-ED ADJECTIVES

Master the nuances of infinitives, -ing forms, and -ing/-ed adjectives to express actions and feelings with precision!

Learning Objectives

  • Identify and correctly use different forms of the infinitive (present, continuous, perfect).
  • Distinguish between the present and perfect gerund (-ing form) and their uses.
  • Apply rules for using infinitives, -ing forms, and bare infinitives after various verbs, adjectives, and prepositions.
  • Understand when verbs can take either an infinitive or -ing form with or without a change in meaning.
  • Differentiate between -ing and -ed adjectives to describe things and feelings accurately.

Key Concepts

♾️

Infinitive

The base form of a verb, often preceded by "to" (e.g., to run, to eat). It can function as a noun, adjective, or adverb. Infinitives can express purpose, follow certain verbs or adjectives, and appear in various tenses to convey timing relative to the main verb.

I want to learn English.It's important to study.She went to the market to buy vegetables.
🏃‍♀️

Gerund (-ing form)

The -ing form of a verb used as a noun (e.g., swimming, reading). It can be the subject of a sentence, the object of a verb or preposition, or part of a compound noun.

Swimming is my favorite sport.I enjoy reading books.She is good at drawing.
🤩

-ing Adjectives

Adjectives ending in -ing describe the characteristic of a person, thing, or situation that causes a feeling (e.g., interesting, boring, exciting). They describe the 'doer' of the feeling.

The movie was very interesting.It was a boring lecture.What an exciting game!
😌

-ed Adjectives

Adjectives ending in -ed describe a feeling or emotion experienced by a person (e.g., interested, bored, excited). They describe the 'receiver' of the feeling.

I am interested in history.He felt bored during the meeting.We were excited about the trip.
➡️

Verbs followed by Infinitive

Many verbs are typically followed by an infinitive (to + base verb), often expressing desire, intention, or decision (e.g., want, decide, agree, hope, plan).

I want to eat pizza.They decided to leave early.She hopes to visit Paris.
🔄

Verbs followed by Gerund

Some verbs are typically followed by a gerund (-ing form), often expressing preference, avoidance, or completion (e.g., enjoy, finish, avoid, mind, suggest).

He enjoys playing football.Have you finished studying?I avoid eating junk food.
↔️

Verbs followed by Infinitive or Gerund (no change in meaning)

Some verbs can be followed by either an infinitive or a gerund with little or no change in meaning (e.g., start, begin, continue, like, love, hate, prefer).

I started to learn / learning French.She loves to sing / singing.He prefers to walk / walking.
⚠️

Verbs followed by Infinitive or Gerund (change in meaning)

A few verbs change their meaning depending on whether they are followed by an infinitive or a gerund (e.g., remember, forget, stop, try, regret).

I stopped to talk (paused to talk).I stopped talking (finished talking).Remember to lock the door (future action).I remember locking the door (past action).

Fun Facts

The word 'infinitive' comes from the Latin 'infinitivus', meaning 'unlimited' or 'undefined', because it's not limited by person or number.
Gerunds can sometimes be confused with present participles, but remember: if it's acting like a noun, it's a gerund!
English is one of the few languages where the infinitive often includes 'to'. In many other languages, it's just the base form of the verb.

Glossary

Infinitive
The base form of a verb, usually preceded by \'to\', functioning as a noun, adjective, or adverb.
Gerund
The -ing form of a verb used as a noun.
Bare Infinitive
The base form of a verb without \'to\', used after modal verbs or certain other verbs (e.g., let, make, help).
-ing Adjective
An adjective ending in -ing that describes the characteristic of something causing a feeling.
-ed Adjective
An adjective ending in -ed that describes a feeling or emotion experienced by a person.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Complete the sentence: She enjoys ______ (read) books.
  1. Identify the verb \'enjoys\'.
  2. Recall that \'enjoy\' is followed by a gerund (-ing form).
  3. Convert \'read\' to its gerund form: \'reading\'.
Final: She enjoys reading books.
Why: The verb \'enjoy\' is always followed by a gerund (the -ing form of a verb acting as a noun).
Example 2: Choose the correct adjective: The movie was very (boring/bored).
  1. Determine what the adjective is describing: \'the movie\'.
  2. Consider if the movie *has* a characteristic that causes a feeling, or if the movie *experiences* a feeling.
  3. Movies cause feelings, they don\'t experience them. Therefore, an -ing adjective is needed.
Final: The movie was very boring.
Why: \'-ing\' adjectives describe the characteristic of something that causes a feeling. The movie itself is \'boring\' (it causes boredom).
Example 3: Rewrite using an infinitive: I want that I learn to play the guitar.
  1. Identify the desire: \'I want\'.
  2. Recognize that \'want\' is followed by an infinitive.
  3. Form the infinitive: \'to learn\'.
  4. Combine with the object: \'to play the guitar\'.
Final: I want to learn to play the guitar.
Why: The verb \'want\' is typically followed by an infinitive (to + base verb) to express a desire or intention.
