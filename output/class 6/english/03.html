ACTIVE AND PASSIVE VOICE

Unlock the power of sentences by learning how to shift focus between the doer and the receiver of an action with active and passive voice!

Unit 3 4 Activities Interactive
Your Progress 0%

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the fundamental difference between active and passive voice.
  • Learn to transform active sentences into passive sentences across various tenses.
  • Identify appropriate contexts for using the passive voice, such as when the agent is unknown or unimportant.
  • Master the correct usage of 'by' for agents and 'with' for instruments or materials in passive constructions.
  • Recognize and apply advanced passive forms, including reporting passives and passives with modal verbs.

Key Concepts

🗣️

Active Voice

Active voice is a grammatical construction where the subject of the sentence performs the action of the verb. It emphasizes the doer of the action, making sentences direct, clear, and often more concise. It's commonly used when the agent is important.

The student wrote the essay.Birds build nests.She sings a beautiful song.He fixed the broken chair.The dog chased the ball.
👂

Passive Voice

Passive voice is a grammatical construction where the subject of the sentence receives the action of the verb. It emphasizes the receiver of the action or the action itself, often when the doer is unknown, unimportant, or obvious. It typically uses a form of 'to be' + past participle.

The essay was written by the student.Nests are built by birds.A beautiful song is sung by her.The broken chair was fixed by him.The ball was chased by the dog.
👤

Agent

In a passive sentence, the agent is the doer of the action, often introduced by the preposition 'by'.

The cake was eaten by *John*.The letter was written by *my mother*.
🛠️

Instrument/Material

In a passive sentence, an instrument or material used to perform the action is often introduced by the preposition 'with'.

The window was broken *with* a hammer.The wall was painted *with* a brush.
💬

Modal Passives

Passive constructions that include a modal verb (e.g., can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would) before 'be' and the past participle. They express possibility, necessity, permission, etc., in a passive context.

The work must be finished by Friday.The problem can be solved.The package might be delivered tomorrow.Rules should be followed.
➡️

Transitive Verb

A verb that takes a direct object. Only transitive verbs can be used to form passive sentences because they have an object that can become the subject of the passive sentence.

She *reads* a book.He *eats* an apple.They *built* a house.
🚫

Intransitive Verb

A verb that does not take a direct object. Intransitive verbs cannot be used to form passive sentences.

The bird *sings*.He *sleeps*.She *arrived* late.
📰

Reporting Passives

A type of passive construction used to report what is said, thought, believed, known, etc., often using verbs like 'say', 'think', 'believe', 'report', 'know'.

It is believed that he is innocent.He is said to be a genius.The problem is known to be complex.

Fun Facts

The passive voice is often used in scientific and academic writing to maintain objectivity and focus on the action or results rather than the researcher.
Journalism sometimes uses the passive voice to avoid assigning blame, especially when the perpetrator is unknown or to soften a statement.
While often criticized for being wordy, the passive voice is essential for certain rhetorical effects and grammatical constructions.
The phrase 'mistakes were made' is a classic example of using the passive voice to avoid taking direct responsibility.

Summary

  • Active voice emphasizes the doer of an action, making sentences direct and clear.
  • Passive voice emphasizes the receiver of the action or the action itself.
  • It is often used when the doer is unknown, unimportant, or to maintain objectivity.
  • Key elements of passive voice include a form of \'to be\' + past participle.
  • The agent (doer) in a passive sentence is often introduced by \'by\', while instruments or materials are introduced by \'with\'.
  • Understanding both active and passive voices allows for versatile and effective communication.

Quick Check

What is the main difference between active and passive voice?
Active voice emphasizes the doer of the action, while passive voice emphasizes the receiver of the action or the action itself.
When is it appropriate to use the passive voice?
When the doer of the action is unknown, unimportant, or obvious, or when you want to emphasize the action or the receiver.

Glossary

Active Voice
A sentence structure where the subject performs the action.
Passive Voice
A sentence structure where the subject receives the action.
Agent
The doer of the action in a passive sentence, usually introduced by \'by\'.
Past Participle
A verb form used to create perfect tenses and passive voice (e.g., \'written\', \'eaten\', \'broken\').

Worked Examples

Example 1: Transform the sentence \'The chef cooked the delicious meal\' into pas
  1. Identify the object of the active sentence: \'the delicious meal\'. This becomes the subject of the passive sentence.
  2. Identify the verb: \'cooked\'. Use a form of \'to be\' (was/were) + past participle (\'cooked\'). Since \'meal\' is singular and past tense, use \'was cooked\'.
  3. Identify the subject of the active sentence: \'The chef\'. This becomes the agent in the passive sentence, introduced by \'by\'.
  4. Combine these parts: \'The delicious meal was cooked by the chef.\'
Final: The delicious meal was cooked by the chef.
Why: The original subject (\'the chef\') is now the agent, and the original object (\'the delicious meal\') is now the subject, receiving the action. The verb is transformed into \'to be\' + past participle.
Example 2: Identify whether the sentence \'The ball was hit by the player\' is ac
  1. Identify the subject: \'The ball\'.
  2. Identify the verb: \'was hit\'. This is a form of \'to be\' + past participle.
  3. Identify the doer of the action: \'the player\', introduced by \'by\'.
  4. Since the subject (\'the ball\') is receiving the action, and the doer is introduced by \'by\', it is passive voice.
Final: Passive Voice
Why: The subject \'the ball\' is receiving the action of being hit, rather than performing it. The structure \'was + past participle\' and the presence of \'by the player\' indicate passive voice.
