The Future Tense

Learn how to talk about things that will happen in the future! We'll explore how to build affirmative, negative, and question sentences using WILL.

Start Learning → Take Quiz 🏆

What is the Future Tense?

The future simple tense is used to talk about actions that will happen in the future. We use the helping verb "will" with the first form of the main verb.

Subject + will + Verb (1st Form) + Object

Examples of All Three Forms

✅ Affirmative ❌ Negative ❓ Interrogative
I will walk to school. I will not (won't) walk to school. Will I walk to school?
We will play a game. We will not (won't) play a game. Will we play a game?
You will read a book. You will not (won't) read a book. Will you read a book?
They will visit the zoo. They will not (won't) visit the zoo. Will they visit the zoo?
He will finish his homework. He will not (won't) finish his homework. Will he finish his homework?
Ali will play cricket. Ali will not (won't) play cricket. Will Ali play cricket?
✅

Affirmative

Use will + verb to say something positive will happen.
"She will sing a song."

❌

Negative

Use will not / won't to say something will NOT happen.
"She will not sing."

❓

Interrogative

Put Will at the start to ask a question.
"Will she sing a song?"

🔍

Sentence Structure

Let's break down how each type of future tense sentence is built — piece by piece!

✅ Affirmative Sentence
Subject + will + Verb (1st form) + Object
Example
I will play cricket.
❌ Negative Sentence
Subject + will + not + Verb (1st form) + Object
Shortcut: will not = won't
Example
I will not (won't) play cricket.
❓ Interrogative Sentence
Will + Subject + Verb (1st form) + Object ?
Example
Will I play cricket?

💡 Key Tip: "will" is the same for ALL subjects!

I will ✓ He will ✓ She will ✓ They will ✓ We will ✓ It will ✓

Unlike other tenses, "will" never changes — no "wills" or "willing"!
