What Are Adjectives?

Adjective — A word we use to describe nouns (things and people) or pronouns.
They tell us more about what something looks like, feels like, sounds like, tastes like, or smells like!

🎯 Adjectives describe nouns

Nouns are things and people. Adjectives tell us what they are like:

Sara is tall. You're crazy! That isn't a beautiful picture.

🔄 Same for singular AND plural

Adjectives do not change their form — they stay the same whether there is one thing or many:

It's a nice colour. They're nice colours.
⭐ No -s added to adjectives, even with plural nouns!

💬 Look at the example!

Boy says:

"What a beautiful picture! It's colourful and interesting."

Girl says:

"It isn't beautiful! It's strange and ugly."

The highlighted words (beautiful, colourful, interesting, strange, ugly) are all adjectives — they describe the picture!

👁️

Where Do Adjectives Go?

📍 Position 1: Before a Noun

Adjective comes directly before the noun it describes:

I like funny films.
We ate a big breakfast.
She has a red bag.
It was a long journey.

📍 Position 2: After "be"

Adjective comes after is / am / are / was / were:

It is red.
He was nice.
Sara is tall.
You are crazy!

🔗 Position 3: After linking verbs

We also use adjectives after become, get, seem, and verbs of sensation:

look feel taste smell sound become get seem
You seem sad today.
I'm getting cold.
The soup tastes hot.
She looks tired.

📊 Adjective Positions — Summary Table

Position Pattern Example
Before noun Adjective + Noun I like funny films.
After be Subject + be + Adjective She is tall.
After linking verbs Subject + verb + Adjective You seem sad.
