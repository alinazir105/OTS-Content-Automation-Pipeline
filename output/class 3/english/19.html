Degrees of Adjectives

Discover how adjectives change to compare things! Learn when to say tall, taller, or tallest — and use them like a grammar star! ⭐

📖

What Are Degrees of Adjectives?

Adjectives describe the quality of a noun — like size, colour, or how something looks or feels. Degrees of adjectives show comparisons, telling us if something is bigger, smaller, better, or worse than something else.

💡

There are three degrees of adjectives: Positive (no comparison), Comparative (comparing two things), and Superlative (comparing three or more things to find the highest or lowest).

😊

Positive Degree

This is the basic form of the adjective, used when there is no comparison.

Ali is tall.
⚖️

Comparative Degree

Used when comparing two things. Add -er or use more before the adjective.

Ali is taller than Ahmed.
🏆

Superlative Degree

Used when comparing more than two things. Add -est or use most before the adjective.

Ali is the tallest boy in the class.

🔑 Key Rules to Remember

Comparative (-er or more)

Short adjectives: add -er
tall → taller
smart → smarter

Long adjectives: use more
beautiful → more beautiful

Superlative (-est or most)

Short adjectives: add -est
tall → tallest
smart → smartest

Long adjectives: use most
beautiful → most beautiful
