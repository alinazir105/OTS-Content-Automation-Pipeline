🎯 Introduction

Understanding what the Past Continuous Tense means

📌 What does it mean?

We use the past continuous tense to say that something was happening at a specific point in the past — an action that was in progress at that time.

Think of it like this...

🎬 Imagine pressing PAUSE on a video. What you see on screen — that's the past continuous!

💡 Quick Examples

Example 1

She was writing an email.

Example 2

They were playing cricket.

Example 3

It was snowing last night.

📖 The Formula

How to build a past continuous sentence

Subject + was / were + Verb¹ + ing + Object

Subject → Helping Verb (was/were) → Main Verb + ing → Object

⚡

Key Rule: was OR were?

Use was with: I, he, she, it, Ali (singular)  |  Use were with: we, you, they (plural)

✅ Affirmative (Positive) Sentences

Making positive statements in the past continuous

SubjectHelping VerbVerb + ingObject
I / he / she / it / Aliwasplayingcricket.
We / you / theywereplayingcricket.

✅ With "was"

She was writing an email.

He was running in the park.

It was raining all day.

I was dreaming about a flying fish.

✅ With "were"

They were playing cricket.

We were studying together.

You were sleeping.

We were having some problems.

❌ Negative Sentences

Making negative statements — add "not" after was/were

SubjectHelping Verb + notVerb + ingObject
I / he / she / it / Aliwas not (wasn't)playingcricket.
We / you / theywere not (weren't)playingcricket.
💬

Contractions (Short Forms)

was not → wasn't   |   were not → weren't
Both mean the same thing! Contractions are used in spoken English.

❌ With "wasn't"

The phone wasn't ringing yesterday.

She wasn't watching a film.

❌ With "weren't"

They weren't helping us.

You weren't listening to me.

❓ Interrogative (Questions)

Asking questions — put was/were FIRST

Was / Were + Subject + Verb + ing + Object ?
Helping VerbSubjectVerb + ingObject
WasI / he / she / it / Aliplayingcricket?
Werewe / you / theyplayingcricket?

❓ Questions with "Was"

Was it raining at six o'clock?

Was she watching a film?

❓ Questions with "Were"

Were you wearing jeans yesterday?

Were they playing football?

🔗 Past Continuous + Past Simple

Using both tenses together to describe interruptions

📌

The Rule

Use past continuous for the long/background action. Use past simple for the short action that interrupted it.

graph LR A["Long Action\n(Past Continuous)\nwas/were + Verb+ing"] -->|"interrupted by"| B["Short Action\n(Past Simple)\nVerb 2nd form"] B --> C["Connected with WHEN"] style A fill:#48DBFB,color:#222 style B fill:#FF6B6B,color:#fff style C fill:#F9CA24,color:#222

When I was sleeping, Sofia woke me up.

→ Sofia woke me up when I was sleeping.

When he was running, he stumbled over a stone.

→ He stumbled over a stone when he was running.

When we were studying, the power went off.

→ The power went off when we were studying.

When my mum was cooking, the doorbell rang.

→ The doorbell rang when my mum was cooking.

When you were going to office, you came across your friend.

→ You came across your friend when you were going to your office.
✨

Two Actions at the Same Time — Use "AND"

We can also join two past continuous verbs with and to show two actions happening at the same time:

I was doing my homework and my grandma was making soup.
