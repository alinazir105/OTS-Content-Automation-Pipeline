📖 What are May and Might?

Modal verbs that show possibility

May and Might are modal verbs. We use them to talk about things that are possible — things that could happen but we are not sure!

🌤️

MAY

Shows 50% chance
Quite possible!

⛅

MIGHT

Shows 30% chance
Less likely!

SENTENCE STRUCTURE

Subject + may / might + Base form of verb

📝 Examples

They may win the match. (50% chance — quite possible)

It might rain tonight. (30% chance — less likely)

She may want help. (Subject + may + base verb)

It might land here. (Subject + might + base verb)

💭 Using May & Might for States

May and Might can also describe states using "be" or "have"

STRUCTURE FOR STATES

Subject + may / might + be / have + extra words

🤒

She may be sick.

🍀

You might be lucky.

💰

She may have money.

📱

Sami might have a cellphone.

❌ Negative Forms

may not (mayn't) and might not (mightn't)

To make a negative sentence, we add not after may or might:

✅ Positive

She may be sick.

You might be lucky.

She may have money.

Sami might have cellphone.

❌ Negative

She may not be sick.

You might not be lucky.

She may not have money.

Sami might not have cellphone.

mayn't = may not 🔑
mightn't = might not 🔑

🙋 May for Permission

We can use "may" in questions to ask for permission

We use May I...? to politely ask for permission to do something.

May + I + base verb + rest of sentence + ?

May I go to play?
Of course. Go ahead! 😊
May I listen to a song?
Yes, you may! 🎵

May I use the binoculars now? 🔭

May I go to the playground? 🏃
