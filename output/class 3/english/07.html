🧠 Understanding Verb Patterns

Two verbs together — what form does the second verb take?

🎯 Rule 1 — Verb + -ing

When we use two verbs together, the second verb sometimes takes the -ing form. This happens with:

love like hate enjoy keep

Use the -ing form to express likes and dislikes.

  • I love reading books.
  • We like watching movies.
  • She hates waiting.
  • They enjoy singing songs.
  • He keeps trying.

🎯 Rule 2 — Verb + to + base form

After some verbs, we use to + base form. This happens with:

need want would like plan hope try

Use to + base to talk about actions you prefer or wish to do.

  • I need to prepare for the exam.
  • We want to eat pizza.
  • They plan to visit the zoo.
  • She hopes to get good grades.
  • I would like to have a cup of tea.

💡 Memory Tip

Think of it this way: FEELINGS (love, like, hate, enjoy) → use -ing. WISHES & PLANS (want, need, hope, plan) → use to + verb.

love / like / hate / enjoy / keep

🌟 📋 🙏
