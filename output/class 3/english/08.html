Introduction

The word make is a very useful English word! We use it in three main patterns to talk about:

😊

Make + Adjective

How things change how someone feels or looks.

e.g. makes me happy

⚡

Make + Verb

How one action causes another action to happen.

e.g. made me jump

🧱

Made of + Noun

What material something is made from.

e.g. made of plastic

Pattern 1: Make + Somebody/Something + Adjective

📖 What does it mean?

We use make somebody or something + adjective to describe how an event, action, person or object changes another person or object.

make / makes / made + somebody / something + adjective

✅ Examples:

  • This music is making me happy.
  • Salty food makes people thirsty.
  • That spider made me scared.
  • Exercise makes me tired.
  • Hot weather makes me tired.
  • Yoga makes people calm and relaxed.

📝 Common adjectives used with MAKE:

FeelingExample sentence
happy 😊Music makes me happy.
sad 😢Failure makes me sad.
angry 😠Bad drivers make him angry.
scared 😨Spiders make her scared.
tired 😴Exercise makes me tired.
relaxed 😌Music makes her relaxed.
thirsty 🥤Salty food makes people thirsty.
yellow 🟡They make everything yellow.
wet 💧Water makes things wet.
💡 Remember: Use makes with he/she/it/singular nouns, and make with I/you/we/they/plural nouns.
📌 The adjective comes after the object: "This music makes me happy" — NOT "This music makes happy me."
⚡

Pattern 2: Make + Somebody/Something + Verb

📖 What does it mean?

We use make somebody or something + verb to describe how an event or action creates a second event or action. The verb after the object is always the base form (no "to", no "-ing").

make / makes / made / is making + somebody / something + base verb

✅ Examples:

  • That made me jump. (NOT: made me to jump / made me jumping)
  • The book is making her smile.
  • The clowns are making them laugh.
  • The onions are making the wheel spin.
  • The flowers are making her sneeze.
  • Chocolate makes me ill. (ill = adjective here)
⚠️ Common mistake: Never say "make me to jump" or "make me jumping". Always use the base form: "make me jump".

🎬 Common verbs used:

cry laugh dance smile sneeze spin jump scream sing run
🧱

Pattern 3: Be Made of + Noun (Material)

📖 What does it mean?

We use be made of + noun to describe objects and the material they are made from.

It / They + is / are + made of + material

✅ Examples:

  • It's made of plastic. 🕷️
  • The table is made of wood. 🪵
  • The keys are made of metal. 🔑
  • The gloves are made of wool. 🧤
  • The jacket is made of leather. 🧥
  • The glass is made of glass. 🥃
