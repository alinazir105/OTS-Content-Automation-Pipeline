Demonstratives

Learn to point to people, animals, and things using the right words — this, that, these, and those!

📍 THIS 📍 THAT 📍 THESE 📍 THOSE
💡 What Are Demonstratives?

Demonstratives are words that point to people, animals, or things. They help us show which one we are talking about.

Think of them as a "pointing finger" in language! Instead of physically pointing, we use words to show if something is near us or far from us, and whether it's one thing or many things.

👆

Pointing Near

When something is close to you, use this or these.

"This apple is sweet." (apple is in your hand)

👉

Pointing Far

When something is far from you, use that or those.

"That mountain is tall." (mountain is far away)

1️⃣

One Thing (Singular)

For one person, animal, or thing, use this (near) or that (far).

"This dog is mine." / "That car is fast."

🔢

Many Things (Plural)

For many people, animals, or things, use these (near) or those (far).

"These books are heavy." / "Those birds sing."

REMEMBER!

  • Use this / these for things near you.
  • Use that / those for things far from you.
  • This and that are for ONE thing (singular).
  • These and those are for MANY things (plural).
📋 Two Types of Demonstratives

🔵 Demonstrative Pronouns

These are used on their own to refer to something specific and replace the noun in a sentence. They show which person or thing is being talked about.

  • This is my favorite book. (Refers to something near)
  • That looks amazing! (Refers to something far)
  • These are delicious cookies. (Refers to things near)
  • Those are the shoes I like. (Refers to things far)

🟦 Demonstrative Adjectives

These words are used before a noun to show which one is being referred to. They describe a noun by pointing it out.

  • This chair is comfortable. (near chair)
  • That book is old. (far book)
  • These pens are new. (near pens)
  • Those apples are fresh. (far apples)

🔑 Quick Difference

Demonstrative PRONOUN:

"This is my book."
(This = the book itself)

Demonstrative ADJECTIVE:

"This book is mine."
(This describes 'book')

🔤 The Four Demonstrative Words
THIS
📍 Near · 1 thing
"This pencil is mine."
(The pencil is near me.)
THAT
📍 Far · 1 thing
"That bag is hers."
(The bag is not near me.)
THESE
📍 Near · Many things
"These toys are fun!"
(The toys are near me.)
THOSE
📍 Far · Many things
"Those birds are flying."
(The birds are far from me.)
Word Distance Number Example
This Near Singular "This tree is tall."
That Far Singular "That house looks old."
These Near Plural "These mushrooms are interesting."
Those Far Plural "Those birds are beautiful."

📝 Summary

What We Learned Today

🔵 Demonstrative Pronouns

Stand alone and replace the noun. They refer to something specific without naming it directly.

e.g. "This is my book."

🟦 Demonstrative Adjectives

Come before a noun to describe which one. They modify the noun directly.

e.g. "This book is mine."

WordNumberDistanceExample (Adjective)Example (Pronoun)

that singular far "That house is big." "That is big."
these plural near "These flowers smell nice." "These smell nice."
those plural far "Those mountains are tall." "Those are tall."
