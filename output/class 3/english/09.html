Introduction

In this lesson, we will learn about Verbs of Sensation — special verbs that describe what our senses notice! Our five senses are sight 👀, hearing 👂, smell 👃, taste 👅, and touch ✋. We use special grammar patterns with these verbs in English.

👀
SEE
Used for things you notice with your eyes
I can see a bird.
👂
HEAR
Used for sounds you notice with your ears
I can hear music.
👃
SMELL
Used for scents you notice with your nose
I can smell flowers.
📘

See, Hear & Smell — Verb Pattern

We use see, smell, and hear to talk about the sights, smells, and sounds that we notice. We usually use "can" with these verbs of sensation.

Pattern: Subject + can + Verb of Sensation + Object + -ing form
Example: I can see a man running.
Verb of Sensation Object –ing Form
I can see a man running.
I can hear music playing.
I can smell food cooking.
✨

Look + Adjective

We use look, taste, smell, feel, sound, and seem to give an opinion about an object, activity, or person. To give an opinion about a feeling, we use a verb of sensation + an adjective.

Pattern: Subject + Verb of Sensation + Adjective
Example: You look happy.
Subject Verb of Sensation Adjective
Youlookhappy.
The skylooksblue.
Shelookstired.
Helooksexcited.
Itlooksdelicious.
🔍

Look Like + Noun

To say that a thing or person is similar to another thing or person, we use a verb of sensation + like + noun.

Pattern: Subject + Verb of Sensation + like + Noun
Example: She looks like a princess.
Subject Verb of Sensation Like Noun
Youlooklikea teacher.
Shelookslikea princess.
Helookslikea superhero.
The drawinglookslikea flower.
That buildinglookslikea castle.
Remember the difference:
✅ You look sad. (adjective — opinion/feeling)
✅ You look like a teacher. (noun — similarity to something)
