🕰️ When do we use Past Simple?

We use the Past Simple Tense for actions that happened in the past — things that are finished and done. We often use special time words to show when something happened.

  • I played cricket yesterday.
  • She cooked dinner last night.
  • They went to school two days ago.
💡

In Past Simple, we use the 2nd form of the verb (also called past tense form) in affirmative sentences. For negatives and questions, we use did / didn't + 1st form (base verb).

📖

What is Past Simple?

✅ Affirmative

Subject + Verb 2nd Form + Object
I/he/she/it/we/you/they + played + cricket.

Use the 2nd form of the verb directly after the subject.

❌ Negative

Subject + did not (didn't) + Verb 1st Form + Object
I/he/she/it/we/you/they + didn't + play + cricket.

Use didn't + base form of the verb.

❓ Interrogative

Did + Subject + Verb 1st Form + Object + ?
Did I/he/she/it/we/you/they + play + cricket?

Start with Did and use the base form of the verb.

⏰ Time Expressions

We often use these words with Past Simple to show when something happened:

yesterday
yesterday morning
yesterday afternoon
last night
last week
in September
two days ago
in 1988

📝 Example Sentences

  • I played cricket yesterday. ✅
  • She cooked the fish. ✅
  • He didn't play cricket. ❌
  • We didn't go to school. ❌
  • Did you play cricket? ❓
  • Did she study yesterday? ❓

Stative Verbs — was / were

The verb "to be" in Past Simple

The verb "to be" in past simple has two forms: was and were. We use them for states — the existence or condition of someone or something.

Subject + was / were + Extra words

✅ Affirmative

SubjectVerbExtra Words
I / he / she / itwasready.
We / you / theywereready.

❌ Negative

SubjectVerbExtra Words
I / he / she / itwas not (wasn't)ready.
We / you / theywere not (weren't)ready.

❓ Interrogative

Stative VerbSubjectExtra Words
WasI / he / she / itready?
Werewe / you / theyready?

🗣️ Examples with was / were

  • I was here.
  • You were right.
  • It wasn't difficult.
  • They weren't late.
  • Were you OK? Yes, I was.
  • Was he in the garden? No, he wasn't.
📌

Note: Has and have indicate possession or relationships in the present while Had is used in the past.

Regular Verbs

How to form the 2nd form of Regular Verbs

We form the 2nd form of regular verbs by adding -d, -ed or -ied to the verb. The 3rd form is made the same way.

  • play → played (add -ed)
  • like → liked (add -d)
  • study → studied (change y to i, add -ed)
  • chat → chatted (double consonant, add -ed)
