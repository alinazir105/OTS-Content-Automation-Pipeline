📖 What is Present Perfect Tense?

Understanding when and why we use it

Definition

We use the present perfect when we have a present result of a past action, or for something that started in the past and has finished in the present with a result.

⏰

Past Action

Something happened at some point in the past. We don't need to say exactly when it happened.

I lost my keys.
→ The losing happened in the past
✅

Present Result

Because of that past action, there is a result or effect that we can see or feel right now.

I have lost my keys.
→ I can't find them NOW!

📐 The Formula

Subject + have / has + Verb 3rd Form + Object
💡

Remember: Use have with I, we, you, they and has with he, she, it, Ali

👁️ Visual Explanation

See how sentences are built with Present Perfect Tense

✅ Affirmative Sentences

The subject does something — we use have/has + 3rd form of verb

Subject Helping Verb Verb (3rd Form) Object
I / We / You / They have played cricket.
He / She / It / Ali has played cricket.

❌ Negative Sentences

Add not after have/has (or use contractions: haven't / hasn't)

Subject Helping Verb + not Verb (3rd Form) Object
I / We / You / They have not (haven't) played cricket.
He / She / It / Ali has not (hasn't) played cricket.

❓ Interrogative (Question) Sentences

Move the helping verb to the front and add ?

Helping Verb Subject Verb (3rd Form) Object + ?
Have I / We / You / They played cricket?
Has He / She / It / Ali played cricket?

💡 Examples

Real sentences to understand present results of past actions

From the Textbook

I've lost my glasses.
= I can't find my glasses NOW.
She's visited Paris three times.
= She knows what Paris is like.
We have had lunch.
= We are not hungry now.
Sami has cleaned the floor.
= The floor is clean now.
The flowers have grown a lot.
= The flowers are tall now.
The film has started.
= It is currently playing.
