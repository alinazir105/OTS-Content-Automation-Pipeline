☀️ 6.1 Day and Night

🌍 Our planet Earth is always moving. It rotates, or turns, once every 24 hours. This rotation causes day and night — one side faces the Sun (daytime) while the other faces away (night time).

🌍 Interactive Earth Model

Drag to rotate the Earth and see day and night sides!

🌍

Earth rotates on its axis every 24 hours, causing day and night!

🌅

Sunrise – East

The Sun appears to rise in the east every morning because Earth rotates from west to east.

☀️

Midday – High Overhead

At midday, the Sun is highest in the sky. Its light shines most directly on Earth's surface.

🌇

Sunset – West

In the evening, the Sun sets in the west. Earth has rotated away from the Sun on that side.

⚡ KEY FACT

Earth rotates from west to east, which makes the Sun appear to move from east to west. The Sun doesn't actually move — Earth does!

🇵🇰 Day and Night in Pakistan

🌞

Daytime in Pakistan

During the day, Pakistan faces the Sun, making it bright and warm. The Sun rises in the east behind the mountains in the north of Pakistan.

🌙

Night time in Pakistan

During the night, Pakistan is turned away from the Sun, making it dark. It will be daytime again in 24 hours when Earth completes one full rotation.

🌓 How Shadows Change

Shadows change with the position of the Sun in the sky throughout the day. Here's how:

Time of Day Sun's Position Shadow Length Shadow Direction
🌅 Sunrise Low in the East Very Long Stretching West-Northwest
🌤️ Morning Rising in the East Long, getting shorter Pointing West
☀️ Midday High Overhead Very Short Directly beneath object
🌤️ Afternoon Descending in West Long, getting longer Pointing East
🌇 Sunset Low in the West Very Long Stretching East-Northeast

💡 Why? When the Sun is low in the sky, its light hits objects at a low angle, creating long shadows. When it's high overhead at midday, light comes straight down, making shadows very short!

🌐 Earth's Axis & Rotation
🏀

What is an Axis?

An axis is an imaginary line through the center of a spinning object. Think of a ball spinning on your fingertip — the line from your fingertip to the top of the ball is its axis!

🌍

Earth's Tilted Axis

Earth rotates around its own axis, which is tilted at 23.4°. The North Pole is at the top and the South Pole is at the bottom of this axis.

⏰

One Full Rotation = 24 Hours

Earth takes 24 hours (one full day) to complete one rotation on its axis. This rotation causes day and night to alternate.

🌍 Earth's Tilt Effect

In Pakistan, Earth's 23.4° tilt affects the length of days and nights throughout the year. In summer, days are longer. In winter, nights are longer. This is all because of Earth's tilted axis!

🍂 Why Do Seasons Change?

🌏 Earth doesn't just rotate on its axis — it also revolves around the Sun! Earth follows a circular path called an orbit. It takes 365 days (one year) to complete one full orbit. As Earth moves, different parts receive different amounts of sunlight, causing seasons to change.

🔄

Revolution vs Rotation

Rotation: Earth spinning on its own axis (24 hours = 1 day)

Revolution: Earth orbiting the Sun (365 days = 1 year)

📐

Why the Tilt Matters

Because Earth's axis is tilted, different hemispheres receive more or less direct sunlight at different times of year — this is what creates seasons!

🌞 Sun's Path Changes!

In summer, the Sun is higher in the sky → longer days and more warmth.
In winter, the Sun is lower in the sky → shorter days and less warmth.

🇵🇰 Seasons in Pakistan

Pakistan experiences four distinct seasons. Each season has special weather and activities!

🌞

Summer

May – August

Pakistan tilts towards the Sun, receiving more direct sunlight. Especially hot in Sindh and Punjab. Great time for mangoes! 🥭

❄️

Winter

November – February

Pakistan tilts away from the Sun, getting less sunlight. Northern areas receive snowfall, while the plains have cold, dry weather. ⛄

🌸

Spring

March – April

A transition season with moderate temperatures. Flowers bloom, trees get new leaves, and the weather is pleasant! 🌺

🍁

Autumn

September – October

Another transition season moving toward winter. Leaves change color and fall, and temperatures become cooler. 🍂

🌙 The Moon

🌙 The Moon is shaped like a ball and revolves around Earth. Half of the Moon is always lit by the Sun, while the other half remains dark. As the Moon orbits Earth, we see different amounts of the lit side — these are called the phases of the Moon!

🔄

Moon's Orbit

The Moon takes about 29.5 days (approximately 4 weeks) to complete one full orbit around Earth. This is called a lunar cycle.

💡

Why Shapes Seem to Change

The Moon doesn't actually change shape! We just see different parts of the lit side as the Moon moves around Earth. It's like seeing different sides of the same ball.

🕌

Importance in Islam

In Pakistan, the Islamic calendar follows the lunar cycle. A new Islamic month begins when the new Moon is sighted. This determines when Ramadan and Eid begin!

🌕 The 8 Phases of the Moon

The Moon has 8 main phases that change as it orbits Earth over about 29.5 days:

🌑
1. New Moon
The Moon is not visible from Earth
🌒
2. Waxing Crescent
A small curved part is visible
🌓
3. First Quarter
Half of the Moon is visible
🌔
4. Waxing Gibbous
More than half is visible
🌕
5. Full Moon
The entire Moon is visible
🌖
6. Waning Gibbous
The lit portion starts to shrink
🌗
7. Last Quarter
Half of the Moon visible again
🌘
8. Waning Crescent
Small curved part before cycle restarts

🕌 The Lunar Cycle & Islamic Calendar
📅

What is the Lunar Cycle?

The lunar cycle is the time it takes for the Moon to go through all 8 of its phases. It takes approximately 29.5 days to complete one full cycle.

🕌

Islamic Calendar

The Islamic calendar is a lunar calendar. Each new month begins when the new Moon (crescent) is first sighted. This is how we determine the start of Ramadan and Eid!

👁️

Moon Sighting in Pakistan

In Pakistan, religious authorities look for the crescent Moon to determine the start of important Islamic months. This centuries-old tradition connects us to the lunar cycle.

🌙 Fun Fact

The word "month" actually comes from the word "Moon"! Ancient civilizations tracked time by watching the Moon's phases — just as the Islamic calendar still does today!

📖 Key Vocabulary

Learn these important science words from Unit 6:

🔄 Rotate
To spin or turn around a central point or axis. Earth rotates once every 24 hours.
🌐 Axis
An imaginary line through the centre of a spinning object. Earth's axis is tilted at 23.4°.
🌍 Revolve
To move around another object in a circular path. Earth revolves around the Sun in 365 days.
⭕ Orbit
The path an object takes as it moves around another object. Earth's orbit around the Sun takes one year.
🌙 Phase
Any shape of the Moon that we see from Earth. There are 8 main phases in the lunar cycle.
📅 Lunar Cycle
The time it takes the Moon to go through all its phases — about 29.5 days.

🎯 Learning Activities

🌓 Activity 1: Shadow Tracking

Throughout the day, observe how shadows change from morning to evening.

  1. Go outside at sunrise and mark where your shadow falls
  2. Check again at mid-morning — is the shadow shorter?
  3. At midday, see how short your shadow is
  4. In the afternoon, watch the shadow grow again
  5. At sunset, your shadow will be longest again — but pointing the other way!
  6. Draw all your observations and write the times next to each shadow

🌙 Activity 2: Moon Observation

Over a full month, become a Moon scientist!

  1. Get a notebook and label 30 pages for each day of the month
  2. Each clear night, look at the Moon and draw its shape
  3. Note the date and time next to each drawing
  4. Try to identify which phase the Moon is in
  5. Mark when you see the New Moon (no Moon visible)
  6. Share your Moon journal with your class!
