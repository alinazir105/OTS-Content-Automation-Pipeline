🌱 5.1 — Living Things Change Their Environment
🌿

How do living things change their environment?

Living things make changes to their environments. Some changes are small — like a bird making a nest. Other changes can be large — like living things breaking down a fallen tree to make the ground better for new plants.

Competition! Plants and animals compete with one another to get food, water, sunlight, and shelter. If some cannot get what they need, they will die. Environments can change as living things compete to meet their needs.
🔄

A Bare Environment Grows!

Here is how a bare patch of land can change over time:

✅ Quick Check

If a plant's sunlight is blocked, it cannot make food through photosynthesis. It will grow weak and may die if the blockage lasts too long.

🦫 How Beavers Change Their Environment
🦫

Beavers: Nature's Engineers

Beavers make many changes to their environment. They use their strong teeth to cut down trees and plants. They build dams across streams, stopping water from flowing and creating pools or ponds. The beavers then build homes in the water.

Beavers use fallen trees, branches, and mud to build their dams and homes.

🦷 Adaptation: Beavers' strong, sharp teeth are the adaptation that helps them cut down trees!
⚖️

Good & Bad Effects of Beaver Dams

❌ Bad Effects

  • Dry land floods with water
  • Water may stop flowing to other places
  • Plants may die
  • Animals may have to move away

✅ Good Effects

  • Fallen trees make room for new plants
  • Wetlands may form
  • New living things are attracted
  • A running stream becomes a deep pool

🏭 How People Change Their Environment
🏙️

People & the Environment

People make more changes to the environment than any other living thing. Some changes can be good. Others can be harmful.

People make a lot of trash. Some of this trash gets into the environment. This is called pollution. It can harm the living things of the land, water, and air.

⚠️ Did You Know?
Most trash in the United States is buried in the ground (6/10), while 3/10 is used for other things, and only 1/10 is burned.
🗑️

What Is Pollution?

Pollution is when harmful things get into the air, water, or land. It can hurt living things in water, on land, and in the air.

♻️ The 3 Rs — Reduce, Reuse, Recycle

People can practise the "3 Rs" to cut back on trash and pollution. These acts will help us keep our environment healthy!

📉

Reduce

Use less of something. Turn off lights, use less plastic, buy only what you need.

🔄

Reuse

Use things more than once. Bring your own bag to the store instead of getting a new plastic bag.

♻️

Recycle

Make new things out of old things. Paper, glass, and plastic can be turned into new products!

✅ Ways People Can Help the Environment

  • Recycle paper, plastic, and glass instead of throwing them away
  • Plant trees to replace those that were cut down
  • Use cloth bags instead of plastic bags
  • Pick up litter in parks and near rivers
  • Save water and electricity
🌧️ 5.2 — How Weather & Natural Events Change Environments
🌧️

Too Much Rain

Too much rain can cause floods, damage homes, roads and crops, cause soil erosion, and lead to water pollution.

☀️

Not Enough Rain

Not enough rain can turn green plants brown and dry up water sources. Animals must move to find water.

⚡

Lightning & Fire

Lightning can start fires in dry grasslands or forests. Grasses may recover quickly, but trees may take many years to grow back.

🌋

Volcanic Eruptions

Volcanic eruptions can make sudden, long-lasting changes. But over time, plants start to grow again even on hardened lava!

🌍

Earthquakes

Earthquakes can reshape the land, block rivers, and destroy habitats. These changes can last for years.

🌀

Storms

Storms can knock down trees, flood areas, and uproot plants. Some living things recover quickly, others may not recover at all.

⏱️ How Long Do Changes Last?

Short-Term Changes

A rainstorm floods a field. When it stops, water drains and plants recover. Grassland grasses come back soon after a fire.

Long-Term Changes

Trees may take many years to grow back after a fire. A volcanic eruption can change land for decades.

Permanent Changes

People cutting down all trees in a forest to build roads. Pollution in rivers and lakes can be lasting and very hard to clean up.

🏡 How Changes Affect Plants & Animals
🏡

What is a Habitat?

A habitat is a living thing's home. Changes to a habitat can hurt the living things there.

  • Their water might dry up
  • Food might become hard to find
  • Their adaptations may not help them survive big changes
  • Some may need to find a new place to live
  • Those that cannot move or change might die
🏜️

🔗 How Living Things Depend on Each Other
🔗

All Living Things Are Linked

Living things may depend on each other for their needs. Many animals use plants for food, water, and shelter. Some animals eat other animals. They all share the same habitat.

If one living thing is harmed, that can lead to other changes in the habitat. All the living things in an environment are linked together.

🐾

The Prairie Example

Prairie dogs eat the grasses in their habitat. They dig burrows to stay safe. Other animals may use these burrows. Big animals eat the smaller animals.

If the small animals went away, the whole environment would change. The big animals that ate them would lose their food source too!

✅ Quick Check: No Rain for Many Months?

If no rain fell for many months in an animal's habitat: grasses would die → animals that eat grass would lose food → water sources would dry up → animals would have to move or could die → predators that ate those animals would also struggle to survive.

🦊 What Happens When New Living Things Move In?
🌍

Moving Into a New Environment

Living things sometimes move from one environment into another:

  • Animals might move to find new food and water
  • Seeds from plants might blow on the wind
  • Seeds might float down a river
  • People can bring new plants and animals into a new place
⚠️

Problems with New Arrivals

New plants and animals can cause changes in an environment:

Competition: New arrivals use food and water that other plants and animals need. This creates competition for resources.
Disease: New animals might bring sickness with them, spreading illness to animals that have no immunity.

Example: People raised foxes for their fur, then let them go. Now the foxes compete with other wild animals for the same food and water!

✅ How might a new illness spread?

When a new animal moves into an environment, it may carry diseases. If local animals have never encountered these diseases, they have no immunity. The illness can spread quickly through the local population, harming or killing many animals.

💀 5.3 — Living Things of the Past: Extinction
❄️

What Can Happen if the Environment Suddenly Changes?

Thousands of years ago, ice covered much of the United States. Then it grew warmer. The ice melted and some plants and animals could not survive in the warmer environment.

🔑 Key Word: Extinct
When a living thing is extinct, it means there are no more left alive anywhere on Earth. It is gone forever.

Causes of extinction:

  • Changes in weather patterns
  • Spreading sickness
  • Things people do to environments (deforestation, pollution)
🦣

How Animals Respond to Change

🦴 How Fossils Help Us Learn
🦕

What Are Fossils?

Fossils are the hardened remains of things that were once alive. They teach us about living things from long ago.

  • Shape & size fossils show us what a plant or animal looked like
  • Teeth fossils: Sharp teeth = ate meat; Flat teeth = ate plants
  • Bone fossils: Foot & leg bones show if it could run or climb; Wing bones mean it could fly
🐟 Fossils tell Earth's history too! Fish fossils found on land tell us that water covered that land long ago. Fossils found deep in the ground are remains of the oldest plants and animals.

🐘 How Living Things Today Compare to the Past

🦣 Woolly Mammoths vs Elephants

🦣

Woolly Mammoth (Extinct)

  • Very large body
  • Long, curved tusks
  • Thick hair for cold habitats
  • Flat teeth — ate plants
  • Lived in cold climates (ice age)
  • Lived in groups
🐘

Elephant (Alive Today)

  • Large body (smaller than mammoth)
  • Shorter tusks
  • Little hair — lives in warm habitats
  • Flat teeth — eats plants
  • Lives in warm climates (Africa, Asia)
  • Lives in groups
Shared Traits (Both): Big size, tusks, living in groups, flat teeth for eating plants.
Mammoth Only: Much bigger, much longer tusks, thick hair, cold habitat.

🦎 Some Animals Stayed Nearly the Same!

Some animals have adaptations that work so well over time that they can survive great changes in their environments. Their fossils look almost the same as the animals today!

🦐

Shrimp

Survived when salt levels changed in the oceans. Nearly the same for over 100 million years!

🪲

Cockroach

Has stayed about the same for millions of years. Its tough body helps it survive in many environments.

🐊

Crocodile

Fossils of crocodiles from long ago look almost identical to crocodiles alive today!

✅ Why Compare Fossils to Living Things?

We compare fossils to living things to understand how animals have changed (or stayed the same) over millions of years. It helps us learn about what they ate, how they moved, and what their environment was like.

🎨 Recycling Craft Project

Understand the importance of reduce, reuse, and recycle by creating new items from old materials!

📦 Materials Needed:

Old newspapers, plastic bottles, cardboard, cans, fabric scraps, glue, scissors, and markers.

1
Introduction: Begin with a brief discussion about how recycling helps reduce pollution and saves natural resources.
2
Activity: Divide into small groups and get recyclable materials. Create something useful or decorative — like a pencil holder from a can, a plant pot from a plastic bottle, or a newspaper collage!
3
Presentation: Each group presents their creation and explains how it helps the environment by reusing materials.
