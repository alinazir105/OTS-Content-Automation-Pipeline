🌿4.1 Introduction: Where Do Living Things Live?

Explore the amazing world of biomes and environments!

Living things live where they can meet their needs. A living thing's environment includes all the living and nonliving things in a place. A biome is a place that has certain kinds of living and nonliving things.

4
Major Land Biomes

🌍 Big Idea: Different biomes have different kinds of weather over time. Some biomes are cold and dry, some are warm and wet. The soil may be hard or soft, moist or dry!
🏜️ Desert Biome
  • Hot, dry climate
  • Less than 25 cm of rain per year
  • Sandy soil
  • Very hot days, cool nights
  • Pakistan: Thar, Cholistan, Kharan
🌾 Grassland Biome
  • Many grasses cover the land
  • Warm and moist conditions
  • Prairies (North America) or Savannas (Africa)
  • Rich or poor soil depending on type
  • Rainy and dry seasons
🌲 Forest Biome
  • Many trees
  • Pakistan: Himalayan and Mangrove forests
  • Provides fresh air, wood, animal homes
  • Tropical rainforests are hot and wet
  • Temperate forests have 4 seasons
🧊 Arctic Tundra
  • Cold biome above Arctic Circle
  • Long, dark, very cold winters
  • Few plants and animals
  • Short, beautiful summers
  • Ground stays frozen all year

💧4.1 Living Things and Their Needs

What do plants and animals need to survive?

🌱 What Do Plants Need?
💧
Water — absorbed through roots from soil
☀️
Sunlight — captured by leaves for energy
🌬️
Carbon Dioxide — from the air, used to make food
🌍
Nutrients — from the soil through roots
Plant Parts:
🌿 Roots — get water and food from ground
🌿 Stems — carry food and water through plant
🌿 Leaves — get sunlight and carbon dioxide
🌿 Flowers/Seeds — help make more plants
🦁 What Do Animals Need?
💧
Water — to stay alive (e.g., lion uses tongue)
🍖
Food — for energy (teeth or beaks help)
🌬️
Oxygen — lungs (land animals), gills (fish)
🏠
Shelter — safe place to live (birds build nests)
Animal Body Parts Help:
🦵 Legs/Wings — help them move
🦷 Teeth/Beaks — help them eat
🫁 Lungs — get oxygen from air
🐟 Gills — get oxygen from water

🔬What Are Adaptations?

Special features that help living things survive!

An adaptation is a body part or a way of acting that helps a living thing survive. Plants might have long roots to reach water deep in the ground. Animals might have a certain way of finding food to eat.

🏜️

Desert Adaptations

Thick stems, waxy skin, deep or shallow roots, thorns and spines to store water and stay safe.

🌾

Grassland Adaptations

Deep roots survive fire, flat teeth to eat grass, burrows to hide and stay cool, running fast to catch prey.

🌲

Forest Adaptations

Layered growth for light, mimicry to hide, bright colours as warning, thick coats for cold winters.

🧊

Arctic Adaptations

Thick fur, fat layers, small limbs to reduce heat loss, wide feet for snow, seasonal colour changes, migration.

🏜️4.2 Life in the Desert

Hot, dry, but full of amazing survivors!

A desert is a biome with warm, dry weather with very little rain. In Pakistan, the main deserts are the Thar Desert, the Cholistan Desert, and the Kharan Desert. These deserts are very hot during the day and can become cool at night.

🌵 🐪 🦎 🌵 ☀️ 🦂 🌵 🐍
Desert Facts:
  • Gets less than 25 cm of rain per year
  • Water quickly sinks into sandy soil — too deep for most roots
  • Very hot in the day, but quickly cools at night
  • Sandy soil makes it hard for many plants to grow

🌵 Desert Plant Adaptations

🌵 Saguaro Cactus

  • 🔪 Sharp spines keep animals away
  • 💧 Thick, waxy stem stores water
  • 🌱 Shallow roots trap rain water

🌳 Mesquite Tree

  • 🍃 Small leaves save water
  • 🌿 Long roots reach deep underground
  • 🔪 Sharp thorns keep animals away

🪴 Aloe Plant

  • ✨ Waxy skin keeps water from escaping
  • 💪 Thick leaves store lots of water
  • ☀️ Survives even when there is little rain

🐍 Desert Animal Adaptations

😴 Nocturnal Behaviour

Some desert animals sleep during the hot part of the day and come out at night when it is cool. Example: Rattlesnakes rest in the daytime.

👂 Big Ears for Cooling

A jackrabbit's big ears help it get rid of body heat. Blood vessels in the ears release heat. A thin body also helps it stay cool.

🎨 Camouflage (Blending In)

Some desert animals have bodies that blend in with their environment. They can hide easily from predators. This helps keep them safe.

🌾4.3–4.5 Life in the Grassland

Wide open spaces filled with grasses and amazing wildlife!

A grassland is a biome with many grasses. It helps keep the land warm and moist. It gives animals a safe place to live and food to eat. There are two kinds of grasslands.

🌻 Prairies (North America)

  • 🌡️ Mild weather with four seasons
  • 🌱 Rich soil that helps grasses grow
  • 🐃 Buffalo and bison roam these lands

🦁 Savannas (Africa)

  • ☀️ Warm all year long
  • 🌧️ Rainy season and dry season
  • 🌳 Have trees and poor soil
  • 🔥 Can dry out and catch fire in dry season

🌿 Grassland Plant Adaptations

🌱 Deep-Rooted Grasses

Most grasses have deep roots to get water from deep in the ground. If fire burns the part above ground, the roots can grow new parts!

🌳 Baobab Tree

The baobab tree only grows leaves in the wet season. It has a thick trunk to store water in the dry season. Its bark protects it against fire.

🦓 Grassland Animal Adaptations

🦷 Flat Teeth (Zebra)

Some animals like zebras have flat teeth to chew and grind the tough grasses in the grassland.

🐾 Burrows (Prairie Dog)

Some animals dig burrows (holes) in the ground to hide from predators and stay cool in the heat.

🏃 Running Fast (Cheetah)

Some grassland animals can run very fast to catch other animals for food or to escape from predators.

🌲4.6 Life in the Forest

Explore tropical rainforests and temperate forests!

A forest is a biome that has many trees. In Pakistan, we have forests like the Himalayan forests in the north and mangrove forests near the coast. Forests give us fresh air, wood, and homes for animals.

🌲 🦜 🐒 🦋 🌲 🦌 🍄 🌲

🌴 Tropical Rainforest

Always hot and wet. Dense and green. Has more kinds of animals than anywhere else on Earth. Plants grow in layers.

Plant Layers:
☀️ Tallest trees get the most light
🌿 Next layer: branches and leaves
🌱 Middle layer: less light
🌑 Forest floor: dark and damp

🍂 Temperate Forest

Has four seasons: spring, summer, autumn, and winter. Changes from season to season. Some trees lose leaves in winter.

4 Seasons:
🌸 Spring — new growth begins
☀️ Summer — full leaves, warm
🍂 Autumn — leaves change colour
❄️ Winter — some trees bare

🌿 Forest Plant Adaptations

🌳 Tall Trees (Special Roots)

Tall rainforest trees have special roots that help keep them standing tall and stable.

🍃 Pointy Leaves

Some shorter plants have leaves with pointed tips to drain away extra water quickly.

🌿 Large Ground Leaves

Plants on the dark forest floor have large leaves to capture as much sunlight as possible.

🐸 Forest Animal Adaptations

🎨 Bright Warning Colours

Some animals have bright, colourful bodies to warn predators. For example, poison dart frogs warn others they may be poisonous!

🌸 Mimicry

Mimicry is when one thing pretends to be another thing. A mantis looks like a flower using colour and body shape to hide!

❄️ Winter Survival

Temperate forest animals grow heavy coats in winter, eat more food, store food, or sleep through the coldest months (like dormouse).

🦔 Defence Mechanisms

A skunk sprays a smelly liquid. A porcupine has sharp quills that hurt any attacker. Most animals leave them alone!

🧊4.7 Life in the Arctic Tundra

The coldest biome on Earth — and how life survives there!

The arctic tundra is a cold biome above the Arctic Circle. Winters are long, dark, and very cold. Few plants and animals can be found. In the middle of winter, the Sun never rises. In the middle of summer, the Sun never sets.

🐻‍❄️ ❄️ 🦊 🦌 ❄️ 🐺 ❄️ 🦢
Arctic Tundra Facts:
  • Located above the Arctic Circle (far north)
  • Part of the ground stays frozen all year
  • Snow melts in summer — water forms puddles on top
  • Small plants grow in summer when animals return

🌼 Arctic Plant Adaptations

🌱 Shallow/No Roots

Arctic plants keep roots close to the top of ground to get water. Some plants like lichen have no roots at all and cling to rocks.

🌿 Short and Small

Arctic plants stay short and small to protect from cold winds. Many grow in clumps to stay warm together.

🌸 Dark Coloured Flowers

The dark colours of arctic flowers attract animals that can help spread seeds, letting new plants grow. Example: dwarf fireweed.

🐻‍❄️ Arctic Animal Adaptations

🧥 Thick Fur & Fat

Heavy fur protects from cold. A thick layer of fat keeps bodies warm. Polar bears have large bodies to keep heat.

🦵 Small Limbs

Some animals have small legs and arms to reduce heat loss. Less surface area means less heat escapes from the body.

🦶 Wide Feet

Animals like lynxes have wide feet to stay on top of snow. Sharp claws keep them from slipping on ice.

✈️ Migration

Migration means moving from one place to another. Caribou, tundra swans, and Canadian geese migrate south in winter to find warmth and food.

🎨 Seasonal Colour Change

The arctic fox changes colour to white in winter to blend in with the snow — perfect camouflage! In summer it is brown/grey.

📖Key Vocabulary

Important words to know in this unit

🌍 Environment
All the living and nonliving things in a place where a living thing lives.
🗺️ Biome
A large area with certain kinds of living and nonliving things that share the same climate.
🔬 Adaptation
A body part or way of acting that helps a living thing survive in its environment.
🏜️ Desert
A biome with hot, dry weather and very little rain (less than 25 cm per year).
🌾 Grassland
A biome with many grasses that keeps the land warm and moist. Includes prairies and savannas.
🌲 Forest
A biome that has many trees, providing homes for animals and clean air for all.
🧊 Tundra
A cold biome in the far north above the Arctic Circle where the ground is often frozen.
🎭 Mimicry
When one living thing looks like or pretends to be another thing to survive (e.g., mantis looks like a flower).
✈️ Migration
When animals move from one place to another (usually south in winter) to find food and warmth.

📅 Arctic Tundra: Seasonal Timeline

❄️ Winter

Long, dark, very cold. Sun never rises in the middle of winter. Animals have migrated south. Very few living things active.

🌸 Spring

Tundra begins to warm. Snow melts on top of frozen ground. Water forms puddles. Plants begin to grow.

☀️ Summer

Short but beautiful. Sun never sets in middle of summer. Small plants grow. Animals return from migration to eat and nest.

🍂 Autumn

Temperatures drop again. Animals like caribou, tundra swans, and geese begin their migration south before winter returns.

🎨Activity: Biome in a Box

Create your own biome diorama!

🎁 Create a Biome Diorama

Create a small diorama representing one of the biomes (tundra, desert, forest, or grassland). Use materials you have at home to bring your biome to life!

1
Choose Your Biome — Pick: Desert 🏜️, Grassland 🌾, Forest 🌲, or Tundra 🧊
2
Gather Materials — Cotton for snow (tundra), sand for desert, green tissue paper for forest leaves, dried grass for grassland
3
Build Your Scene — Use a shoebox or cardboard box. Create the landscape and background of your chosen biome.
4
Add Plants & Animals — Draw, cut out, or use small toy figures of animals and plants that live in your biome.
5
Label the Adaptations — Add small labels or flags pointing to each animal/plant. Write how the adaptation helps them survive!
6
Present Your Biome — Share your diorama with your class or family. Explain how each living thing survives in that environment!
