Introduction

What makes your body strong and able to move?

🦴

Bones Give Structure

Bones provide shape and support to your body. Without bones, our body would be like jelly! They also protect important organs inside.

💪

Muscles Move You

Muscles are soft, stretchy tissues attached to bones. When your brain sends a signal, muscles pull on bones to create movement.

🤝

Together They Work

Bones and muscles form a system that helps us stand, move, run, jump, and do many activities every single day!

💡

Fun Fact!

A baby is born with around 270–300 bones, but as we grow, some bones fuse together. By the time we are adults, we have exactly 206 bones!

3.1 The Skeleton

A framework of 206 bones that gives us shape, support, and protection

What is a Skeleton?

The skeleton is a framework of 206 bones in our body. It gives shape, support, and protection to our organs. Without bones, our body would be like jelly! The skeleton also works with muscles to help us move in many ways.

Main Parts of the Skeleton

💀

Skull

Protects our brain — the most important organ in our body. It acts like a hard helmet for your brain.

🔗

Backbone (Spine)

Supports our back and helps us stand upright. The spine is made of many small bones called vertebrae stacked on top of each other.

🫁

Ribcage

Protects our heart and lungs. The ribs form a cage-like structure around our chest to keep these vital organs safe.

🦾

Limbs (Arms & Legs)

Help us to lift, walk, run, swim, and do many other activities. Arms and legs have many bones working together.

3.2 Joints & Movement

Where bones meet and movement happens

What are Joints?

Joints are the places where two or more bones meet. They allow our body to bend and move. For example, our elbows and knees are joints that help our arms and legs bend. Without joints, we would not be able to move at all!

🚪

Hinge Joints

Allow back-and-forth movement like a door hinge. They can only move in one direction — opening and closing, just like a door hinge swings back and forth.

🦵 Knee 💪 Elbow
⚽

Ball-and-Socket Joints

Allow movement in many directions. A round ball fits into a cup-shaped socket, allowing rotation and movement in all directions — up, down, forward, backward, and around.

🙋 Shoulder 🦯 Hip
🤔

Think About It!

Try to move your knee sideways — it won't go far! Now try moving your shoulder in circles. That's the difference between a hinge joint and a ball-and-socket joint!

3.3 Muscles

The engine of your body!

What are Muscles?

Muscles are soft, stretchy tissues that are attached to bones. They help us move by pulling on the bones. When you want to move your arm, your brain sends a signal to the muscles in your arm, and they pull the bones to make the arm move.

How Muscles Work — Step by Step

1

Your brain decides you want to move

2

Brain sends a signal through nerves

3

The muscle contracts (gets shorter)

4

The muscle pulls the bone to create movement

Types of Muscles

💪 Voluntary Muscles

Muscles you can control, like in your arms and legs. You use them when you want to pick up a toy or kick a ball. You decide when they move!

Example: Skeletal muscles

🫀 Involuntary Muscles

Muscles that work without you thinking, like those in your stomach and heart. They help in digesting food and making your heart beat automatically.

Example: Smooth muscles

❤️ Cardiac Muscles

They are special muscles found only in the heart. They keep pumping blood throughout our body all the time — day and night, without rest!

Example: Heart muscle

3.4 Tendons & Ligaments

The connectors that hold everything together

🔴

Tendons

Strong, flexible cords that attach muscles to bones. When a muscle contracts, the tendon transmits the pulling force to the bone, causing movement. Without tendons, muscles could not move bones!

🔵

Ligaments

Tough tissues that connect bones to each other, holding joints together. Ligaments are found at joints and prevent bones from moving too far out of place, protecting us from injury.

Easy Way to Remember!

  • Tendons: Muscle → Bone (T for Tight cord)
  • Ligaments: Bone → Bone (L for Locking joints)

🔬 Activity: Create a Joint Model

Hands-on learning — build and demonstrate!

🛠️ Materials You Need

🎨 Modeling clay or playdough 🥤 Two plastic straws 🤸 Rubber bands (for ligaments) ⚽ Small plastic ball

📋 Step-by-Step Instructions

Hinge Joint Model: Use two plastic straws to represent bones. Attach them with a rubber band at one end so they can only move back and forth, simulating a hinge joint (like the elbow or knee).

Ball-and-Socket Joint Model: Attach a plastic ball to the end of one straw using modeling clay or playdough. Insert this ball into a "socket" of clay on another straw to simulate movement in many directions (like the shoulder or hip).

Add Ligaments: Wrap rubber bands around the joint connection to represent ligaments holding the joint together.

Explain: Show your classmates or family members how each joint allows for different types of movement. Observe how the structure of each joint limits or enables certain movements.

🚪 Hinge Joint Result

Your model should only open and close in one direction — just like your real elbow and knee joints!

⚽ Ball-and-Socket Result

Your model should rotate in many directions — just like your shoulder can swing in circles!

🧠 Critical Thinking

Use what you've learned to think deeper!

Imagine you are holding a heavy bag!

What muscles are working, and what bones are supporting you? Think about which joints are involved, what type of muscles are being used, and how tendons and ligaments are helping.


🌟

Sample Answer Guide

When holding a heavy bag: Bicep muscles (voluntary/skeletal) contract. The arm bones (humerus, radius, ulna) support the weight. The elbow (hinge joint) bends. Tendons connect muscle to bone, and ligaments keep the elbow joint stable!
