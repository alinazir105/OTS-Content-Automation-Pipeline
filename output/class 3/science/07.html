🌍 The Big Idea

How can we describe rocks and soil?

Rocks and soil are all around us! From the ground we walk on to the mountains we see, Earth is made up of amazing materials called rocks and minerals.

💡

Scientists called geologists study rocks and help us understand how Earth was formed and how it changes over time!

✏️

The lead in your pencil is made from a mineral called graphite! You use minerals every day without knowing it!

🪨 What Are Rocks?

Learn how scientists describe and study rocks

👨‍🔬 Who Studies Rocks?

A geologist is a scientist who studies rocks. Geologists look at many things about rocks to understand them better.

🎨 Rock Colours

Many rocks are one colour. Others are more than one colour. Most rocks are grey, but some are black, brown, red, white, or pink!

⚖️ Size & Weight

Geologists also look at the size of rocks. But here's something surprising — rocks that are the same size might NOT weigh the same!

⚖️

Two rocks can be the same size but have different weights because they are made of different minerals!

💎 What Are Rocks Made Of?

All rocks are made of minerals. Some rocks are made of one mineral. Others are made of many minerals.

⬜

Quartz

The grey parts of granite are the mineral quartz. It shines like glass!

🤍

Feldspar

The white parts of granite are feldspar. It is one of the most common minerals on Earth.

🖤

Mica

The black parts of granite are mica. It has thin, sparkly layers!

🌟 Granite = 3 Minerals!

Granite is made of THREE minerals: Quartz (grey) + Feldspar (white) + Mica (black)

🏠 Minerals in Everyday Life

✏️

Pencil Lead

Made from the mineral graphite

🪥

Toothpaste

Contains fluoride from the mineral fluorite

🥦

Food

We get minerals our body needs from the foods we eat!

💎 Properties of Minerals

How do we describe and identify minerals?

🔍 What Is a Property?

A property tells you something about an object. Minerals have several important properties that help us identify them.

🎨

Colour

The colour of a mineral is the first thing you notice. Some minerals have a unique colour that helps identify them.

grey gold white
✨

Lustre

Lustre tells how a mineral looks when light shines on it. Some shine like glass, others look dull.

🔮 Quartz → glassy shine
🪙 Pyrite → metallic (like gold)
⬜ Halloysite → dull lustre
💪

Hardness

Hardness is how tough a mineral is. Soft minerals can be scratched easily. Hard ones cannot.

😊 Talc → softest mineral (fingernail can scratch it)
💎 Diamond → hardest mineral (only cut by diamond)

⚡ How Do Rocks Change?

Weathering, erosion, and other forces that change rocks

🌊 What Is Weathering?

Weathering is the way water and wind change rocks. Most rocks are very hard, but they can change their size and shape over time.

💧 Water Weathering

When water gets into the cracks of rocks, it can freeze and push against the rocks. Then the cracks get bigger and the rocks break apart into smaller pieces.

💨 Wind Weathering

Strong winds can blow sand against rocks. The sand acts like sandpaper and wears rocks down. Wind and sand can even make holes in rocks over many years!

🏔️ Rocks Sliding

When rocks slide down a hill, they may break apart and become smaller. As they bump into other rocks, they get smoother and smaller.

🌱 Plant Roots

Plants can grow in soil inside the cracks of rocks. Sometimes a plant's roots are so strong they cause the rocks to break from the inside!

🌍 Earthquakes

During an earthquake, rocks rub against each other and break into smaller pieces. Earthquakes are a powerful force that changes rock formations.

💦 Water & Minerals

Water can cause minerals inside rocks to change colour. It causes copper to turn green and iron to rust and turn red and brown!

🌱 Soil

What is soil made of and what types are there?

🔬 What Is Soil Made Of?

Soil is made up of tiny rocks and bits of plants and animals. It forms when:

🪨

Weathering makes large rocks break down into smaller rocks, which become part of the soil

🍂

When plants and animals die, they break down and become part of the soil

Plants grow in soil by taking in minerals from the soil. People need minerals too — we get them by eating plants!

🌿→👨

Soil → Plant → People (Minerals travel up the food chain!)

🌍 Types of Soil

🔴

Red Clay Soil

Gets its red colour from tiny pieces of iron. It does not hold much water and can be sticky when wet.

❌ Poor water retention
🟡

Sandy Soil

Light brown in colour. Does not hold much water. Plants that don't need much water grow best here.

💦 Low water retention
⚫

Topsoil

Dark brown or black. Can hold lots of water. Has bits of dead animals and plants. Best for most plants!

✅ Best for plants

🐛 Animals & Soil

How do animals help make soil better?

🕳️ Digging Tunnels

Animals like ants, worms, rabbits, and gophers live underground. They dig tunnels in the soil.

🐜

Ants dig tiny tunnels that aerate the soil

🪱

Worms mix the soil and add nutrients as they move

🐇

Rabbits dig burrows that allow water deeper into soil

🐿️

Gophers dig and mix soil, helping air reach plant roots

🌱 Why Tunnels Help

The digging mixes the soil. This helps air and water get into the soil. The air and water then help plants grow stronger!

💀

When animals die, their bodies break down and become part of the soil. This makes the soil healthy and rich, helping new plants to grow!

🔬 Hands-On Activities

Learn by doing!

🪨 Rock Observation Experiment

Collect and study different rocks!

1
Collect three different rocks (smooth, rough, and shiny)
2
Observe their colours, texture, and weight
3
Use a nail to gently scratch each rock and record which one is hardest
4
Compare results and discuss why some rocks are harder than others

📝 Record Your Observations

Which rock was hardest? Which had the most interesting colour? Write down what you see!

🌱 Soil Absorption Test

Test which soil holds the most water!

1
Gather three soil samples: sandy, clay, and topsoil
2
Pour equal amounts of water on each soil type
3
Observe how much water each type absorbs
4
Discuss which soil retains the most water and why that matters for plants
