Key Vocabulary

Important words for this unit

⚙️ Simple Machine
A tool that makes the force of your push or pull stronger, making moving objects easier.
🔧 Lever
A simple machine that lets you use less force to lift something. Examples: forks, scissors, see-saws.
📐 Ramp
A simple machine that helps you move things to a higher place. Pushing up a ramp is easier than lifting.
🌍 Gravity
A force that pulls things toward each other. The larger the object, the stronger its gravity.
⚖️ Weight
The amount of force gravity pulls an object toward Earth's center. Measured in grams, kilograms, or tons.
🧲 Attract
To pull objects. A magnet attracts (pulls) objects made of iron.
🔵🔵 Poles
The two parts of a magnet (north and south) where its pull is the strongest.
↔️ Repel
When two poles of the same kind push away from each other (north-north or south-south).

8.1 Tools & Simple Machines

How do machines make our work easier?

What is a Simple Machine?

A simple machine makes moving an object easier. It makes the force of your push or pull stronger. Simple machines help us do the same amount of work with less effort!

💡 Key Idea A tool can be one simple machine (like a hammer) or many simple machines working together (like a car engine).
🔧

Lever

A lever lets you use less force to lift something. The longer the lever, the easier it is to lift! Examples: forks, scissors, see-saws, and using a metal stick to open a paint can.

📐

Ramp (Inclined Plane)

A ramp helps you move things to a higher place. Pushing a box up a ramp is much easier than lifting it straight up. Used in loading docks, wheelchair ramps, and more!

⭕

Wheel

Wheels let you roll objects instead of lifting them. This reduces friction and makes moving heavy things easy. Examples: strollers, carts, cars, and wheelbarrows.

🔨

Hammer

A hammer is a tool that is one simple machine. It transfers force from your arm to the nail, making it easy to drive nails into wood.

🥄

Spatula

A spatula can push or pull food. It acts as a lever, giving you control to flip, mix, and move food around while cooking.

🔩

Nutcracker

A nutcracker creates a force strong enough to break hard shells. It works as a lever — squeezing the handles gives you more force than your hand alone could apply.

🌟 Cool Fact! A can opener uses a sharp wheel that spins to cut through a metal lid. Turning the handle makes it easy to open cans without much effort!

8.2 Gravity

The invisible force that keeps us on the ground

What is Gravity?

Gravity is a force that pulls things toward each other. The larger an object is, the stronger the force of gravity it has.

You cannot see gravity, but you can feel it. Gravity is what keeps you on the ground. When you throw a ball in the air, gravity pulls it back down to Earth.

The Earth has a very strong force of gravity because of its enormous size. That's why everything falls toward the ground!

🪂 Example When a parachutist jumps from a plane, gravity pulls them toward Earth. The parachute slows the fall, but gravity is always pulling!

What is Weight?

Gravity pulls things toward the center of the Earth with a certain amount of force. This amount of force is called weight.

You can find the weight of an object by putting it on a scale. A scale measures weight in grams, kilograms, or even tons.

Different planets and moons have more or less gravity than Earth. The Moon is much smaller than Earth, so it has less gravity!

🚀 Astronaut Fact! An astronaut can jump much higher on the Moon because there is less gravity to pull them down. If you weighed 60 pounds on Earth, you would weigh only about 10 pounds on the Moon!

8.3 Magnets Push, Magnets Pull

Discover the invisible forces of magnetism

What Does a Magnet Pull?

A magnet can attract (pull) objects made of iron. Magnets will NOT attract materials that don't contain iron.

  • ✅ Iron nails — attracted!
  • ✅ Steel paperclips — attracted!
  • ❌ Pennies (copper/zinc) — NOT attracted
  • ❌ Plastic spoon — NOT attracted
  • ❌ Aluminum foil — NOT attracted
  • ❌ Wood — NOT attracted

Magnets Work Through Materials!

Magnets can move things without even touching them. They can pull iron through:

📄 Paper 🧴 Plastic 🪟 Glass 💧 Liquids 💨 Gases
💡 Amazing! Magnets can pull iron nails through a glass of water or through a stack of paper — no touching required!

🔴🔵 What Are Poles?

Every magnet has two poles — a North pole (N) and a South pole (S). The poles are where the pull of the magnet is the strongest.

🟢 ATTRACT (Pull Together)

🔴N→←S🔵

When opposite poles face each other (North + South), the magnets pull toward each other. Opposites attract!

🔴 REPEL (Push Apart)

🔵S←→N🔴

When same poles face each other (N+N or S+S), the magnets push away from each other. Like poles repel!

🌟 Remember: Opposites Attract, Likes Repel! North pole + South pole = ATTRACT. North pole + North pole = REPEL. South pole + South pole = REPEL.

Hands-On Activity

Magnet Power! — Discover how magnets attract and repel

🧲 Magnet Power Experiment

Follow the steps below and record your observations!

📦 Materials Needed
🧲 Two Bar Magnets 🔩 Small Iron Nails 🥄 Plastic Spoon 🌟 Aluminum Foil
📋 Procedure
1
Place the two magnets close to each other with opposite poles facing (N facing S). Observe what happens — do they pull together or push apart?
2
Flip one magnet so the same poles are facing (N facing N). Observe what happens now — do they still attract?
3
Try picking up different materials with the magnet: iron nails, plastic spoon, and aluminum foil. Record which items the magnet attracts.
🔍 Observation Questions

Question 1

What happens when opposite poles are facing?

Question 2

What happens when similar poles face each other?

Question 3

Which objects did the magnet attract? Why?

🌟 Expected Results Opposite poles should attract (pull together). Same poles should repel (push apart). The magnet should attract the iron nails but NOT the plastic spoon or aluminum foil, because magnets only attract iron-based objects!
