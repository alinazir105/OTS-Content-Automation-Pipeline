🔊 What is Sound?

Sound is a kind of energy you hear. Sound is made when something vibrates, or moves back and forth.

💡 When a guitar string is plucked, it vibrates. The vibrating air travels to your ear. When your eardrum vibrates, you hear the sound!

🗣️ When you speak, air moves from your lungs to your throat. The air moves over your vocal cords and causes them to vibrate. Try touching your throat as you speak — you will feel the vibrations!
🎶 When you blow air through a flute, it vibrates and makes a sound!
📣 Loud and Soft Sounds

When a sound is loud, the vibrations are big. When a sound is soft, the vibrations are small.

🦁
Loud Sound
Big vibrations
A lion's roar is so loud you can hear it up to 6 miles away!
🐱
Soft Sound
Small vibrations
A cat's meow is much softer than a lion's roar.
📢 When you yell → big vibrations → loud sound!
When you whisper → small vibrations → soft sound!

🎵 High and Low Sounds

Sounds can be high, low, or in between. The speed of vibrations decides how high or low a sound is!

🐱
High Sound
Fast vibrations
Examples: Cat's meow, child's voice, bird chirp, whistle
🐄
Low Sound
Slow vibrations
Examples: Cow's moo, man's voice, thunder, big drum
🎹 Play the Xylophone!

Click the bars to hear high and low sounds!

Short bars = High sound  |  Long bars = Low sound

❓ Which dog's bark is a higher sound — a small dog or a big dog? The small dog makes a higher sound because it creates faster vibrations!
👂 How Do We Hear?

Sound vibrations travel through the air and reach your ear. Inside your ear is a special part called the eardrum. When the vibrations hit the eardrum, it vibrates too — and that is how you hear!

👂
Outer Ear
Collects sound waves from the air
🥁
Eardrum
Vibrates when sound hits it — this is how you hear!
🦴
Tiny Bones
Carry vibrations deeper into the ear
🧠
Brain
Receives the signal and lets you understand the sound
🎯 The part of your ear that vibrates to help you hear is called the EARDRUM!
📏 Distance and Loudness

Think about a lion's roar. When you are near a lion, its roar sounds loud. When you are far away, its roar sounds soft.

The farther away you are from a sound, the softer it sounds.

🔬 Fun Activities to Try!
🎸

1. Vibration Experiment

Stretch a rubber band over a box and pluck it. Observe how it vibrates and produces sound. Try plucking harder and softer to see the difference in loudness!

🚶

2. Sound Walk

Take a walk around your school or home and list 5 loud sounds and 5 soft sounds you hear. Discuss why some sounds are louder than others.

📞

3. DIY Telephone

Use two paper cups and a string to create a simple telephone. Speak into one cup and listen from the other to understand how sound travels through solids!
