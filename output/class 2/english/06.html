Present Tense of "Be" — Is, Am, Are

Learn when to use is, am, and are to describe states and conditions!

🌟 📝 🎯
📖
What is the Verb "Be"?

The verb "be" is a stative verb. It shows a state or condition rather than an action.

It tells us what someone or something is like right now.

The form is: Subject + is / am / are + extra words

✅
Affirmative Sentences (Positive)

Use these forms to say something is true:

SubjectVerbExample
IamI am tall.
We / You / TheyareWe are good friends.
He / She / It / ZainisHe is happy.
❌
Negative Sentences

Add "not" after the verb to make a sentence negative:

SubjectVerb + notExample
Iam notI am not tall.
We / You / Theyare notWe are not good friends.
He / She / It / Zainis notHe is not happy.
❓
Asking Questions with "Be"

To ask a question, put the verb before the subject:

VerbSubjectExtra words
AmItall?
AreWe / You / Theygood friends?
IsHe / She / It / Zainhappy?

⚠️ In positive short answers, use the full form:

✔️ Yes, I am. (NOT "Yes, I'm")

✔️ Yes, you are. (NOT "Yes, you're")

✂️
Short Forms (Contractions)

We often use short forms in speaking and informal writing:

Affirmative Contractions

I am→I'm
You are→You're
She is→She's
He is→He's
It is→It's
We are→We're
They are→They're

Negative Contractions

I am not→I'm not
You are not→You aren't
She is not→She isn't
He is not→He isn't
It is not→It isn't
We are not→We aren't
They are not→They aren't
