Countable & Uncountable Nouns

Learn which nouns we can count and which ones we can't!

🍎

Countable Nouns

Countable nouns are the names of things that we can count. They have a singular and a plural form.

🍎
apple → apples
🌳
tree → trees
👧
child → children
🍐
pear → pears
🍌
banana → bananas
🥚
egg → eggs
💡
We can say: one apple, two apples, three trees. We can count them!
🍵

Uncountable Nouns

Uncountable nouns are the names of things we cannot count. They only have a singular form.

🍵
tea
🥛
milk
🍬
sugar
🫒
oil
💰
money
🍝
spaghetti
💡
We cannot say: one milk or two sugars. We can only say some milk or some sugar.
📝

A, An and Some

In affirmative sentences, we use a or an before singular countable nouns, and some before plural countable nouns and uncountable nouns.

Singular Countable
a / an
There's a child.
There's an apple.
Plural Countable
some
There are some children.
There are some apples.
Uncountable Noun
some
There's some tea.
There's some milk.
Remember
a / an ?
Use an before a vowel sound
Use a before a consonant sound
💡
There's = There is  |  There're = There are
