Introduction

Introduction to MS Word

MS Word is used to write and format text in documents. Besides basic formatting like bold and italics, MS Word allows you to use superscript and subscript to format text for mathematical equations or chemical formulas. You can also change the line spacing and indentation to make the text easier to read.

Feature 1

Superscript

Superscript makes text smaller and raises it above the normal line of text. It is most commonly used in mathematical equations to show exponents or powers.

Example: Area of a Square
A = S2
Where S² represents the square of the side length

📋 What is Superscript?

Superscript is a formatting feature that makes selected text appear smaller and positioned above the normal line. It is represented by the button X² in the Font group on the Home tab.

Steps to Apply Superscript

🎯 Follow these steps in MS Word:

1
Highlight the text or number you want to format as superscript.
2
Go to the Home tab in the toolbar at the top.
3
In the Font group, locate the Superscript (X²) button.
4
Click the Superscript button — selected text will be raised above the line.
5
Alternatively, press Ctrl + Shift + = on your keyboard as a shortcut!
6
To remove superscript, click the button again or use the same keyboard shortcut.

✍️ Example Sentence

The formula for calculating the area of a square is A = S2, where S2 represents the square of the side length.

Feature 2

Subscript

Subscript makes text smaller and lowers it below the normal line of text. It is most commonly used in chemical formulas or footnotes.

Example: Chemical Formula for Water
H2O
The subscript '2' shows there are 2 hydrogen atoms

📋 What is Subscript?

Subscript is a formatting feature that makes selected text appear smaller and positioned below the normal line. It is represented by the button X₂ in the Font group on the Home tab.

Steps to Apply Subscript

🎯 Follow these steps in MS Word:

1
Highlight the text or number you want to format as subscript.
2
Go to the Home tab in the toolbar.
3
In the Font group, locate the Subscript (X₂) button.
4
Click the Subscript button — selected text will appear below the line.
5
Alternatively, press Ctrl + = on your keyboard as a shortcut!
6
To remove subscript, click the button again or use the same keyboard shortcut.

✍️ Example Sentence

Water is made up of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom, written as H2O, where the subscript '2' represents the number of hydrogen atoms in the molecule.

🧪 Subscript Uses

ChemistryH₂O, CO₂
Matha₁, b₂ sequences
FootnotesText below
PhysicsF₁, m₂ labels

Feature 3

Line Spacing

Line spacing refers to the space between lines in a paragraph. You can increase or decrease this space to make your text more readable. More space makes reading easier; less space fits more text on a page.

📋 What is Line Spacing?

Line spacing controls the vertical distance between each line of text. The default in most MS Word documents is 1.15, but you can change it to 1.0 (single), 1.5, or 2.0 (double) for different effects.

Visual Comparison

1.0 — Single Spacing

This is an example of single line spacing. The text appears more compact and fits more content on the page. Good for formal reports.

1.5 — One-and-a-half Spacing

This is an example of 1.5 line spacing. The text has more breathing room and is easier to read, especially for longer documents.

2.0 — Double Spacing

This is an example of double line spacing. There is a lot of space between lines, making it great for proofreading and marking.

Steps to Adjust Line Spacing

1.Select the paragraph(s) where you want to adjust spacing.
2.Go to the Home tab in the toolbar.
3.In the Paragraph group, click the Line and Paragraph Spacing icon.
4.A drop-down will show: 1.0, 1.15, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0
5.Select the desired spacing option from the menu.
★For custom spacing: select "Line Spacing Options..." and set specific value
6.Click OK to apply the changes.

Feature 4

Indentation

Indentation refers to the space added at the beginning of a paragraph. This makes the document look neat, organized, and helps to clearly distinguish between different sections of text.

📋 What is Indentation?

Indentation is the distance between the text and the margin of the page. You can control how far in from the left or right margin the paragraph text starts, or just indent the very first line.

Types of Indentation

⬅️ Left Indent

Moves the entire paragraph away from the left margin. Example: Paragraph shifted 0.5 inches from the left edge.

➡️ Right Indent

Moves the paragraph away from the right margin. Example: Paragraph shifted 0.5 inches from the right edge.

📄 First Line Indent

Indents only the first line of the paragraph. Example: First line moved 0.5 inches inward while rest stays normal.

🔽 Hanging Indent

The first line stays at normal position, but all other lines are moved to the right. Often used in bibliographies.

Steps to Apply Indentation

1.Place the cursor at the beginning of the paragraph.
2.Go to the Home tab in the toolbar.
3.In Paragraph group, click Increase Indent (arrow pointing right).
★Keyboard shortcut: press Tab to indent first line
4.To decrease, click Decrease Indent (arrow pointing left).
5.For precise control: Right-click → Paragraph → Indentation settings.

📐 Using the Ruler

The ruler at the top of MS Word lets you drag indent markers:

  • Enable ruler: View tab → Ruler
  • Drag the left rectangle to adjust left indent
  • Drag the small triangle for first line indent
  • Drag the right rectangle to adjust right indent
