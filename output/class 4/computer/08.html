Definition

What is Robotics?

Definition

Robotics is one of the most exciting emerging technologies. Robots are machines designed to perform tasks automatically or with some level of human guidance. They are capable of doing tasks that are repetitive, dangerous, or require a high level of precision.

Robotics has been around for many years, primarily used in the manufacturing industry. Today, it extends far beyond factories into medicine, space exploration, and disaster response.

How Robots Are Controlled

💻

Pre-programmed Instructions

The robot follows a sequence of commands to complete tasks, such as painting a car. These instructions are coded in advance.

🖐️

Manual Guidance

A human operator either guides the robot arm directly or uses sensors. The robot then saves these actions and repeats them automatically.

📌 Smart Sensor Example

Robots equipped with sensors can ensure they don't spray paint when no car is present, or stop working if the paint runs out. This makes them efficient and reliable in repetitive tasks.

📖 Key Terms

Robotics The science and technology of designing and using robots to perform tasks automatically.
Robot A machine that can perform tasks automatically or with human guidance.
Drone A robotic flying device that operates without a pilot on board.
Sensor A device that detects changes in the environment (e.g., detecting a car before spraying paint).
Precision Performing a task with very high accuracy and exactness.
AI Artificial Intelligence — machines that can learn and solve problems like humans.

Applications

Applications of Robotics

Robots are used in many different fields. Here are the three main application areas covered in this unit:

🏭

1. Manufacturing

Robots are widely used in manufacturing to improve efficiency and consistency. They perform tasks such as:

  • Painting car bodies
  • Welding parts of cars
  • Manufacturing microchips and electrical goods
  • Storing and retrieving items in automatic warehouses
🚁

2. Use of Drones

Drones are robotic devices that can fly without a pilot. They are used in:

  • Military: reconnaissance and surveillance
  • Mapping landscapes in 3-D
  • Monitoring weather conditions
  • Search and rescue missions during natural disasters
  • Tasks too dangerous or difficult for humans
🏥

3. In Surgery

Robots are used in the field of medicine, especially in surgeries. Robotic surgery enables:

  • Precise and complex procedures
  • Flexibility and control beyond standard techniques
  • Surgeons use camera arms and mechanical joints
  • Joints that mimic human movement
  • Surgeries become safer and more effective

Benefits & Challenges

Advantages & Disadvantages of Robotics

✅ Advantages

Safety: Can work in dangerous environments harmful to humans (e.g., toxic chemicals, extreme heat).
Efficiency: Robots can work non-stop (24/7), unlike humans who need rest.
Cost-Effectiveness: Though expensive initially, cheaper long-term — no wages or holidays needed.
Consistency: Robots produce uniform results, e.g., all cars on an assembly line are identical.
Time-Saving: They handle repetitive tasks, freeing humans for creative and skilled work like quality control or designing.

⚠️ Disadvantages

Limited Flexibility: Robots struggle with unique or unusual tasks, such as creating custom glassware.
Unemployment: Replacing humans with robots can reduce jobs for skilled workers.
Skill Loss: Over-reliance on robots may lead to a decline in human skills like welding.
High Costs: Setting up and maintaining robots is very expensive.
Job Relocation: Factories equipped with robots can be moved to other regions, impacting local employment.

Lab Activity

🔬 Making a Robot Model from Cardboard

Understand basic robot features by creating a robot model using household materials. Then watch a video on "How Robots Work" and discuss with your class!

🧰 Materials Needed

📦 Cardboard boxes (small & medium) 🧻 Toilet paper / tissue rolls 🔘 Plastic bottle caps 🪙 Aluminum foil ✂️ Glue, tape, scissors 🎨 Markers or paint 💡 Optional: LED lights, old wires

📋 Steps

1

Prepare the Base

Use a medium-sized cardboard box as the robot's body. Attach a smaller box on top for the robot's head.

2

Add Features

Use tissue rolls or small cartons for arms and legs. Attach them with glue or tape. Stick bottle caps on the head as "eyes" or "buttons."

3

Decorate

Wrap parts in aluminum foil for a metallic look. Draw or paint a control panel on the robot's body.

4

Optional Additions

Attach wires or LED lights for a futuristic look. Create movable joints using split pins for a more dynamic model.

5

Show and Tell

Display your robot and explain its name, design, and the tasks it could perform if it were a real robot!
