Part 1

What vs. Which

Both words ask questions, but we use them in different situations. The key is: how many choices do you have?

🟢 Using WHICH

We use which when choosing between a small, limited number of things, people, or places. The choices are already known or restricted.

  • Which is your car, black or blue?
  • Which student is your best friend?
  • Which city do you want to visit?

🟠 Using WHAT

We use what when choosing between a large or unlimited number of things, people, or places. There are many possible answers.

  • What sort of cars do you like?
  • What do you want to be when you grow up?
  • What country would you like to visit?
💡 Quick Tip: The Choice Rule

Small number of choices → WHICH
"Which of these two books do you prefer?"
Large / open-ended choices → WHAT
"What book are you reading?"

🔍 Worked Example: Choosing Correctly

Let's practise choosing what or which step by step.

1 Read the sentence: "_____ colour do you want, red or green?"
2 Ask yourself: How many choices are there? → Only 2 choices (red or green).
3 Small, limited number of choices? → Use WHICH.
4 Answer: "Which colour do you want, red or green?" ✅

You can use what, which, and whose with OR without a noun.

✅ Whose are these? (without noun)

"How much water?"

"How many apples?"

If you can replace it with "who is", use who's.

✅ "Who's that?" → "Who is that?" ✓

Part 2

Whose vs. Who's

These two words sound exactly the same but have completely different meanings. Let's learn the difference!

🔑 Using WHOSE — Possession

We use whose for questions about possession (who owns something).

Structure / Form
Whose + (noun) + be (is/am/are) + extra words?
  • Whose jacket is that?
  • Whose are these?
  • Whose computer did you use?

🧩 Using WHO'S — Contraction

Who's is a contraction of "Who is" or "Who has". It sounds the same as whose but means something different.

  • Who's that? (= Who is that?)
  • Who's your favourite singer? (= Who is your favourite singer?)
⚠️ Common Mistake Alert!

Students often confuse whose and who's because they sound the same. Remember: who's = who is. If you can replace the word with "who is", use who's. If you're asking about ownership, use whose.

🔍 Worked Example: Whose or Who's?

Follow the steps to decide which word to use.

1 Sentence: "_____ shoes are those near the door?"
2 Test: Can you replace it with "who is"? → "Who is shoes are those?" ❌ Doesn't make sense.
3 Are you asking about ownership/possession? → Yes! (who owns the shoes?)
4 Use WHOSE: "Whose shoes are those near the door?" ✅
Part 3

How + Adjective / Adverb Questions

We can make detailed questions by combining how with an adjective or adverb. This gives us many useful question forms!

📐 How Questions

Use how followed by an adjective or adverb to ask for specific details.

  • How old is he? → Ten years old.
  • How fast can you run? → Ten kilometres per hour.
  • How much does it cost? → Twelve rupees.
QuestionPossible Answer
How old is your brother?He's 12 years old.
How often do you exercise?Three times a week.
How much does it weigh?Five kilograms.
How big is your house?Very big! Three floors.
How tall are you?I'm 130 cm tall.
How long is the movie?Two hours.
How fast is the car?200 km per hour.
📌 Note: How Much vs How Many

How much is used for quantity (uncountable nouns): "How much water do you drink?"
How many is used for numbers (countable nouns): "How many books do you have?"

🔍 Worked Example: Choosing a "How" Word

Let's complete: "How _____ are you?" (asking about age)

1 The question is asking about someone's age.
2 Which adjective means relating to age? → OLD
3 Combine: How old are you?
4 Possible answer: "I am 9 years old." ✅
