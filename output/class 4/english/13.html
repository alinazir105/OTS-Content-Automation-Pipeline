To + The Infinitive

When do we use "to" before a verb?

What is it?

When we place to in front of the base form of a verb (e.g., to study, to eat, to run), we call it the to-infinitive. It has several important uses in English sentences.

📋 Rules for To + Infinitive

RuleExample
To express purpose (why someone does something) She went to the library to study.
After adjectives It's easy to learn English.
After It + be + adjective It's important to drink water every day.
After certain verbs (hope, agree, want, decide…) She hopes to win the match.
NOT after modal verbs (must, can, will…) He must go. ✅
He must to go. ❌
🌳 Verbs + To + Infinitive
Purpose
She went to buy bread
He came to help
After Verbs
hope to win
agree to help
want to learn
decide to go
After Adjectives
easy to learn
nice to meet
important to drink

📌 Book Examples

  • She hopes to win the match.
  • He agreed to help his friend.
  • I went to the shop to buy some bread.
  • It's nice to meet new people.
  • We want to learn swimming.

How to rewrite: Combine two sentences by removing the second sentence's subject and using to + infinitive to show purpose.
Original Sentences

What was her purpose? → To see the flowers
Step 2 — Combine the sentences
Keep the first action, add "to + infinitive" for the purpose.

The -ing Form

Gerunds — when verbs become nouns or descriptors!

What is it?

The -ing form (gerund) is when we add -ing to the base verb (e.g., swim → swimming, read → reading). It can act as a noun, follow certain verbs, or come after prepositions.

📋 Rules for -ing Form

RuleExample
After certain verbs: like, enjoy, keep, hate, mind… I enjoy reading books.
After prepositions: by, for, about, on, in, at… He improved his skills by practising regularly.
As the subject of a sentence Swimming is a good exercise.
As the object of a sentence She likes swimming.
After go + recreational activity We go cycling every weekend.
For actions in progress (gerunds) I am cooking dinner right now.
🎭

Verbs that need -ing

enjoy, like, hate, keep, mind, love, avoid, finish, practise

🔗

Prepositions + -ing

good at drawing, by practising, for cooking, about running

⬆️

As Subject

Reading books improves vocabulary. Swimming is great exercise.

🏃

After "go"

go camping, go cycling, go swimming, go running

📌 Book Examples

  • I enjoy watching movies.
  • They went camping last week.
  • Reading books improves vocabulary.
  • She keeps forgetting her homework.
  • He is good at drawing pictures.

🔄 -ing Verbs Quick List

💡 Transform Tips

"It's easy to cook fish"
↓
"Cooking fish is easy."

Move the to + infinitive phrase to the start, change it to -ing, and flip the sentence!

Verbs + -ing: enjoy, keep, like, hate, mind, avoid, finish

Prepositions + -ing: good at drawing, by practising

As subject: Reading is fun. Swimming is healthy.

Go + -ing: go camping, go cycling, go swimming

Lesson Summary

Key points to remember!

✅ To + Infinitive

  • To express purpose
  • After adjectives (easy, nice, important)
  • After verbs: hope, want, agree, decide
  • NOT after modal verbs

🔄 -ing Form

  • After enjoy, like, keep, hate, mind
  • After prepositions (by, for, about)
  • As subject of a sentence
  • After go + recreational activity

🔑 The Golden Rule

Some verbs can be followed by either form (like, love, hate), but many are specific. Always check which pattern the verb needs!

Test yourself: "I enjoy ______ (swim)" → swimming ✅  |  "She hopes ______ (win)" → to win ✅
