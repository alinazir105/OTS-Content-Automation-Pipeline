Chapter 14 · Class 4 English

Use of CAN, COULD & BE ABLE TO

Learn how to talk about abilities and possibilities in the present, past, and future!

🟢 Present Abilities 🕐 Past Abilities 🚀 Future Abilities

What Are We Learning?

We use "can," "could," and "be able to" to talk about abilities and possibilities at different times.

📌 Three Key Modal Expressions

These expressions help us express what someone can do, what they could do before, or what they will be able to do in the future.

Time Expression Used For
Present can / is able to Current abilities & general skills
Past could / was/were able to Past abilities & specific past achievements
Future can / could / will be able to Future plans, possibilities & certain abilities

🗺️ Quick Structure Guide

CAN (Present): Subject + can + base verb
IS/AM/ARE ABLE TO: Subject + am/is/are + able to + base verb
COULD (Past): Subject + could + base verb
WAS/WERE ABLE TO: Subject + was/were + able to + base verb
WILL BE ABLE TO: Subject + will be able to + base verb

🌟 Key Rules

Can = general ability (I can swim)
Is able to = specific effort/situation
Could = general past ability
Was/Were able to = specific past success
Will be able to = planned future ability

💡 Quick Examples

I can ride a bicycle.
She is able to focus.
I could read at 5.
He was able to finish.
I will be able to speak French.

🧩 Remember!

"Can" & "Could" are used for general abilities.

"Is/Was able to" focuses on specific achievements or efforts in a situation.

🟢 Present

"Can" and "Is Able To"

Talk about what we can do now or in general.

📖 Use

Use can and is/am/are able to to talk about present and general abilities.

🔀 The Difference

"Can"

Used for general abilities and possibilities in everyday language.

Example: I can swim. (I know how to swim.)

"Is Able To"

Used for specific achievements or situations, often focusing on effort or success.

Example: She is able to focus on her homework even with the TV on. (Highlights effort in a situation.)

📝 Present Examples

  • ✅ I can ride a bicycle.
  • ✅ She is able to solve difficult maths problems.
  • ✅ We can play cricket after school.

🔍 Guided Example: Choosing CAN vs IS ABLE TO

Step 0 of 4

Step 1: Read the sentence

"___ (can / is able to) swim very fast."

Step 2: Is this a general skill or a specific effort?

Swimming fast is a general skill — something I know how to do at any time, not a special effort in one particular situation.

Step 3: Apply the rule

General ability → Use CAN. If it were about focusing despite distraction or achieving something difficult, use is able to.

✅ Step 4: Answer

I can swim very fast. ✔️

🕐 Past

"Could" and "Was/Were Able To"

Talk about what someone was able to do in the past.

📖 Use

Use could and was/were able to to talk about past abilities and achievements.

🔀 The Difference

"Could"

Describes general abilities in the past.

Example: She could paint beautifully when she was 10. (General skill in the past.)

"Was/Were Able To"

Focuses on specific achievements or successes.

Example: He was able to solve a tricky maths problem yesterday. (Specific achievement.)

📝 Past Examples

  • ✅ I could read books when I was 5.
  • ✅ He was able to finish his homework on time.
  • ✅ They could climb trees easily when they were younger.

🔍 Guided Example: COULD vs WAS ABLE TO

Click "Next Step" to work through the decision.

Step 0 of 4

Step 1: Read the sentence

"She ___ (could / was able to) finish the race despite being tired."

Step 2: Is this a general past skill or a specific achievement?

Finishing a race despite tiredness is a specific achievement — something that happened in a particular situation with effort.

Step 3: Apply the rule

Specific past achievement → Use WAS ABLE TO. If it were about a general skill she had in the past, use could.

✅ Step 4: Answer

She was able to finish the race despite being tired. ✔️

🕐 Past Structure

Subject + could + base verb

⚠️ Common Mistake

❌ He could finish his homework despite being tired.

✅ He was able to finish his homework despite being tired.

(Specific effort = was able to)

🚀 Future

"Can," "Could" & "Will Be Able To"

Talk about future possibilities, suggestions, and planned abilities.

🔀 Three Ways to Discuss the Future

"Can" in Future

Talk about things someone will be allowed or able to do in informal situations.

Example: You can go to the park tomorrow.

"Could" in Future

Used for possibilities or suggestions about the future.

Example: We could visit the museum next week.

"Will Be Able To"

Used for planned or certain future abilities.

Example: I will be able to speak French after my course.

📝 Future Examples

  • ✅ You can go to the park tomorrow.
  • ✅ We could visit the museum next week.
  • ✅ I will be able to speak French after my course.

🚀 Future Structure

CAN (informal):
Subject + can + base verb

COULD (suggestion):
Subject + could + base verb

WILL BE ABLE TO:
Subject + will be able to
+ base verb

💡 Tips

Use will be able to when you are certain about a future ability after practice, training, or completing something.

Use could to make polite suggestions.
