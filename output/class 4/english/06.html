Present Continuous Tense

Learn how to talk about actions happening right now, temporary situations, and future plans!

🎯 Actions Now ⏳ Temporary 📅 Future Plans 🔄 Changing Situations
How to Build It

Formation

🔑 Key Rule: Present Continuous = am / is / are + verb + ing
Affirmative:  Subject + am/is/are + verb(ing) + object Negative:     Subject + am/is/are + not + verb(ing) + object Interrogative: Am/Is/Are + subject + verb(ing) + object?

Formation Table

Type Structure Example
Affirmative Subject + am/is/are + verb(ing) + object She is walking to school now.
Negative Subject + am/is/are + not + verb(ing) + object She isn't walking to school now.
Interrogative Am/Is/Are + subject + verb(ing) + object? Is she walking to school now?

More Sentence Examples

Subject Affirmative ✅ Negative ❌ Interrogative ❓
I I am walking to school now. I am not walking to school now. Am I walking to school now?
She / He / It / Ali She is walking to school now. She isn't walking to school now. Is she walking to school now?
We / You / They They are walking to school now. They aren't walking to school now. Are they walking to school now?
When to Use It

Uses of Present Continuous

Use Examples
🔵 Actions happening now
The water is boiling.
I'm studying for an exam.
🟠 Temporary situations
She is working in Lahore this week.
They are staying at a hotel.
⏱ With time expressions
now, at the moment, this week, today, nowadays, these days
We're having dinner at the moment.
Why are you walking today?
I'm learning English nowadays.
📅 Future plans
We are going to Lahore next week.
She is buying a new car by the end of this month.
🔄 Changing situations
She is getting bored.
It is getting cold in Quetta.

⚡ Quick Rules

  • Use am with "I"
  • Use is with she, he, it, name
  • Use are with we, you, they
  • Add -ing to the main verb
  • For negatives, add not after am/is/are
  • For questions, move am/is/are before the subject

⏰ Time Expressions

now today at the moment this week nowadays these days right now currently

📝 -ing Spelling Tips

  • walk → walking
  • run → running (double consonant)
  • write → writing (drop final -e)
  • study → studying
  • sit → sitting (double consonant)
