What is an Adjective?

An adjective is a word that describes or modifies a noun or pronoun. It provides additional information about the quality, size, colour, shape, quantity, or condition of the noun or pronoun, making sentences more descriptive and engaging.

🌸
beautiful flower
🏠
large house
👗
red dress
📚
five books
🛣️
curvy road
📦
damaged box

In the examples above, each bold word is the adjective that describes the noun beside it.

Types of Adjectives

Adjectives describe nouns in different ways. Here are the main types with examples from the lesson:

✨ Quality
This is a beautiful flower.

Describes the character or nature of something.

📏 Size
He has a large house.

Tells us about the physical size of a noun.

🎨 Colour
She is wearing a red dress.

Describes the colour of a noun.

🔢 Quantity
There are five books on the table.

Tells us how many or how much.

🔷 Shape
The road is curvy.

Describes the shape or form of a noun.

🩹 Condition
The box is damaged.

Tells us the current state or condition.

🧠 Remember: Adjectives usually come before the noun they describe (e.g., "a beautiful flower") but can also come after a linking verb (e.g., "The box is damaged").

Degree Definition Example How to Form
Positive Base form of the adjective, no comparison. This flower is beautiful. Use the adjective as it is. E.g., tall, big, beautiful
Comparative Compares two things or people. This flower is more beautiful than that one. Add -er (one syllable) or use more (two syllables)
Superlative Compares three or more things or people. This is the most beautiful flower in the garden. Add -est (one syllable) or use most (two syllables)

🔍 Guided Example: Using Degrees

Press Next Step to reveal each step of choosing the right degree.

Step 1 — Read the sentence
Sentence: "The Nile is the _____ river in the world."
Options: long / longer / longest
Step 2 — Count what is being compared
The sentence compares the Nile to ALL rivers in the world (more than 2 things).
Step 3 — Choose the degree
Comparing 3 or more → we need the Superlative Degree.
Step 4 — Apply the rule
"long" is one syllable → add -est → longest
✅ Answer: "The Nile is the longest river in the world."

Forming Comparative & Superlative Degrees

Different types of adjectives follow different rules when forming comparative and superlative degrees:

📏

Short Adjectives (1 syllable)

Add -er for comparative and -est for superlative.

tall → taller → tallest
big → bigger → biggest
🔤

Adjectives Ending in -y

Replace -y with -ier for comparative and -iest for superlative.

happy → happier → happiest
🔠

Long Adjectives (2+ syllables)

Use more for comparative and most for superlative.

beautiful → more beautiful → most beautiful
⚡

Irregular Adjectives

The word form completely changes — must be memorised!

good → better → best
bad → worse → worst
📌 Tip: When an adjective ends in a consonant + vowel + consonant (like "big"), double the final consonant before adding -er or -est: big → bigger → biggest.
