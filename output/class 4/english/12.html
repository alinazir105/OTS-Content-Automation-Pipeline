Future Continuous Tense

Learn how to describe actions that will be in progress at a specific time in the future!

Subject + will be + Verb(-ing) + Object
📌 Quick Structure ▼
Affirmative: Subject + will be + verb(-ing)
Negative: Subject + won't be + verb(-ing)
Interrogative: Will + subject + be + verb(-ing)?
💡 4 Main Uses ▼
  • Actions in progress at a specific future time
  • Polite inquiries about future plans
  • Predicting ongoing future actions
  • Changing and developing situations
🔑 Key Signal Words ▼
at 8 AM tomorrow, next week, in the evening, when we arrive, at this time tomorrow
1

Formation

How to build the Future Continuous Tense

Key Idea: The Future Continuous Tense shows that an action will be happening at a particular moment in the future. It is formed using will be + the -ing form of the verb.
// AFFIRMATIVE Subject + will be + Verb(-ing) + Object // NEGATIVE Subject + will not (won't) be + Verb(-ing) + Object // INTERROGATIVE Will + Subject + be + Verb(-ing) + Object + ?
Type Structure Example
Affirmative Subject + will be + verb(-ing) + object She will be walking to school at 8 AM.
Negative Subject + won't be + verb(-ing) + object She won't be walking to school at 8 AM.
Interrogative Will + subject + be + verb(-ing) + object? Will she be walking to school at 8 AM?

Sentence Patterns with Different Subjects

Subject: I
Affirmative I will be walking to school at 8 AM.
Negative I won't be walking to school at 8 AM.
Interrogative Will I be walking to school at 8 AM?
Subject: She / He / It / Ali
Affirmative She will be walking to school tomorrow.
Negative She won't be walking to school tomorrow.
Interrogative Will she be walking to school tomorrow?
Subject: We / You / They
Affirmative They will be watching the game.
Negative They won't be watching the game.
Interrogative Will they be watching the game?

📌 Quick Structure

Affirmative
Subject + will be + verb(-ing)
Negative
Subject + won't be + verb(-ing)
Interrogative
Will + subject + be + verb(-ing)?

⚠️ Watch Out!

  • Always use be after will
  • Verb must end in -ing
  • Never say "will be walk" ❌
  • Say "will be walking" ✓

🔑 Time Signal Words

These words often appear with Future Continuous:

at 8 AM tomorrow next week in the evening when we arrive at this time
2

Uses of Future Continuous

4 important ways to use this tense

🕐 Actions in Progress at a Specific Time

Describes an action that will be happening at a certain point in the future.

She will be studying at 10 AM tomorrow.
I will be watching TV at 9 PM tonight.

🤝 Polite Inquiries About Future Plans

Used to ask about someone's plans in a polite and indirect way.

Will you be using the car later?
Will they be travelling next week?

🔮 Predicting Ongoing Future Actions

Used to predict or assume what will be happening at a future moment.

They will be watching the match when we arrive.
The kids will be sleeping when we get home.

📈 Changing and Developing Situations

Describes situations that are expected to change or develop over time.

She will be getting bored soon.
He will be feeling better by tomorrow.

Structure Overview

graph TD A[Future Continuous Tense] --> B[Affirmative] A --> C[Negative] A --> D[Interrogative] B --> E["Subject + will be + verb-ing"] C --> F["Subject + won't be + verb-ing"] D --> G["Will + Subject + be + verb-ing?"] E --> H["She will be walking to school"] F --> I["She won't be walking to school"] G --> J["Will she be walking to school?"]

Guided Examples

Follow each step to build sentences

📝 Building a Future Continuous Sentence

Click "Next Step" to reveal each step of building a sentence.

Task: Change "He plays football in the evening" to Future Continuous.

Step 1 — Identify the Subject
Subject = He
The subject does NOT change in Future Continuous.
Step 2 — Add "will be"
He will be ...
We always use "will be" — it doesn't change for any subject!
Step 3 — Convert Verb to -ing form
play → playing
Add -ing to the base form of the verb.
Step 4 — Add the Rest
He will be playing football in the evening.
Don't forget the time expression!
✅ Final Answer
He will be playing football in the evening.

📝 Making a Negative Sentence

Click each step to learn how negatives work.

Task: Make negative: "They will be watching TV in the evening."

Step 1 — Find "will be"
Original: They will be watching TV in the evening.
Step 2 — Replace with "won't be"
They won't be watching TV in the evening.
"won't" = "will not" (contraction)
✅ Final Answer
They won't be watching TV in the evening.

📝 Making an Interrogative Sentence

Click each step to form a question.

Task: Turn into a question: "She will be reading a book at 8 AM."

Step 1 — Move "Will" to the Front
Affirmative: She will be reading...
Will → moves to the beginning of the sentence.
Step 2 — Place Subject After "Will"
Will she be reading a book at 8 AM?
Step 3 — Add Question Mark
Don't forget the ? at the end!
✅ Final Answer
Will she be reading a book at 8 AM?

💡 Remember

For interrogatives, move WILL to the start:

She will be → Will she be...?

🔄 Will NOT = Won't

Both are correct! Contractions are more common in spoken English.

will not be walking
= same meaning =
won't be walking

❌ Common Mistakes

  • She will be walk ❌
  • She will be walking ✓
  • Were they will be playing? ❌
  • Will they be playing? ✓

Everything you need to remember

Always use: will be + verb(-ing)

Subject + will be + walking/playing/studying...

Subject + will be + verb(-ing) + object

➖ Negative

Subject + won't be + verb(-ing) + object

She won't be walking to school at 8 AM.

❓ Interrogative

Will + subject + be + verb(-ing) + object?

Will she be walking to school at 8 AM?
🌟 4 Uses to Remember:
  • Actions in progress at a specific future time — "She will be studying at 10 AM tomorrow."
  • Polite inquiries about future plans — "Will you be using the car later?"
  • Predicting ongoing future actions — "They will be watching the match when we arrive."
  • Changing/developing situations — "She will be getting bored."
