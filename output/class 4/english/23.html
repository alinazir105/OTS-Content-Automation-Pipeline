Poem 23.1

Be Thankful

A poem of gratitude reminding us to appreciate everything in our lives — written by Anonymous.

🌸
Poem 23.1

Be Thankful

"By Anonymous"
Be thankful for every day, And all the small things that come your way.
Be thankful for your health, And fill your heart with gratitude and wealth.
Be thankful for the roof over your head, And that you have a warm, comfy bed.
Be thankful for the knowledge you know, And the learning you have left to grow.
Be thankful for nature all around, And the soothing, calming sounds.
Be thankful for the air that you breathe, And for every blessing you receive.

🌟 Main Theme: Gratitude

The poem encourages us to be thankful for everyday blessings — our health, shelter, knowledge, nature, and the air we breathe. The key message is: Be thankful for everything you receive.

📚 Key Vocabulary

Gratitude
The quality of being thankful; readiness to show appreciation.
Blessing
A thing that brings happiness or good fortune.
Soothing
Having a calming, gentle, or pleasant effect.
Wealth
Here used to mean richness of heart or spirit, not just money.

🔍 Analysing the Poem — Step by Step

❓ Question: What is the main message of the poem "Be Thankful"?
Step 1 — Read the Title
The title is "Be Thankful". This immediately tells us the poem is about gratitude. We can predict the poem will ask us to appreciate things in our life.
Step 2 — Find the Repeated Phrase
Every stanza starts with "Be thankful for..." — this repetition is called anaphora. It emphasises the message: keep being grateful!
Step 3 — List What We Are Thankful For
The poem lists: every day, health, a home (roof + bed), knowledge, nature, and air. These are basic needs and gifts in life.
Step 4 — Identify the Main Message
Answer: The main message is that we should be grateful for everything we receive — both big and small things in life.

Poem 23.2

The Caterpillar

By Christina Rossetti — A gentle poem about the life cycle of a caterpillar, full of hope and transformation.

🦋
Poem 23.2

The Caterpillar

"By Christina Rossetti"
Brown and furry, Caterpillar in a hurry; Take your walk To the shady leaf, or stalk.
May no toad spy you, May the little birds pass by you; Spin and die, To live again a butterfly.

🦋 Main Theme: Transformation & Hope

The poem follows a caterpillar's journey. The poet wishes it safety so it can complete its metamorphosis. The final line — "To live again a butterfly" — is a powerful message of renewal and hope.

📚 Key Vocabulary

Furry
Covered with fur or soft hair; fluffy.
Stalk
The stem of a plant that supports leaves or flowers.
Spy
To see or catch sight of; to notice.
Metamorphosis
The process of transformation (e.g., caterpillar → butterfly).

🎨 Themes & Devices in "The Caterpillar"

🎨
Imagery
"Brown and furry" creates a vivid picture of the caterpillar.
🙏
Wish / Prayer
"May no toad spy you" — the poet wishes the caterpillar safety.
🦋
Transformation
The caterpillar dies as a cocoon to be reborn as a butterfly.
🎵
Rhyme Scheme
furry/hurry, walk/stalk, you/you, die/butterfly.
🦋 Life Cycle Stages
🥚
Egg — Laid on a leaf
🐛
Caterpillar (Larva) — Eats and grows
🫙
Cocoon (Pupa) — "Spin and die" stage
🦋
Butterfly (Adult) — "Live again"

📅

Poem 23.3

The Crocodile

By Lewis Carroll — A witty, ironic poem about a sly crocodile luring fish with a deceptive smile.

🐊
Poem 23.3

The Crocodile

"By Lewis Carroll"
How doth the little crocodile Improve his shining tail, And pour the waters of the Nile On every golden scale!
How cheerfully he seems to grin, How neatly spreads his claws, And welcomes little fishes in, With gently smiling jaws!

🐊 Main Theme: Deception & Irony

The crocodile appears cheerful and welcoming, but it is actually luring the fish to eat them. This is irony — the poem describes danger in gentle, positive-sounding words. The "gently smiling jaws" are actually deadly!

📚 Key Vocabulary

Doth
Old English word meaning "does".
Scale
One of many small thin plates covering a reptile's body.
Grin
To smile widely, showing teeth.
Jaws
The upper and lower bones of the mouth, containing teeth.

📅

📚

😄

🎭 Irony Explained

The crocodile "smiles" and "welcomes" the fish — words that sound friendly. But the crocodile is really going to eat them! When words mean the opposite of what they seem, it's called irony.
