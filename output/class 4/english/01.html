Section 1

Countable & Uncountable Nouns

Learn the difference between nouns you can count and those you cannot.

✅ Countable Nouns

Can be counted and have singular and plural forms.

book car dog pen tomato egg

🔒 Uncountable Nouns

Cannot be counted, only have a singular form.

cake bread money milk butter water

💡 Key Rule

Uncountable nouns are measured using container/measure words:
a jar of butter · a bucket of water · a glass of milk · a cup of tea

📋 Plural Cheat Sheet

  • Most nouns → add -s
  • Ends in -s/-sh/-ch/-x/-z → add -es
  • Consonant + y → change y to -ies
  • Vowel + y → just add -s
  • -f/-fe → change to -ves
  • Some -f words → just add -s
  • Irregular: learn by heart!

  • woman → women
  • child → children

  • foot → feet
  • mouse → mice
  • sheep → sheep (same!)
  • fish → fish (same!)
Section 3

Quantities & Articles

Know when to use a / an / the / some and when to use no article.

a / an

Singular Countable

Use a before consonants, an before vowel sounds.

a car, an orange, an egg, a book

some

Plural or Uncountable

Use with plural countable nouns or uncountable nouns.

some chairs, some water, some bread

the

Specific / Known

Use for specific or previously mentioned nouns.

the sun, the piano, the dog

🔑 a vs an — The Key Rule

Use "a" before words starting with a consonant sound: a car, a book, a pencil, a table, a uniform (sounds like "y")

Use "an" before words starting with a vowel sound: an apple, an egg, an orange, an idea, an umbrella

🎯 When to Use "THE"

Use the for:

  • Something already mentioned: I saw a dog. The dog was barking.
  • Something unique: The sun is shining.
  • Musical instruments: She plays the piano.
  • Specific things: Can you pass me the salt?

🚫 No Article — When?

With plural nouns and uncountable nouns used in a general sense:

Fruit is good for health.
Water is essential for life.
Nurses work in hospitals.
Children like to play outside.
Education is important for everyone.

💡
Step-by-Step: Choosing the Right Article

Let's figure out which article to use for: "I saw ___ elephant at ___ zoo yesterday."

Step 1 — Identify the noun
The nouns are elephant and zoo. Both are countable nouns (singular).
Step 2 — Is it first mentioned or specific?
Elephant is mentioned for the first time → use a/an.
Zoo — we mean a specific/known zoo (we both know which one) → use the.
Step 3 — a or an?
Elephant starts with a vowel sound (E) → use an.
an elephant
✅ Final Answer
I saw an elephant at the zoo yesterday.
I saw an elephant at the zoo yesterday.

📌 Article Rules at a Glance

  • a + consonant sound: a book
  • an + vowel sound: an apple
  • the = specific/known: the sun
  • some = plural/uncountable: some water
  • No article = general nouns: Milk is healthy

📚 Study Tips

  • Countable nouns can have "a/an" before them
  • If you can say "one ___", it's countable
  • Uncountable = no "a/an" directly before it
  • Use "some" for both types in positive sentences
  • "The" = we both know which one
  • "An" comes before vowel sounds (a, e, i, o, u)
  • No article for general statements
