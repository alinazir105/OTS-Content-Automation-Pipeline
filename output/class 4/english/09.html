Present Perfect Tense

Learn how to talk about actions that happened in the past but are connected to the present — using have / has + past participle.

Formation

The Present Perfect Tense is formed using an auxiliary verb (have or has) followed by the past participle of the main verb. There are three types of sentences you need to know:

🔑 Key Rule: Use has with singular subjects (she, he, it, Ali) and have with plural or I/you/we/they.
✅ AFFIRMATIVE: Subject + have/has + past participle + object ❌ NEGATIVE: Subject + have/has + NOT + past participle + object ❓ INTERROGATIVE: Have/Has + Subject + past participle + object?

Formation Table

TypeStructureExample
AffirmativeSubject + have/has + past participle + objectShe has walked to school today.
NegativeSubject + have/has + not + past participle + objectShe hasn't walked to school today.
InterrogativeHave/Has + subject + past participle + object?Has she walked to school today?

Subject + Auxiliary Combinations

SubjectAffirmativeNegativeInterrogative
I I have walked to school today. I haven't walked to school today. Have I walked to school today?
She / He / It / Ali She has walked to school today. She hasn't walked to school today. Has she walked to school today?
We / You / They They have walked to school today. They haven't walked to school today. Have they walked to school today?

🛠️ How to Form a Present Perfect Sentence

Let's build the sentence: "She finish homework."

Step 1 ✅ Identify the subject: She → singular, so use has
Step 2 ✅ Find the past participle of finish: finished
Step 3 ✅ Build affirmative: She has finished her homework.
Step 4 ❌ Build negative: She hasn't finished her homework.
Step 5 ❓ Build question: Has she finished her homework?

⚡ Quick Reference

  • Use has with He/She/It/Ali
  • Use have with I/We/You/They
  • Negative: add not after have/has
  • Question: invert have/has and subject
  • Contraction: haven't = have not
  • Contraction: hasn't = has not

📝 Common Past Participles

  • walk → walked
  • finish → finished
  • visit → visited
  • eat → eaten
  • see → seen
  • call → called
  • live → lived
  • go → gone
  • read → read
  • catch → caught
  • meet → met
  • clean → cleaned

🔍 Signal Words

  • already
  • yet
  • just
  • ever / never
  • for (+ duration)
  • many times
  • this week / month
  • today

Uses of Present Perfect Tense

We use the Present Perfect Tense when we have a present result of a past action. The action happened in the past but is still relevant or connected to the present moment.

✅

Completed Actions

An action that was completed recently, often with a connection to now.

I have visited Lahore.
She has finished her homework.
⏳

Ongoing Duration

An action that started in the past and continues to the present.

He has lived here for five years.
🔄

Repeated Actions

An action that has happened more than once up to now.

They have gone to the library many times this month.
💡 Remember: When we have a present result of a past action, we use the Present Perfect Tense. The exact time of the action is NOT important.
