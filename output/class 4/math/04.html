Key Concepts

Understanding the parts of a division problem.

What is Division?

Division is the inverse operation of multiplication. It is the process of finding how many times a divisor can be taken from the dividend.

Dividend

The number to be divided. It goes inside the division bracket.

Divisor

The number which divides the dividend. It goes outside the bracket.

Quotient

The result obtained after dividing. It appears on top of the bracket.

Remainder

The number left over that cannot be divided further by the divisor.

Example: 36 ÷ 4

−36
0 Remainder

Check:

Quotient × Divisor + Remainder = Dividend

9 × 4 + 0 = 36 ✓

Quotient × Divisor + Remainder = Dividend

Always use this to verify your answer!

💡Quick Tips

1

Always divide from left to right, starting with the largest place value.

2

If a digit is smaller than the divisor, bring down the next digit.

3

The remainder must always be less than the divisor.

4

Multiply to check: Quotient × Divisor + Remainder = Dividend.

Guided Examples

Example 1 Simple Division   72 ÷ 8

Step 1 — Identify the parts
Dividend = 72, Divisor = 8
Step 2 — Think: 8 × ? = 72
8 × 9 = 72, so the Quotient = 9
Step 3 — Subtract
72 − 72 = 0 → Remainder = 0
Step 4 — Verify
9 × 8 + 0 = 72 ✓
Final Answer
72 ÷ 8 = 9
// Quotient = 9, Remainder = 0

Example 2 With Remainder   56 ÷ 5

Step 1 — Identify the parts
Dividend = 56, Divisor = 5
Step 2 — Divide first digit: 5 ÷ 5 = 1
Write 1 above the tens column. 5 × 1 = 5. Subtract: 5 − 5 = 0. Bring down 6.
Step 3 — Divide 6 ÷ 5 = 1 remainder 1
Write 1 above the units column. 5 × 1 = 5. Subtract: 6 − 5 = 1.
Step 4 — Record
Quotient = 11, Remainder = 1
Step 5 — Verify
11 × 5 + 1 = 55 + 1 = 56 ✓
Final Answer
56 ÷ 5 = 11 R 1
// Quotient = 11, Remainder = 1

Example 3 3-Digit Dividend   451 ÷ 5

Step 1 — Look at hundreds: 4 ÷ 5
4 is less than 5, so look at the first two digits: 45 ÷ 5 = 9. Write 9 above tens column.
Step 2 — Subtract: 45 − 45 = 0
Bring down the ones digit: 1. Now we have 01.
Step 3 — Divide 1 ÷ 5
1 is less than 5, so Quotient digit = 0, Remainder = 1.
Step 4 — Verify
90 × 5 + 1 = 450 + 1 = 451 ✓
Final Answer
451 ÷ 5 = 90 R 1
// Quotient = 90, Remainder = 1

Dividing by 10, 100 & 1,000

When you divide by powers of 10, the digits shift to the right.

The Pattern

When dividing by 10 → shift digits 1 column right  |  By 100 → shift 2 columns  |  By 1,000 → shift 3 columns  |  By 10,000 → shift 4 columns

Operation TTh (10,000s) Th (1,000s) H (100s) T (10s) U (1s) Result
52,000 ÷ 10 052005,200
52,000 ÷ 100 00520520
52,000 ÷ 1,000 0005252
52,000 ÷ 10,000 00005.25.2

Visual: Digit Shift When Dividing by Powers of 10

Example: 76 ÷ 10

Solution
Divide: 7 × 10 = 70
Subtract: 76 − 70 = 6
Quotient = 7, Remainder = 6
So: 76 ÷ 10 = 7 R 6

Example: 868 ÷ 10

Solution
Step 1: 8 ÷ 1 (tens) = 80
800 → bring down 68
Step 2: 68 ÷ 10 = 6 remainder 8
Quotient = 86, Remainder = 8

Example: 396 ÷ 100

Solution
3 × 100 = 300
396 − 300 = 96
Quotient = 3, Remainder = 96

Example: 4,129 ÷ 1,000

Solution
4 × 1000 = 4000
4129 − 4000 = 129
Quotient = 4, Remainder = 129
// So, 4129 ÷ 1000 = 4 R 129

🎯Key Insight

The number of zeros in the divisor tells you how many places to shift the digits to the right.

10 → 1 zero → 1 shift
100 → 2 zeros → 2 shifts
1000 → 3 zeros → 3 shifts

⚠️Remember!

!

When shifting right, digits that fall off become the remainder.

!

The remainder is always less than the divisor.

!

If the number divides exactly, remainder = 0.
