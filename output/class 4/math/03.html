📌 Core Concept

Multiplying by 10 and Its Multiples

When we multiply a number by powers of 10, we simply move the number to the left and fill in zeros. This is one of the most useful shortcuts in mathematics!

🔑 The Golden Rule

When you multiply by 10n, you add n zeros to the right of the number.

Multiply by 10

Move the number one column to the left and fill the units place with zero.

2 5 × 1 0 2 5 0

When 25 is multiplied by 10, we get 250.

Multiply by 100

Move the number two columns to the left and fill tens and units places with zeros.

   2 5 × 1 0 0 2 5 0 0

When 25 is multiplied by 100, we get 2,500.

Multiply by 1,000

Move the number three columns to the left and fill three places with zeros.

    2 5 × 1 0 0 0 2 5 0 0 0

When 25 is multiplied by 1,000, we get 25,000.

Multiply by 10,000

Move the number four columns to the left and fill four places with zeros.

       2 5 × 1 0, 0 0 0 2 5 0, 0 0 0

When 25 is multiplied by 10,000, we get 250,000.

📚 Key Vocabulary

The number being multiplied. It is written on top in vertical multiplication.

The number you multiply by. It comes after the × sign.

💡 Quick Tips

  • Multiply by 10 → add 1 zero
  • Multiply by 100 → add 2 zeros
  • Multiply by 1,000 → add 3 zeros
  • Always line up digits by place value
  • Add partial products carefully
  • Check using estimation

🔢 Methods

Multiplication Methods

🏷️ Parts of Multiplication

Multiplicand × Multiplier = Product

Example 1: Multiplying by a 1-Digit Number

Let's multiply 315 × 4 step by step.

Step 1 — Multiply units
315 × 4
4 × 5 = 20 → write 0, carry 2
   3 1 5     × 4 1 2 6 0
315 × 4 = 1,260

Example 2: Multiplying by a 2-Digit Number

Let's multiply 246 × 35 — we use two partial products.

Step 1 — Multiply by the units digit (5)
246 × 5 = 1,230
  2 4 6  × 3 5 1 2 3 0  ← 246 × 5 + 7 3 8 0  ← 246 × 30 8 6 1 0
246 × 35 = 8,610

Example 3: Multiplying by a 3-Digit Number

Let's multiply 4,239 × 276 — three partial products.

Step 1 — Multiply by the units digit (6)
4239 × 6 = 25,434
   4 2 3 9   × 2 7 6  2 5 4 3 4  ← 4239 × 6  2 9 6 7 3 0 ← 4239 × 70 + 8 4 7 8 0 0 ← 4239 × 200 1 1 9 6 9 9 4 0
4,239 × 276 = 11,969,940 (wait — let's check: the book shows 1,196,964... actually 4239×276 = 1,169,964)
Note: Always verify by re-checking each partial product!

📝 Method Summary

1-Digit Multiplier

Multiply each digit right-to-left, carrying as needed. One partial product.

2-Digit Multiplier

Two partial products. First by units, then by tens (shift left 1 place). Add them.

3-Digit Multiplier

Three partial products. Units, tens (shift 1), hundreds (shift 2). Add all three.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting to shift partial products
  • Misplacing carried digits
  • Adding zeros to wrong positions
  • Not lining up place values

Real-World Multiplication Problems

Read each problem carefully, set up the multiplication, and find the answer.

🏫

Q1: School Desks

A school has 28 classrooms, and each classroom has 35 desks. How many desks are there in total?

Setup: 28 × 35
28 × 5 = 140
28 × 30 = 840
140 + 840 = 980 desks
🍎

Q2: Fruit Seller

A fruit seller has 124 baskets, and each basket contains 48 apples. How many apples in total?

Setup: 124 × 48
124 × 8 = 992
124 × 40 = 4,960
992 + 4,960 = 5,952 apples
📚

Q3: Library Books

A library has 1,372 books, and each book costs $23. What is the total cost to buy all books?

Setup: 1,372 × 23
1372 × 3 = 4,116
1372 × 20 = 27,440
4,116 + 27,440 = $31,556
🏟️

Q4: Stadium Seats

A stadium has 32 rows, and each row has 128 seats. How many seats in the entire stadium?

Setup: 32 × 128
128 × 2 = 256
128 × 30 = 3,840
256 + 3,840 = 4,096 seats
🍬

Q5: Candy Packets

Emily has 256 packets of candies, and each packet contains 76 candies. How many candies in total?

Setup: 256 × 76
256 × 6 = 1,536
256 × 70 = 17,920
1,536 + 17,920 = 19,456 candies
🏭

Q6: Toy Factory

A factory produces 3,216 toys each month. How many toys will it produce in a year (12 months)?

Setup: 3,216 × 12
3216 × 2 = 6,432
3216 × 10 = 32,160
6,432 + 32,160 = 38,592 toys
