Lesson 1

Units of Measurement

Mass tells us how much matter is in an object. We use two main units.

🏋️ Kilogram (kg)

Used to measure heavier objects.
Example: a bag of sugar weighs 1 kg.

📎 Gram (g)

Used to measure lighter objects.
Example: a paperclip weighs about 5 g.

Key Conversion

1 kg = 1,000 g ↔ 1,000 g = 1 kg

Types of Weighing Instruments

⚖️ Common Balance

Has two pans. Place the item on one side and standard weights on the other. When balanced, they are equal in mass.

🔢 Weighing Machine

Shows the weight directly on a dial. No separate weights needed — just place the object and read the dial.

Lesson 2

Conversion of Units

Learn to convert between grams and kilograms in both directions.

Grams → Kilograms

kg = g ÷ 1,000

Example: 5,000 g ÷ 1,000 = 5 kg

Example: 3,500 g ÷ 1,000 = 3.5 kg

Kilograms → Grams

g = kg × 1,000

Example: 34 kg 108 g
= (34 × 1,000) + 108 = 34,108 g

🧩 Guided Worked Example

Convert 7 kg 250 g to grams

Step 1
Identify the kg part: 7 kg
Step 2
Convert kg to g: 7 × 1,000 = 7,000 g
Step 3
Add the remaining grams: 7,000 + 250 = 7,250 g
✅ Answer
7 kg 250 g = 7,250 g

Lesson 3

Addition of Mass

Adding kg and g values step by step — including carrying over!

📋 Steps for Addition

  1. Write kilograms (kg) and grams (g) in separate columns.
  2. Add grams first.
  3. If grams total exceeds 1,000, carry over the extra to kilograms.
  4. Add the kg column (including any carry).

🧩 Guided Example: Addition

18 kg 925 g + 12 kg 575 g = ?

Step 1 – Grams Column
925 + 575 = 1,500 g
Step 2 – Carry over
1,500 g = 1 kg + 500 g → write 500 g, carry 1 kg
Step 3 – Kilograms Column
18 + 12 + 1 (carry) = 31 kg
✅ Answer
18 kg 925 g + 12 kg 575 g = 31 kg 500 g

Lesson 4

Subtraction of Mass

Subtracting mass — including borrowing from kilograms when needed.

📋 Steps for Subtraction

  1. Write kilograms (kg) and grams (g) in separate columns.
  2. Subtract grams first.
  3. If top grams < bottom grams, borrow 1 kg (= 1,000 g) from the kg column.
  4. Subtract the kg column.

🧩 Guided Example: Subtraction

18 kg 450 g − 9 kg 600 g = ?

Step 1 – Grams Column
Can we do 450 − 600? No! (450 < 600) → We need to borrow.
Step 2 – Borrow 1 kg
Borrow 1 kg from 18 kg → 18 − 1 = 17 kg
Add 1,000 g: 450 + 1,000 = 1,450 g
Step 3 – Subtract Grams
1,450 − 600 = 850 g
Step 4 – Subtract Kilograms
17 − 9 = 8 kg
✅ Answer
18 kg 450 g − 9 kg 600 g = 8 kg 850 g

Lesson 6

Word Problems

Apply your knowledge to real-life mass problems. Try solving first, then reveal the answer!

Q1: John purchases 7 kg 200 g of sugar and 9 kg 395 g of rice. What is the total weight?

Solution:
7 kg 200 g + 9 kg 395 g
Grams: 200 + 395 = 595 g
kg: 7 + 9 = 16 kg
Answer: 16 kg 595 g
Q2: Raheela buys 8 kg 400 g of tomatoes, 2 kg 130 g of brinjal, and 7 kg 300 g of watermelon. How much does she buy in total?

Solution:
8 kg 400 g + 2 kg 130 g + 7 kg 300 g
Grams: 400 + 130 + 300 = 830 g
kg: 8 + 2 + 7 = 17 kg
Answer: 17 kg 830 g
Q3: The total weight of Tania's bag is 45 kg 750 g, and Diana's bag is 43 kg 950 g. Whose bag is heavier and by how much?

Solution:
Compare: 45 kg 750 g vs 43 kg 950 g → Tania's bag is heavier.
45 kg 750 g − 43 kg 950 g:
Grams: 750 < 950, borrow 1 kg → 1,750 − 950 = 800 g
kg: (45−1) − 43 = 44 − 43 = 1 kg
Answer: Tania's bag is heavier by 1 kg 800 g
Q4: A truck carries 352 kg 100 g of pumpkins and 207 kg 432 g of watermelons. Find the total weight.

Solution:
352 kg 100 g + 207 kg 432 g
Grams: 100 + 432 = 532 g
kg: 352 + 207 = 559 kg
Answer: 559 kg 532 g
Q5: A shopkeeper sells 67 kg 626 g of wheat on Saturday and 125 kg 200 g on Sunday. Find the total weight of wheat sold.

Solution:
67 kg 626 g + 125 kg 200 g
Grams: 626 + 200 = 826 g
kg: 67 + 125 = 192 kg
Answer: 192 kg 826 g
