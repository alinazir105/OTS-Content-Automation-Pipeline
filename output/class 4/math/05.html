What is a Fraction?

A fraction represents a part of a whole. It tells us how many parts of a certain size we have.

📌 Definition

A fraction has two main parts: the numerator (top number — how many parts we have) and the denominator (bottom number — how many equal parts make the whole).

FRACTION STRUCTURE

3 ← Numerator (parts we have)
───── ← Fraction bar
4 ← Denominator (total equal parts)

For example, in the fraction ³⁄₄: 3 is the numerator and 4 is the denominator. This means 3 out of 4 equal parts.

🍕

Pizza Example

A pizza divided into 4 equal slices — eating 1 slice means you ate ¼ of the pizza.

🍫

Chocolate Bar

A chocolate bar broken into 2 equal pieces — each piece is ½ of the bar.

🍎

Apple Halves

Cut an apple into 2 equal parts — each part is ½ of the apple.

Types of Fractions

There are three main types of fractions. Understanding each type helps you work with fractions effectively.

Proper Fraction

Numerator < Denominator

3/5

The numerator is smaller than the denominator. The value is always less than 1.

More examples: ½, ⅔, ⁴⁄₇, ⁵⁄₆

Improper Fraction

Numerator ≥ Denominator

5/4

The numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator. The value is 1 or more.

More examples: ⁷⁄₄, ⁹⁄₅, ¹¹⁄₃

Mixed Number

Whole Number + Fraction

1½

A mixed number combines a whole number and a proper fraction.

More examples: 2¾, 3⅓, 4½

Like and Unlike Fractions

🟰

Like Fractions

Same denominator. These fractions are easy to add and subtract directly.

Example: ¼ and ¾ — both have denominator 4.

¼ + ²⁄₄ = ¾
≠

Unlike Fractions

Different denominators. Need to find a common denominator before adding or subtracting.

Example: ⅓ and ⅕ — denominators 3 and 5 are different.

⅓ ≠ ⅕

Comparing Fractions

Fractions show parts of a whole. We can compare them to see which is larger or smaller.

🔑 Comparing Rules

  • Same denominators: Compare numerators — larger numerator = larger fraction
  • Same numerators: Compare denominators — larger denominator = smaller fraction (smaller slices)
  • Different both: Find common denominator first, then compare

Worked Example: Comparing ³⁄₈ and ³⁄₄

Which is greater: ³⁄₈ or ³⁄₄?
Observation
The numerators are the same (both = 3). When numerators are equal, compare the denominators.
Step 1 — Compare Denominators
Denominators are 8 and 4. A fraction with a larger denominator means each piece is smaller. So ⅛ < ¼.
Step 2 — Conclusion
Since ⅛ < ¼, then ³⁄₈ < ³⁄₄.
³⁄₄ is the greater fraction ✓

Equivalent Fractions

Equivalent fractions are different fractions that represent the same value. They look different but mean the same amount.

🟰 Key Idea

To find equivalent fractions, multiply or divide both numerator and denominator by the same number.

½ = ²⁄₄ = ⁴⁄₈ = ⁶⁄₁₂

Converting Fractions

Improper Fraction → Mixed Number

Divide the numerator by the denominator. The quotient is the whole number; the remainder becomes the new numerator.

🔄

Worked Example: Convert ⁹⁄₄ to a Mixed Number

Convert ⁹⁄₄ to a mixed number.
Identify
We have the improper fraction ⁹⁄₄. Since 9 > 4, it is improper.
Step 1 — Divide
Divide 9 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 1. The quotient (2) becomes the whole number.
Step 2 — Write Remainder
The remainder (1) becomes the new numerator. Keep the original denominator (4).
Answer
⁹⁄₄ = 2¼ ✓
Step 1 of 4

Mixed Number → Improper Fraction

STEPS: Mixed → Improper

1. Multiply whole number × denominator
2. Add the numerator to that result
3. Place result over original denominator

EXAMPLE: 1½
1 × 2 = 2 → 2 + 1 = 3 → ³⁄₂

EXAMPLE: 2²⁄₃
2 × 3 = 6 → 6 + 2 = 8 → ⁸⁄₃

Operations with Fractions

Adding Like Fractions

Rule

Same denominators → just add the numerators, keep the denominator the same.

EXAMPLE
2/7 + 3/7 = (2+3)/7 = 5/7

Adding Unlike Fractions

STEPS

1. Find LCM (Least Common Multiple) of denominators
2. Convert fractions to equivalent forms with LCM
3. Add the numerators; keep the common denominator
4. Simplify if possible

EXAMPLE: 3/4 + 2/5
LCM(4,5) = 20
3/4 = 15/20 (×5)
2/5 = 8/20 (×4)
15/20 + 8/20 = 23/20 = 1³/₂₀

Subtracting Fractions

Rule

Like fractions: Subtract numerators, keep denominator.

Unlike fractions: Find LCM first, convert, then subtract.

LIKE EXAMPLE: 5/8 − 3/8
(5−3)/8 = 2/8 = 1/4

UNLIKE EXAMPLE: 7/10 − 2/5
LCM(10,5) = 10
2/5 = 4/10 (×2)
7/10 − 4/10 = 3/10

Multiplying Fractions

Rule — Multiply straight across!

Multiply numerator × numerator and denominator × denominator.

EXAMPLE: 2/5 × 3/7
= (2×3) / (5×7)
= 6/35

Dividing Fractions

Rule — Keep, Change, Flip (KCF)

Keep the first fraction. Change ÷ to ×. Flip (reciprocal) the second fraction. Then multiply.

EXAMPLE: 1/2 ÷ 3/4
= 1/2 × 4/3 (flip 3/4 → 4/3)
= (1×4) / (2×3)
= 4/6 = 2/3
➕

Worked Example: 1/3 + 2/9

Calculate: ⅓ + ²⁄₉
Identify
Denominators are 3 and 9 — they are different, so these are unlike fractions.
Step 1 — Find LCM
LCM of 3 and 9 = 9 (since 9 is a multiple of 3).
Step 2 — Convert
⅓ = ³⁄₉ (multiply numerator and denominator by 3)
²⁄₉ stays as ²⁄₉
Step 3 — Add
³⁄₉ + ²⁄₉ = ⁵⁄₉
Answer
⅓ + ²⁄₉ = ⁵⁄₉ ✓
