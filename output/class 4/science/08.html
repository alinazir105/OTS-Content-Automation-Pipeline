What is a Mixture?

In our daily lives, we often mix things together, like sugar in water or fruits in a salad. But have you ever wondered what happens when things are mixed? A mixture is formed when two or more things are combined without changing their basic properties.

🧪 Definition: Mixture

A mixture is formed when two or more substances are combined together. Each substance keeps its own properties and can still be separated. For example, when you mix rice and beans, you can still see and separate each part.

The key idea is that no new substance is created — the original materials remain unchanged. They are simply placed together.

Examples of Mixtures in Daily Life

  • 🏖️ Sand and water — sand doesn't dissolve; it settles at the bottom
  • 🥣 Cereal and milk — you can see both components clearly
  • 🪙 Coins in a jar — different coins mixed together
  • 🍚 Rice and beans — both can be picked out separately

Types of Mixtures

Mixtures are grouped into two main types based on how they look and whether their parts are visible.

🔵

Homogeneous Mixture

Looks the same throughout. You cannot see separate parts. The components are evenly distributed.

Heterogeneous Mixture

What is a Solution?

A solution is a special type of mixture where one substance completely dissolves in another. The substances in a solution cannot be separated easily by looking at them.

🍹 Definition: Solution

A solution is a homogeneous mixture formed when one substance (the solute) dissolves completely in another substance (the solvent). The result looks uniform with no visible separate parts.

Parts of a Solution

💧

Solvent

The substance in which another substance dissolves. It is usually present in a larger amount.

Example: Water

🧂

Solute

The substance that dissolves in the solvent. It disappears into the solvent and is usually in a smaller amount.

Example: Salt or Sugar

💡 Remember:

When salt dissolves in water → Salt = Solute, Water = Solvent. Together they form a Saltwater Solution.

Examples of Solutions

  • 🧂 Saltwater — salt (solute) dissolved in water (solvent)
  • 🍵 Sugar dissolved in tea — sugar (solute) in tea (solvent)
  • 🍋 Lemonade — lemon juice and sugar dissolved in water
  • 💊 Syrup medicine — medicine dissolved in liquid

🔍 Worked Example: Identifying Solute & Solvent

Let's work through how to identify the solute and solvent in lemonade.

Step 1: What are the ingredients in lemonade? → Lemon juice, sugar, and water.
Step 2: Which substance is present in the most? → Water is the main liquid.
Step 3: Which substances dissolve into the water? → Sugar and lemon juice dissolve.
Step 4 (Answer): 💧 Solvent = Water  |  🍋 Solute = Sugar + Lemon juice

How to Separate Mixtures

We can separate mixtures in different ways depending on their type and the size of their parts.

1

Handpicking

Used to separate large, visible pieces by hand. The simplest method.

e.g. Stones from rice
2

Filtration

Used to separate solids from liquids using a filter paper or strainer.

e.g. Sand from water
3

Sieving

Used to separate particles of different sizes using a mesh or sieve.

e.g. Flour and sugar

// HOW FILTRATION WORKS — Step by Step
Step 1:
Pour the mixture (e.g., muddy water) into a funnel lined with filter paper.
// The filter paper has tiny holes — small enough to stop solid particles
Step 2:
The liquid passes through the filter paper into the beaker below.
// This liquid is called the filtrate (e.g., clean water)
Step 3:
The solid stays on top of the filter paper.
// This solid is called the residue (e.g., sand, mud)
Result:
Solid and liquid are now separated!

Importance of Mixtures & Solutions

Mixtures and solutions are very useful in our daily lives across many activities:

🍳

Cooking

Mixing ingredients to prepare food — like flour, water and salt to make dough, or making curries with spices dissolved in oil and water.

💊

Medicine

Solutions like syrups and liquid medicines help us when we are sick. The medicine (solute) is dissolved in a sweet liquid (solvent) for easy intake.

🧼

Cleaning

Soap and water make a solution that helps clean dirt from surfaces, clothes and our hands. Detergents also form solutions with water.

Activities

Hands-on activities to explore mixtures and solutions.

Activity 1: Identify Mixtures Around You

Look around your kitchen or home and find five mixtures.

  1. Look for items that are combined but can still be separated.
  2. Write down each mixture and what it contains.
  3. Classify each as homogeneous or heterogeneous.

Examples to look for: salt in water, sugar in tea, rice in a pot, coins in a bowl, fruits in a bowl.

Activity 2: Making a Solution

  1. Take a glass of water.
  2. Add a teaspoon of sugar and stir.
  3. Observe how the sugar dissolves completely — the water becomes sweet!
  4. Now try adding sand to another glass of water. Stir it. What do you notice?
  5. Observe the difference — sugar dissolves, sand does not.

Expected Observation:

Sugar dissolves completely in water forming a clear solution. Sand does NOT dissolve — it sinks to the bottom. This shows the difference between a solution and a simple mixture.

Activity 3: Separating a Mixture

  1. Mix sand and water in a bowl.
  2. Set up a funnel with filter paper over a beaker.
  3. Slowly pour the sand-water mixture through the filter.
  4. Observe: sand stays on the filter paper, water passes through.
  5. Write down what you observe.

Activity 4: Separating with a Sieve

  1. Mix small stones and flour in a bowl.
  2. Use a sieve to separate them — hold the sieve over a bowl and pour the mixture in.
  3. Shake the sieve gently. The flour falls through; stones remain in the sieve.
  4. Write down what you observe after sieving.

📌 Key Terms

  • Mixture — Two or more substances combined without changing their properties
  • Solution — A mixture where one substance dissolves in another
  • Solute — The substance that dissolves (e.g. salt, sugar)
  • Solvent — The substance in which dissolving happens (e.g. water)
  • Filtration — Separating solids from liquids using a filter
  • Sieving — Separating particles of different sizes
  • Handpicking — Separating large visible pieces by hand

🔑 Quick Facts

  • All solutions are mixtures, but NOT all mixtures are solutions
  • In a solution, the solute disappears into the solvent
  • Homogeneous = same throughout
  • Heterogeneous = visible different parts
  • Water is the most common solvent
  • A salad is a heterogeneous mixture

💡 Remember!

Solute = the substance that dissolves

Solvent = the substance that does the dissolving

Think: SOlvent = Substance Outside (that surrounds)

📋 Separation Methods

  • Handpicking — Large, visible pieces (stones from rice)
  • Filtration — Solid from liquid (sand from water)
  • Sieving — Different particle sizes (flour and stones)

🌟 Real-Life Uses

  • 🍳 Cooking uses mixtures every day
  • 💊 Medicine syrups are solutions
  • 🧼 Soap + water = cleaning solution
  • 🏊 Pool water has chemicals dissolved in it
  • 🩸 Blood is a complex mixture in our bodies
