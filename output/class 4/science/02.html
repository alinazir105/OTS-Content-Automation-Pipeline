Getting Started

What is a Life Cycle?

Every living thing goes through stages as it grows. This journey from birth to adulthood is called a life cycle. In this unit, we will explore the life cycles of a butterfly and a frog, which go through big changes called metamorphosis.

📖 Key Definition: Life Cycle
A life cycle is the series of changes a living organism goes through from the beginning of its life until it produces offspring and the cycle begins again.
🔄 Key Definition: Metamorphosis
Metamorphosis is a dramatic change in body form and structure that occurs during the development of some animals, such as butterflies and frogs.

Stage by Stage

Life Cycle of a Butterfly 🦋

Butterflies are colourful insects that go through four stages during their life cycle. Each stage looks completely different from the others — this is what makes metamorphosis so amazing!

01
🥚
Egg Stage
Life begins as a tiny egg laid on leaves. These eggs are small and can be round, oval, or cylindrical. The mother butterfly chooses leaves carefully so the hatched caterpillar will have food nearby.
02
🐛
Larva Stage (Caterpillar)
The egg hatches into a caterpillar, which eats leaves to grow. As it grows, the caterpillar sheds its skin multiple times. This stage is all about eating and growing.
03
🫘
Pupa Stage (Chrysalis)
The caterpillar wraps itself in a protective shell called a chrysalis. Inside the chrysalis, the caterpillar transforms into a butterfly through an incredible process.
04
🦋
Adult Stage (Butterfly)
A butterfly emerges, dries its wings, and flies away. It finds flowers for nectar and lays eggs to start the cycle again. Adults help pollinate flowers!

🔬 Follow the Butterfly Journey — Step by Step

Click "Show Next Step" to follow a butterfly through its complete life cycle.

📍 Starting Point
A female butterfly lands on a leaf and lays tiny eggs. She chooses leaves that will feed her young when they hatch. The eggs stick to the leaf with a special glue-like substance.
🐛 Step 1 → Egg hatches into Larva (Caterpillar)
After a few days, the egg hatches. A tiny caterpillar emerges. Its main job is to eat! It munches on leaves day and night, growing bigger. As it grows, it sheds its skin (called moulting) several times.
🫘 Step 2 → Caterpillar becomes Pupa (Chrysalis)
When the caterpillar is fully grown, it forms a hard protective shell around itself called a chrysalis. Inside, an amazing transformation takes place. The body breaks down and rebuilds itself completely into a butterfly form.
🦋 Step 3 → Adult Butterfly Emerges
The adult butterfly breaks out of the chrysalis. It pumps fluid into its wings to expand them, then waits for them to dry. Once ready, it flies away to find flowers, drink nectar, find a mate, and lay eggs — starting the cycle again!

🔄 Chrysalis vs Cocoon

Students often confuse these!

  • Chrysalis = butterfly pupa (hard shell)
  • Cocoon = moth pupa (silk wrapping)
  • Both protect the transforming insect

Aquatic Beginnings

Life Cycle of a Frog 🐸

Frogs are amphibians, meaning they live in water AND on land. Their life cycle also has four stages and involves a remarkable transformation from a water-breathing tadpole into an air-breathing adult frog.

🐸 What is an Amphibian?
Amphibians are animals that can live both in water and on land. They usually begin life in water and move to land as adults. Examples include frogs, toads, and salamanders.
01
🫧
Egg Stage (Frogspawn)
Frogs lay eggs in clusters called frogspawn in water. Frogspawn looks like tiny jelly bubbles with dark spots inside. Each spot is a developing frog embryo.
02
🐟
Tadpole Stage
The eggs hatch into tadpoles that swim in water, breathe through gills, and eat plants. Tadpoles look like tiny fish and spend all their time in water.
03
🦎
Tadpole with Legs (Froglet)
Tadpoles grow legs and lose their tails as they develop. At this stage, they begin to live on both land and water. They are now called froglets.
04
🐸
Adult Frog
The tadpole fully transforms into an adult frog with lungs for breathing air and strong legs for jumping. Adults return to water to lay eggs and restart the cycle.

🔬 Follow the Frog Journey — Step by Step

Click "Show Next Step" to follow a frog through its complete life cycle.

📍 Starting Point
An adult frog returns to a pond or lake and lays hundreds of eggs in clusters called frogspawn. The eggs are surrounded by a jelly-like coating that protects them and helps them float.
🐟 Step 1 → Frogspawn hatches into Tadpole
After about a week, tiny tadpoles hatch from the eggs. They look like small fish with tails. They breathe through gills (like fish), swim in water, and eat tiny plants called algae.
🦎 Step 2 → Tadpole grows into Froglet
Over several weeks, the tadpole begins to change. It grows back legs first, then front legs. Its tail gets shorter and shorter. Its gills disappear and lungs develop. This stage is called a froglet.
🐸 Step 3 → Froglet becomes Adult Frog
The froglet's tail is fully absorbed and it becomes an adult frog. It now breathes air with its lungs, has four strong legs for jumping, and can live on land. When it is ready to breed, it returns to water to lay eggs — starting the cycle again!

🧠 Remember This!

The key difference: Butterfly young (caterpillar) lives on land, while frog young (tadpole) lives in water.

Also: butterflies have a special chrysalis (pupa) stage — frogs do not!

Similarities: Both start from eggs, both have 4 stages, both undergo metamorphosis.

Differences: Butterfly eggs on leaves (land), frog eggs in water. Caterpillar crawls, tadpole swims. Butterflies have a chrysalis stage.

Q1: What are the four stages of a butterfly's life cycle?
The four stages of a butterfly's life cycle are: 1) Egg — laid on leaves; 2) Larva (Caterpillar) — hatches from egg, eats leaves, sheds skin; 3) Pupa (Chrysalis) — caterpillar wraps in chrysalis and transforms; 4) Adult Butterfly — emerges, flies, lays eggs to start the cycle again.
Q2: How does a tadpole survive in water?
A tadpole survives in water by breathing through gills (like fish), which extract oxygen from water. It also has a tail for swimming, and eats aquatic plants (algae) for food. Its body is adapted for life entirely in water at this stage.
Q3: What is the difference between frogspawn and butterfly eggs?
Frogspawn is a cluster of frog eggs laid in water, surrounded by a jelly-like coating that looks like transparent bubbles with dark spots. Butterfly eggs are tiny individual eggs laid on leaves (on land), and can be round, oval, or cylindrical in shape. The key differences are: location (water vs land) and appearance (jelly clusters vs small individual eggs).
Q4: What happens inside a chrysalis?
Inside a chrysalis, the caterpillar undergoes a complete transformation (metamorphosis). The caterpillar's body breaks down into cells and completely rebuilds itself into the form of a butterfly. Wings, antennae, and other adult features develop during this stage. The chrysalis acts as a protective shell during this remarkable change.
Q5: Why do adult frogs need strong legs?
Adult frogs need strong legs for several important reasons: to jump away from predators quickly, to catch prey (insects), to move on land between feeding areas, and to swim in water (particularly when returning to water to lay eggs). Frogs can jump up to 20 times their body length using their powerful hind legs.
Q6: Compare the movements of a caterpillar and a tadpole.
A caterpillar crawls on land — it moves slowly across leaves and branches using its many legs (true legs and prolegs), staying in its land habitat to eat plants. A tadpole swims in water — it moves by wiggling its tail from side to side, like a fish, and lives entirely in the aquatic environment. So the caterpillar moves on land by crawling, while the tadpole moves in water by swimming.
