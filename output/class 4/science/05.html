Foundation

What Are Rocks?

Rocks are the solid materials that make up the Earth's crust. They are found everywhere on Earth — in mountains, valleys, and even underwater. Rocks are made of minerals, and their properties depend on the type and arrangement of these minerals.

Rocks are divided into three main types based on how they are formed:

🪨 Three Main Rock Types

1. Igneous Rocks — formed from cooled magma or lava
2. Sedimentary Rocks — formed from compressed particles and organic material
3. Metamorphic Rocks — formed when existing rocks are changed by heat and pressure

📚 Key Terms

  • Rock — solid material of Earth's crust made of minerals
  • Mineral — natural, inorganic solid with a specific chemical structure
  • Magma — molten rock below Earth's surface
  • Lava — molten rock that reaches Earth's surface
  • Fossil — preserved remains of ancient organisms
  • Sediment — tiny particles of rock, sand, or organic matter
  • Crystal — solid with atoms arranged in a regular repeating pattern

💡 Quick Reference

Igneous: Magma/lava → cools → rock

Sedimentary: Particles → compress → rock

Metamorphic: Rock + heat & pressure → new rock

Rock Type 1

🔥 Igneous Rocks

Igneous rocks form when molten rock (magma or lava) cools and solidifies. The word "igneous" comes from the Latin word for fire. There are two main types:

🏔️ Intrusive Igneous Rocks

Formed when magma cools slowly beneath the Earth's surface, creating large crystals.

Example: Granite

🌋 Extrusive Igneous Rocks

Formed when lava cools quickly on the surface, creating small crystals or smooth texture.

Example: Basalt

🔑 Key Difference

Speed of cooling determines crystal size! Slow cooling underground = BIG crystals (granite). Fast cooling on surface = tiny or no crystals (basalt).

HOW IGNEOUS ROCKS FORM — Step by Step
1
Deep inside Earth, extreme heat melts rock into magma.
2
Magma rises through cracks — stays underground (intrusive) OR erupts as lava (extrusive).
3
The magma/lava cools and solidifies into igneous rock.
4
Slow cooling → large crystals → Granite. Fast cooling → fine crystals → Basalt.

🔥 Igneous Facts

  • About 90% of Earth's crust is made of igneous rock
  • Granite is used in kitchen countertops and buildings
  • Basalt forms much of the ocean floor
  • Obsidian is a glassy extrusive rock with no crystals
  • Pumice is so light it can float on water!
🌋

Fun Fact!

When lava cools very quickly underwater, it forms pillow-shaped blobs called "pillow basalt."

Rock Type 2

📦 Sedimentary Rocks

Sedimentary rocks are formed from small pieces of other rocks, sand, and organic material that are compressed and cemented over time. They often have layers and may contain fossils.

Examples: Sandstone, Limestone

HOW SEDIMENTARY ROCKS FORM
1
Weathering & Erosion — Wind, water, and ice break rocks into tiny pieces called sediment.
2
Transportation — Rivers and wind carry sediment to new locations (often to lakes or oceans).
3
Deposition — Sediment settles in layers at the bottom of water bodies.
4
Compaction & Cementation — New layers press down, squeezing out water. Minerals glue particles together forming rock.

🦕 Why Do Sedimentary Rocks Contain Fossils?

When organisms die, they can be buried by sediment before they decay. Over millions of years, as sediment compresses into rock, the organism's remains are preserved as a fossil. This is why fossils are mostly found in sedimentary rocks.

🏜️

Sandstone

Made of sand-sized grains cemented together. Often reddish or yellow. Used in building construction.

Type: Sedimentary | Grains: Sand-sized
🐚

Limestone

Formed mainly from the remains of marine organisms like shells and corals. Often contains fossils.

Type: Sedimentary | Source: Marine organisms

📦 Sedimentary Facts

  • About 75% of rocks on Earth's surface are sedimentary
  • Sedimentary layers are called "strata"
  • Coal is a sedimentary rock made of ancient plant material
  • Chalk is a type of limestone made from tiny shell fragments
  • The Grand Canyon exposes millions of years of sedimentary layers
🦕

Fossil Fun!

Dinosaur fossils are found in sedimentary rock because they were buried by sediment after they died!

Rock Type 3

💎 Metamorphic Rocks

Metamorphic rocks are formed when existing rocks are changed by heat and pressure deep within the Earth. The word "metamorphic" means "changed form." These rocks often have a banded or crystalline texture.

Examples: Marble, Slate

🔄 Transformation

Any rock type can become metamorphic! When limestone is subjected to intense heat and pressure, it transforms into marble. When mudstone is transformed, it becomes slate.

HOW METAMORPHIC ROCKS FORM
1
An existing rock (igneous or sedimentary) is buried deep underground.
2
The rock is subjected to extreme heat and pressure — but does NOT melt.
3
Minerals in the rock recrystallize into new arrangements.
4
A new metamorphic rock with banded or crystalline texture is formed.
🏛️

Marble

Formed from limestone under heat and pressure. Smooth, often white or coloured. Used for statues and buildings.

Parent rock: Limestone | Used in: sculptures, floors
🪨

Slate

Formed from mudstone. Dark grey, splits into flat sheets. Used for roofing tiles and flooring.

Parent rock: Mudstone | Used in: roofing tiles
🏛️

Fun Fact!

Marble, a metamorphic rock, is often used in making statues and buildings. Famous statues like Michelangelo's David are made of marble!

💎 Metamorphic Facts

  • Metamorphic means "changed form" in Greek
  • Diamonds form deep in Earth under extreme pressure
  • Marble is formed from limestone
  • Slate was once muddy sediment
  • Quartzite forms when sandstone is metamorphosed

🔁 Rock Transformations

  • Limestone → Marble
  • Mudstone → Slate
  • Sandstone → Quartzite
  • Granite → Gneiss

The Big Picture

🔄 The Rock Cycle

Rocks constantly change from one type to another in a process called the rock cycle. This is a continuous process that has been happening for billions of years.

🔄 The Rock Cycle — How It Works

✅ Igneous rocks can break down through weathering to form sedimentary rocks.
✅ Both igneous and sedimentary rocks can be transformed by heat and pressure into metamorphic rocks.
✅ Metamorphic rocks can melt to form magma, which cools to form new igneous rocks — starting the cycle again!

ROCK CYCLE — KEY PROCESSES
1
Melting — Any rock can melt deep underground to form magma.
2
Cooling & Solidification — Magma/lava cools to form igneous rock.
3
Weathering & Erosion — Rocks break into sediment.
4
Compaction & Cementation — Sediment layers become sedimentary rock.
5
Heat & Pressure — Rocks transform into metamorphic rock.

🧠 Critical Thinking

If the rock cycle stopped, how would it affect the formation of new rocks and landscapes?

Think about: Without weathering and erosion, mountains could never form new sediment. Without melting, volcanic activity would stop. Without heat and pressure, no metamorphic rocks would form. Earth's surface would slowly stop changing, and soils formed from weathered rock would eventually run out!

⏱️ Rock Cycle Timeline

The rock cycle takes millions of years to complete one full cycle, but all processes are happening simultaneously around the world!

  • Sediment forms over thousands of years
  • Sedimentary rock forms over millions of years
  • Metamorphic rock forms deep underground over millions of years
  • Igneous rock can form in days from lava!

💬 Answer These Questions

1
What are the three types of rocks, and how are they formed?
The three types of rocks are: (1) Igneous rocks — formed when magma or lava cools and solidifies; (2) Sedimentary rocks — formed when small pieces of rock, sand, and organic material are compressed and cemented together over time; (3) Metamorphic rocks — formed when existing rocks are changed by heat and pressure deep within the Earth.
2
How are intrusive and extrusive igneous rocks different?
Intrusive igneous rocks form when magma cools slowly beneath the Earth's surface, which creates large crystals (example: granite). Extrusive igneous rocks form when lava cools quickly on the Earth's surface, creating small crystals or a smooth texture (example: basalt). The main difference is the speed of cooling and where the rock forms.
3
Describe the process of the rock cycle.
The rock cycle is a continuous process where rocks change from one type to another. Igneous rocks can break down through weathering and erosion to form sediment, which compresses into sedimentary rocks. Both igneous and sedimentary rocks can be transformed by heat and pressure deep underground into metamorphic rocks. Metamorphic rocks can melt to form magma, which cools to become igneous rock again — and the cycle continues!
4
Define rocks.
Rocks are the solid materials that make up the Earth's crust. They are found everywhere on Earth — in mountains, valleys, and even underwater. Rocks are made of minerals, and their properties depend on the type and arrangement of these minerals.
5
Why are fossils mostly found in sedimentary rocks?
Fossils are mostly found in sedimentary rocks because when organisms die, they can be quickly covered by layers of sediment (sand, mud, etc.) before they fully decay. Over millions of years, as these sediment layers are compressed into rock, the remains of the organism are preserved within the rock as fossils. The gentle process of compaction preserves the shape without destroying the remains.
