What Is Soil Made Of?

Components of Soil

Soil is one of the most important natural resources on Earth. It provides nutrients to plants, supports animal life, and helps store water. Soil forms over thousands of years through the breakdown of rocks (weathering) and the decomposition of organic matter like dead plants and animals.

🔬 Key Definition

Weathering is the process by which rocks break down into smaller particles over time due to wind, water, and temperature changes.

Soil is made up of four main components, each playing a crucial role:

🪨

Minerals

Tiny rock particles that come from the breakdown of larger rocks. They provide structure to soil.

🍂

Organic Matter

Decayed plants and animals (called humus) that add essential nutrients to the soil.

💧

Water & Air

Fill the spaces between soil particles — water delivers nutrients; air provides oxygen for roots.

🪱

Living Organisms

Earthworms, bacteria, and insects that break down organic matter and improve soil quality.

🌍 Did You Know?

A single teaspoon of healthy soil contains more microorganisms than there are people on Earth!

📖 Key Terms

WeatheringBreakdown of rocks into small particles by nature.
HumusDecayed organic matter that enriches soil.
TopsoilThe uppermost, nutrient-rich soil layer.
SubsoilLayer below topsoil; has minerals, less organic matter.
BedrockSolid rock layer at the bottom of the soil profile.
ErosionWashing or blowing away of topsoil by water or wind.
LoamyBest agricultural soil; mix of sand, silt, and clay.
FertileRich in nutrients; able to support plant growth.

⚡ Quick Facts

🌱Soil forms at a rate of about 1 cm per 1,000 years.
🌱95% of our food comes from soil.
🌱Earthworms help aerate soil by burrowing through it.
🌱Loamy soil is ideal for most crops due to balanced drainage and nutrients.
🌱Sandy soil is found in deserts and coastal areas.
🌱Clay soil can be used to make pottery and bricks.

Comparing Soils

Types of Soil

Different types of soil have different properties. The three main types are sandy, clayey, and loamy soil. Each type behaves differently with water and nutrients.

🏖️ Sandy Soil

  • Made of large particles
  • Drains water very quickly
  • Poor in nutrients, making it less fertile
  • Found in deserts and coastal areas
  • Feels rough and gritty when touched

🧱 Clayey Soil

  • Made of tiny particles that stick together
  • Holds water very well, but is dense and heavy
  • Fertile but hard to plow
  • Becomes sticky when wet, hard when dry
  • Used for making pottery and bricks

🌾 Loamy Soil

  • A mix of sand, silt, and clay
  • Drains water well but retains enough moisture
  • Rich in nutrients — ideal for agriculture
  • Dark and crumbly in texture
  • Best soil for growing most crops

Organic Layer

Surface layer of decomposed leaves and plant matter. Provides nutrients as it breaks down.

🪱

Topsoil

The topmost mineral layer, rich in organic matter and nutrients. This is where most plants have their roots.

⛏️

Subsoil

Below the topsoil, contains minerals like iron and clay. Has fewer organic materials than the topsoil.

🗿

Bedrock

The bottom layer, made of solid rock. It eventually breaks down over thousands of years to form soil above.

Why Soil Matters

Importance of Soil

Soil plays a vital role in supporting life on Earth. Without soil, most plants couldn't grow, animals wouldn't have food or shelter, and water quality would suffer.

🌿

Supports Plant Life

Provides nutrients, water, and a base for roots to anchor and grow strong plants.

🐛

Habitat for Animals

Home to many insects, worms, moles, and small animals that live in or near the soil.

💧

Filters Water

Removes impurities and pollutants as water passes through the soil layers to reach groundwater.

🛡️

Prevents Erosion

Plant roots in soil hold the land together and stop it from being washed or blown away.

🌾

Supports Agriculture

About 95% of our food supply depends directly or indirectly on soil as a growing medium.

♻️

Nutrient Cycling

Soil organisms break down dead matter and recycle nutrients back into the ecosystem.
