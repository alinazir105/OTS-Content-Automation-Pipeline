How Do We Classify Animals?

Grouping animals by their similarities

Classify means to group animals or things that are alike. Scientists classify animals into groups — some have backbones (vertebrates) and some do not (invertebrates).

Did You Know? Your backbone goes from your hips all the way up to your head! Touch the back of your neck — those bumps are part of your backbone (spine).

Animals With Backbones

Vertebrates: 5 main groups

🐘

1. Mammals

  • Warm-blooded animals
  • Have hair or fur
  • Give birth to live young
  • Feed milk to their babies
  • Breathe through lungs
Examples: Humans, cats, dogs, cows, lions, dolphins, whales
Whales and dolphins are mammals even though they live in water!
🦎

2. Reptiles

  • Cold-blooded animals
  • Have dry, scaly skin
  • Most lay eggs
  • Breathe through lungs
Examples: Snakes, crocodiles, turtles, lizards, alligators
Snakes can smell with their tongues!
🐸

3. Amphibians

  • Live on both land and water
  • Cold-blooded
  • Have moist skin
  • Lay eggs in water
  • Young ones swim like fish
Examples: Frogs, toads, newts, salamanders
Frogs say "ribbit!" while toads make a deep grunt sound!
🦜

4. Birds

  • Only animals with feathers
  • Warm-blooded
  • Have beaks and wings
  • Lay eggs
  • Not all birds can fly
Examples: Pigeons, eagles, parrots, penguins, ostriches
Penguins are birds that can't fly but are excellent swimmers!
🐟

5. Fish

  • Live in water
  • Cold-blooded
  • Have scales and fins
  • Breathe through gills
  • Lay eggs
Examples: Tuna, sharks, goldfish, trout, salmon
Fish don't have eyelids, so they can't blink!

📌 Key Definitions

🌡️Warm-blooded: Body temperature stays the same regardless of the environment
❄️Cold-blooded: Body temperature changes with the surroundings
🦴Vertebrate: Animal with a backbone (spine)
🦑Invertebrate: Animal without a backbone
🫧Gills: Organs fish use to breathe oxygen from water

Tip: Mammals = Milk + Hair + Warm blood + Live birth

Birds are the ONLY animals with feathers!

Animals Without Backbones

Invertebrates and how they stay safe

There are many kinds of animals that do not have backbones. Some have shells or hard body coverings that help keep them safe. Others have soft bodies and must use other ways to stay safe.
🦋

Dragonfly

Has a hard body covering. Uses its wings to fly away from enemies quickly.
Defense: Flying away 🪽
🦞

Lobster

Has a hard body covering (exoskeleton). Uses its claws to break open food and defend itself.
Defense: Hard shell + claws 🦀
🪲

Beetle

Has a hard shell. Has three body parts and six legs. The shell protects its soft body.
Defense: Hard outer shell 🛡️
🪼

Jellyfish

Does NOT have a shell. Has a soft body. Stings other animals to stay safe from predators.
Defense: Stinging cells ⚡

🔬 Guided Example: How do animals without backbones stay safe?

1

Identify if the animal has a shell

First, check: does the invertebrate have a hard shell or body covering? Examples: beetles, lobsters, crabs → YES they have hard coverings.

2

If it has a hard shell →

The shell acts as a natural armor! It protects the soft body inside from predators. Some animals like turtles can even pull their body inside their shell.

3

If it does NOT have a shell →

The animal must use other strategies: stinging (jellyfish), camouflage (octopus), venom (some spiders), or simply hiding.

4

Conclusion

All animals — with or without backbones — have evolved special features or behaviors to survive in their environment!

Life Cycles & Animals From Eggs

How animals grow and change

Why do animals lay many eggs? Animals lay many eggs because some of the young animals will not survive — many get eaten by other animals. Laying many eggs increases the chances of survival for the species.

Crab Fact: Female crabs can carry up to 2.5 million eggs on their abdomen throughout the winter season! At approximately four years of age, male crabs reach a size suitable for harvesting. Female crabs are released back into the sea to continue the lifecycle.

🐢 Sea Turtles: A Special Case

1

Female lays eggs

Female sea turtles come onto the beach and bury their eggs in the sand, then leave. They do NOT care for their young.

2

Eggs hatch

When the eggs hatch, the tiny baby turtles must find their own way to the ocean — completely alone!

3

Danger from predators

Many young turtles get eaten by other animals such as seagulls before they reach the water. This is why turtles lay many eggs!

4

Human help

Sometimes people protect baby sea turtles from predators, helping more of them survive and reach the ocean safely.

Who lays eggs? Birds 🦜, Reptiles 🦎, Fish 🐟, Insects 🦋, and many sea animals all lay eggs. A queen bee can lay more than 2,000 eggs in one day! Salmon lay thousands of eggs between rocks in rivers.

How Animals Look and Act

Adaptations for survival

All animals look and act in special ways that help them live. Sometimes their colour or body parts help them. Sometimes the things they do can help them.
🦒

Giraffe

Giraffes have long necks that help them eat leaves from tall trees that other animals cannot reach.
Adaptation: Long neck for reaching tall trees 🌳
🦚

Male Peacock

Male peacocks have bright, colorful feathers and sing loud songs that help females find them for mating.
Adaptation: Bright feathers to attract mates 💫

🛡️ How Do Animals Stay Safe?

Animals use many different strategies to stay safe: flying away, blending into their surroundings (camouflage), fighting, using hard shells, or having poisonous bites/stings.
🐢

Tortoise

Pulls its head and legs inside its hard shell for protection. The shell acts as a natural armor against predators.
Strategy: Hard shell defense 🛡️
🦅

Bird

Birds fly away when they sense danger. Their ability to fly quickly gives them a huge advantage over land predators.
Strategy: Flying away to escape 🪽
🐍

Snake

Some snakes have poisonous bites. They may also blend into their surroundings using camouflage to hide from predators.
Strategy: Venom + camouflage ☠️

Populations of Animals

What is a population and how can they differ?

A population is a group of the same kind of animal living near each other. For example, the black bears in Yosemite National Park are one population. The black bears in Maine are another population.

🐻 How Do Populations Differ?

Animals in different populations do not always look the same. In order to live safely in different places, animals can change in many ways.

🐻‍❄️

Black Bears in Maine

Black bears in Maine have black fur. This helps them blend into dark forests.

🐻

Black Bears in Yosemite

Don't be fooled by the name! These "black" bears actually have brown fur.

🐻‍❄️

Polar Bears in Alaska

Polar bears have light/white fur that helps them hide in the snow — perfect camouflage!

🦡 Meerkats: Same Population, Different Individuals

Animals in the same population may also look and act differently. Meerkats live in the African desert. Some meerkats are bigger than others. Some are more careful, others are more curious.
🦡

How Meerkats Differ

• Some are bigger than others
• Some are more careful
• Others are more curious

🦡

How Meerkats Are Alike

• All eat insects
• Live underground in burrows
• Have long claws for digging
• Live in large groups
