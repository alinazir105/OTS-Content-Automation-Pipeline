Introduction

What Is a Food Chain?

All living organisms depend on each other for food, shelter, and protection. The connection of "who eats whom" forms a food chain, showing how energy passes from one living thing to another.

🤝

Interdependence

Every living organism in the universe depends on others for food, shelter, and protection to survive.

⚡

Energy Flow

Food is the main source of energy. A food chain shows exactly how this energy moves from one organism to another.

☀️

The Sun Starts It All

Every food chain begins with the Sun — the ultimate source of energy for all living organisms in our universe.

🔄

Ecosystem Balance

Food chains help maintain balance in ecosystems by ensuring energy and nutrients cycle continuously through nature.

Discovery Zone: Food Chain Definition

A food chain refers to the order of events in an ecosystem, which shows how energy is transferred from one organism to another. It usually starts with the Sun, which provides energy for plants to grow. This energy then moves through different organisms, step by step.

Background

How Energy Is Transferred

Energy doesn't appear magically — it travels through a sequence of organisms, each depending on the one before it.

// Energy Transfer Pathway Sun → Producers (Plants) → Consumers (Animals) → Decomposers

Did You Know? 🤔

☀️

The Sun's Role

The Sun provides about 1,361 watts of energy per square metre to Earth. Plants capture only about 1% of this for photosynthesis!

🌿

Photosynthesis

Plants use sunlight, water (H₂O), and carbon dioxide (CO₂) to produce glucose — their own food. This process is called photosynthesis.

🍄

Decomposers

Without decomposers like bacteria and fungi, dead matter would pile up everywhere and plants would be starved of nutrients!

🔢

Energy Loss

At each level of a food chain, about 90% of energy is lost as heat. Only 10% passes on — that's why food chains rarely have more than 5 links!

Core Concepts

Producers, Consumers & Decomposers

Every organism in a food chain has a specific role. Let's explore each one in detail.

🌿

Producers

Plants are the producers of the ecosystem. They make their own food using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide through a process called photosynthesis. Organisms that can produce their own food are called producers.

// Photosynthesis Sunlight + Water + CO₂ → Glucose (Food) + Oxygen

Examples: Wheat 🌾, Grass 🌱, Mango Trees 🥭

🦁

Consumers

Consumers cannot make their own food. They depend on producers — eating plants or other animals for energy. There are three types of consumers:

Type ↕ What They Eat ↕ Examples ↕ Icon
Herbivores Plants only Goats, Cows, Rabbits 🐄
Carnivores Other animals only Lions, Tigers, Eagles 🦁
Omnivores Both plants & animals Humans, Bears, Crows 🐻

💡 Click a row to learn more!

🍄

Decomposers

Decomposers are tiny but mighty organisms like bacteria and fungi. They break down dead plants and animals into nutrients, which enrich the soil.

Why Decomposers Are Critical

Without decomposers, dead matter would pile up and plants would not get the nutrients they need to grow. Decomposers are nature's recyclers — they close the loop of the food chain!

Examples: Bacteria 🦠, Fungi (Bracket fungus / Shelf fungus) 🍄, Earthworms 🪱

Producers: They make their own food. Always plants. They appear right after the Sun in the chain.
Primary consumers: Eat producers (plants). Always herbivores.
Secondary consumers: Eat primary consumers. Usually carnivores or omnivores.
Tertiary consumers: At the top of the chain. Rarely get eaten themselves.
Decomposers: Break down dead matter from ALL levels. They don't appear in the middle of a chain — they work on everything!
