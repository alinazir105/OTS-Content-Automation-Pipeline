Introduction

What is a Life Cycle?

Every animal goes through a series of changes from birth to death. These stages together form the life cycle.

What Makes a Life Cycle?

Birth, growth, reproduction and death represent the four stages of the life cycles of all animals. In this unit, we explore the life cycles of a hen and a fish. These cycles show how animals grow, change, and reproduce to create the next generation.

🥚

Birth

Every life cycle begins with the birth or hatching of a new organism from an egg or the womb.

📈

Growth

The young organism grows bigger and stronger, developing the features it needs to survive.

❤️

Reproduction

Adult animals reproduce to create new offspring, ensuring the species continues to exist.

🍂

Death

Eventually all animals die, but their offspring carry on the life cycle for the next generation.

Life Cycle Formula
Birth → Growth → Reproduction → Death
This cycle repeats for every generation of animals 🔄

Life Cycle of a Hen

Hens are birds that live up to 6–7 years in favourable conditions. Their life cycle begins with an egg.

1. Egg Stage

Hens lay eggs. The egg contains a developing chick inside and is protected by a hard shell.

🔬

2. Embryo Development

When a hen sits on the egg to keep it warm (incubation), the embryo inside starts developing. This process takes about 21 days.

🐣

3. Hatchling

After 21 days, the chick hatches out of the egg. It is small, fluffy, and depends on the hen for warmth and protection.

🐥

4. Chick

The hatchling grows into a chick. Chicks start to grow feathers and learn to find food on their own.

🐔

5. Adult Hen

Over time, the chick becomes an adult hen or rooster, capable of laying eggs or reproducing to continue the cycle.

🐟 Fish

Life Cycle of a Fish

Fish are aquatic animals whose life cycle begins with eggs laid in water. Each stage prepares the fish for survival.

🫧

1. Egg Stage

Female fish lay soft, jelly-like eggs in water. These eggs are sticky and form "clutches". Male fish then fertilize the eggs in the water.

🦠

2. Larva Stage

When eggs hatch, tiny larvae emerge. Each larva has a yolk sac that provides nutrition until it can find food on its own.

🐠

3. Fry Stage

As larvae grow, they develop fins and start swimming. At this stage they are called fry.

🐟

4. Juvenile Stage

The fry grows bigger and begins to look like an adult fish. They learn to hunt for food and avoid predators.

🐡

5. Adult Fish

The juvenile becomes an adult fish capable of reproducing. The cycle begins again when adult fish lay eggs.

Hatching Times Vary!

Fish hatching time varies by species:

  • Goldfish eggs hatch after about 2–4 days
  • Nemo (Clownfish) eggs hatch in about 6–10 days
The yolk sac is a small, nutrient-rich pouch attached to the fish larva. It acts like a built-in lunchbox! When the larva first hatches, it cannot yet hunt for food, so it absorbs nutrients directly from the yolk sac. Once the yolk sac is completely absorbed, the fry must find its own food to survive.
Comparison

Hen vs Fish Life Cycle

Both hen and fish start life as eggs, but there are many fascinating differences in how they develop. Click a row to see more details!

Stage 🐔 Hen 🐟 Fish
Starting Stage Egg with a hard shell Jelly-like eggs in water
More Info: A hen's egg has a hard calcium shell that protects the embryo from physical damage. A fish egg is soft and jelly-like, relying on water for support and temperature regulation.
Development Inside the egg (incubation by hen) In water, fertilized by male
More Info: In hens, the embryo develops inside the egg while the hen keeps it warm through incubation. In fish, the female lays unfertilized eggs in water, and then the male swims over them to fertilize them externally.
Hatching Time About 21 days A few days to weeks
More Info: A hen's egg always takes about 21 days to hatch. Fish hatching times vary by species — goldfish hatch in 2–4 days while clownfish eggs take 6–10 days.
Young Stage Chick Fry
More Info: A young hen is called a chick — it is fluffy, warm-blooded, and needs care from the mother. A young fish (fry) lives independently in water and must find its own food once the yolk sac is absorbed.
Adult Stage Hen or Rooster Adult Fish
More Info: An adult hen (female) or rooster (male) lives on land, breathes through lungs, and cares for its eggs. An adult fish lives in water, breathes through gills, and reproduces by laying eggs in water.
Habitat Land (terrestrial) Water (aquatic)
More Info: Hens are terrestrial birds that breathe through lungs. Fish are aquatic animals that live entirely in water and breathe through gills. This fundamental difference affects every stage of their life cycle.

🔍 Critical Thinking: What factors affect embryo development?

Consider these factors that can affect how an embryo develops inside an egg:

🌡️

Temperature

Correct temperature is vital. Too hot or too cold can harm the embryo.

💧

Moisture / Humidity

Adequate moisture prevents the egg from drying out during development.

🌀

Turning / Movement

Eggs need to be gently turned to prevent membranes sticking together.

🌊

Water Quality (Fish)

For fish eggs, clean water with proper oxygen levels is essential for development.
