Background

What are Plants?

A plant is a living thing that grows on Earth. Plants are essential to life, providing food, oxygen, fibre, medicine, fuel, and shelter for humans and animals.

🌞

Light to Food

Plants turn sunlight from the Sun into food required for their growth through the amazing process of photosynthesis.

💧

Water & Nutrients

Plants need water and nutrients from the soil, plus carbon dioxide that they absorb from the air around them.

🐄

Food Chain Base

Animals can eat plants to use the food plants created for their own growth — plants are the foundation of all food chains.

Basic Parts of a Plant

# Part Primary Function Click for Details
1 🌿 Roots Absorb water and nutrients from the soil ▼
Roots anchor the plant in the soil and act like straws, drawing up water and dissolved minerals. These are transported upward through the stem to the rest of the plant. Without roots, photosynthesis cannot happen — there would be no water supply!
2 🌿 Stem Supports the plant and transports water ▼
The stem acts as the plant's highway. It contains tiny tube-like structures (xylem and phloem) that carry water up to the leaves and transport food made in the leaves to the rest of the plant.
3 🍃 Leaves Make food through photosynthesis ▼
Leaves are the food factories of the plant! They are flat and wide to capture maximum sunlight. Inside leaves, tiny structures called chloroplasts contain chlorophyll — the green pigment that powers photosynthesis.
4 🌸 Flowers Produce seeds and fruits ▼
Flowers are the reproductive organs of a plant. After pollination, flowers develop into fruits that contain seeds, enabling the plant to reproduce and spread to new locations.
5 🍎 Fruits Contain seeds and help spread them ▼
Fruits develop from flowers and protect the seeds inside. When animals eat fruits, they may carry seeds far away, helping the plant species spread to new areas.

Do You Know? 🔍

Leaves of plants are called "food factories" because when they get hungry, they prepare food through photosynthesis using sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water — right inside the leaf cells!

Core Concept

What is Photosynthesis?

The word "photosynthesis" comes from two words: "photo" (light) and "synthesis" (to make). So photosynthesis literally means making with light!

⚗️ The Photosynthesis Equation
Sunlight + Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) + Water (H₂O)
──────────────────────────────────────────
→ Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) + Oxygen (O₂)

Step by Step

Steps of Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis happens in a series of carefully ordered steps, each building on the last.

  1. 1
    Plants absorb water from the soil through their roots, which travel up the stem to the leaves.
  2. 2
    Carbon dioxide (CO₂) enters the leaves through tiny pores called stomata.
  3. 3
    Sunlight provides the energy needed to power the photosynthesis reaction inside the leaf.
  4. 4
    Chlorophyll in the leaves uses sunlight to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose (a type of sugar) and oxygen.
  5. 5
    The glucose is used as food by the plant, and oxygen is released into the air through the stomata.
  6. 6
    Any unused glucose is stored in the plant in the form of starch for later use.

Why It Matters

Why is Photosynthesis Important?

Photosynthesis is one of the most critical processes on Earth. Here's why every living being depends on it:

01

Produces Oxygen: It produces the oxygen we breathe, which is essential for all animal and human life on Earth.

02

Base of Food Chain: Plants are primary producers — they form the foundation of every food chain on the planet.

03

Reduces CO₂: Plants absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, helping to reduce greenhouse gases and fight climate change.

04

Provides Energy: The glucose made during photosynthesis provides energy for the survival of all living organisms — directly or indirectly.

Did You Know? 💡 Click to Find Out!

📅

Plants know their season...

Click to reveal

Plants know their season — but they don't use a calendar! They detect changes in day length and temperature to determine when to flower, grow, or drop leaves.

🌍

How much oxygen?

Click to reveal

Plants and algae together produce about 70% of Earth's oxygen! Without photosynthesis, the air we breathe wouldn't exist as we know it.

🔬

Stomata facts

Click to reveal

A single leaf can have hundreds of thousands of stomata! They open during the day to let in CO₂ and close at night to conserve water.

Exercise 4: Short Answer Questions

Q1. Why photosynthesis is important for plants?

Think about: How do plants get their food? What would happen if they couldn't make glucose?
Model Answer: Photosynthesis is important for plants because it is the process by which they make their own food (glucose). Without photosynthesis, plants would not have the energy they need to grow, reproduce, and survive.

Q2. What are the three main things required for photosynthesis?

Look at the formula box! What three things appear on the left side of the equation?
Model Answer: The three main things required for photosynthesis are: (1) Sunlight — provides the energy, (2) Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) — absorbed from the air through stomata, (3) Water (H₂O) — absorbed from the soil through roots.

Q3. Explain the role of leaves in making food.

Think about chloroplasts, chlorophyll, and stomata — all found in leaves!
Model Answer: Leaves are the food factories of the plant. They contain chloroplasts with chlorophyll which absorbs sunlight. Carbon dioxide enters leaves through stomata. The leaves use sunlight, CO₂, and water to produce glucose and oxygen through photosynthesis.

Q4. Which gas do plants release during photosynthesis, and why is it important for humans?

What do humans (and all animals) breathe in to survive?
Model Answer: Plants release oxygen (O₂) during photosynthesis. This is important for humans because we breathe oxygen to survive. Without the oxygen produced by plants, humans and animals could not live on Earth.

Q5. What would happen if plants stopped performing photosynthesis?

Consider: no food, no oxygen, no energy for living things...
Model Answer: If plants stopped photosynthesis, they would not be able to make food and would die. Without plants, animals that eat plants would have no food. Oxygen levels in the atmosphere would drop. Carbon dioxide levels would rise. All food chains would collapse, and life on Earth would be threatened.
Critical Thinking

Challenge Question 🧠

Think Deeply...

If plants perform photosynthesis to produce food, why is it important for them to have roots, even though they don't make food in the roots?

Consider: What do roots supply to the leaf? Could photosynthesis work without the water that roots absorb? What else do roots do besides supply water?
Great thinking! 🌟 Model Answer:

Even though roots do not make food, they are essential to photosynthesis because:
  1. Roots absorb water (H₂O) from the soil — one of the key ingredients for photosynthesis.
  2. Roots absorb dissolved minerals and nutrients that the plant needs to build chlorophyll and stay healthy.
  3. Roots anchor the plant in the ground, keeping it stable so the leaves can face the sun.
  4. Without water supplied by roots, the chloroplasts in the leaves would have nothing to work with, and photosynthesis would stop completely.
So roots are like the supply chain — photosynthesis happens in the leaves, but it depends entirely on what the roots provide!

🔬 Classroom Activity: Photosynthesis Model

Materials Needed: A small potted plant, a clear plastic bag, and a rubber band.

  1. Cover a leaf of the plant with the plastic bag and secure it with a rubber band.
  2. Place the plant in sunlight for a few hours.
  3. Observe the tiny droplets of water forming inside the plastic bag.

Why does this happen? The water droplets form because leaves release water vapour through transpiration (through their stomata). This shows that the leaves are actively processing water — the same water used in photosynthesis!
