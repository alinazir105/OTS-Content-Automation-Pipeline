Background

What Is an Eclipse?

An eclipse happens when the Sun, Moon, and Earth align, and one blocks the light of another.

The Sun, Moon, and Earth create eclipses when one blocks the light of another. Eclipses are fascinating astronomical events that have been observed by humans for thousands of years. There are two main types: Solar Eclipses (involving the Moon blocking the Sun) and Lunar Eclipses (involving the Earth blocking the Moon).

Solar Eclipses

What Is a Solar Eclipse? ☀️

A solar eclipse occurs when the Moon comes between the Sun and Earth, blocking sunlight from reaching Earth.

A solar eclipse occurs when the Moon comes in between the Sun and Earth, blocking sunlight from reaching the Earth. This makes the Sun appear dark for a short time. Solar eclipses can only be seen from certain parts of the Earth and they happen during the day.

The Moon's shadow falls on specific areas of Earth. If you are in the umbra (dark central shadow), you see a total eclipse. In the penumbra (outer shadow), you see a partial eclipse.

Three Types of Solar Eclipses

🌑
Type 1

Total Solar Eclipse

The Moon completely blocks the Sun. The sky becomes very dark, and you can see the Sun's corona (outer atmosphere). This is the most dramatic type of solar eclipse.

🌗
Type 2

Partial Solar Eclipse

The Moon blocks only part of the Sun. The Sun appears as a crescent shape. Most solar eclipses seen around the world are partial solar eclipses.

💍
Type 3

Annular Solar Eclipse

The Moon blocks the center of the Sun, leaving a bright ring (annulus) around it. This happens when the Moon is farther from Earth and appears smaller than the Sun.

// Solar Eclipse Alignment Sun → Moon → Earth (Moon between Sun and Earth)
Result: Moon's shadow falls on Earth
Visible from: Only specific regions on Earth
Time of day: Daytime
Lunar Eclipses

What Is a Lunar Eclipse? 🌕

A lunar eclipse occurs when the Earth comes between the Sun and the Moon, blocking sunlight from reaching the Moon.

A lunar eclipse occurs when the Earth comes between the Sun and the Moon, blocking sunlight from reaching the Moon. The Moon appears dark or reddish in color. Lunar eclipses happen at night and can be seen from anywhere on the night side of Earth.

During a total lunar eclipse, the Moon turns reddish — this is called the "Blood Moon." It happens because Earth's atmosphere bends some red light around Earth, which then reaches the Moon.

Two Types of Lunar Eclipses

🔴
Type 1

Total Lunar Eclipse

The entire Moon moves into the Earth's shadow. The Moon appears dark and may turn a reddish-orange color (Blood Moon). This is safe to watch with the naked eye.

🌒
Type 2

Partial Lunar Eclipse

Only part of the Moon enters the Earth's shadow. One side of the Moon appears darker while the other side remains bright and visible.

// Lunar Eclipse Alignment Sun → Earth → Moon (Earth between Sun and Moon)
Result: Earth's shadow falls on the Moon
Visible from: Anywhere on the night side of Earth
Time of day: Night
Moon color: Dark or reddish (Blood Moon)

Did You Know? 🤔

🌍

Earth's Shadow

Earth's shadow is much larger than the Moon, so during a total lunar eclipse, the Moon can be fully covered for up to 104 minutes!

🩸

Blood Moon

The Moon turns red during a total lunar eclipse because Earth's atmosphere scatters blue light and lets red light pass through, like a sunset projected on the Moon.

💍

Ring of Fire

An annular solar eclipse is called the "Ring of Fire" because the Moon is farther away and appears smaller, leaving a glowing ring of sunlight visible around it.

📅

Monthly?

We don't see eclipses every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted about 5° compared to Earth's orbit. Perfect alignment is rare!

How Are Eclipses Different?

Click on any row to expand and learn more details. Compare solar and lunar eclipses side by side.

Feature ☀️ Solar Eclipse 🌕 Lunar Eclipse
Time of occurrence During the day At night
Solar eclipses can only happen during a new moon phase, while lunar eclipses can only happen during a full moon phase.
What is blocked? Moon blocks sunlight from reaching Earth Earth blocks sunlight from reaching the Moon
In a solar eclipse, you are standing in the Moon's shadow. In a lunar eclipse, the Moon is in Earth's shadow.
Visibility Only from certain parts of Earth Visible from anywhere on the night side of Earth
Solar eclipses are visible in a narrow path (the Moon's shadow). Lunar eclipses are visible to anyone where the Moon is above the horizon.
Duration Totality lasts only a few minutes Can last over an hour
Total solar eclipse totality lasts only about 7 minutes at most. Total lunar eclipse can last up to about 104 minutes.
Safety to observe Dangerous — never look directly Safe with naked eye
During a solar eclipse, harmful UV and infrared rays can damage your eyes even when the Sun is partially covered. Always use certified eclipse glasses.
Types Total, Partial, Annular Total, Partial
Solar eclipses have an extra type — the Annular eclipse — which occurs when the Moon is farther away and appears too small to fully cover the Sun.

Safety First!

Safety During Eclipses 🛡️

It's important to know how to observe eclipses safely. Follow these guidelines!

🚫

Never Look Directly!

Never look directly at the Sun during a solar eclipse. The Sun's harmful rays can cause permanent eye damage or even blindness, even when the Sun is partially covered by the Moon.

👓

Use Eclipse Glasses

Always use special certified eclipse glasses (ISO 12312-2 standard) when observing a solar eclipse. Regular sunglasses are NOT safe — they do not filter enough harmful radiation.

📦

Pinhole Projector

Use a pinhole projector to safely observe a solar eclipse. Make a small hole in a card, let sunlight pass through it, and project the image onto another surface. Simple and safe!

🌕

Lunar Eclipses: Totally Safe

Lunar eclipses are completely safe to watch with the naked eye! Since you're looking at the Moon (not the Sun), there's no danger. Enjoy watching the Moon turn reddish!

Hands-on Activity

Create a Model of an Eclipse 🔦

Simulate eclipses at home using simple materials!

🔦

Materials Needed

• A flashlight (Sun)
• A small ball (Moon)
• A larger ball (Earth)

1️⃣

Step 1

Place the flashlight on a table to act as the Sun. Make sure it's pointing in one direction.

2️⃣

Step 2

Hold the large ball (Earth) in front of the flashlight. Notice its shadow.

3️⃣

Step 3 – Lunar Eclipse

Put the smaller ball (Moon) behind the Earth. Observe the shadow on the Moon — this is a lunar eclipse!

4️⃣

Step 4 – Solar Eclipse

Move the smaller ball between the flashlight and the Earth. Observe the shadow on Earth — this is a solar eclipse!
