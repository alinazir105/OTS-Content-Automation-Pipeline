Background

Why Vocabulary Matters in Science

Understanding scientific words helps you think clearly, describe observations accurately, and communicate ideas like a real scientist.

What is a Glossary?

A glossary is an alphabetical list of important terms with their meanings. In science, precise vocabulary allows us to describe the natural world without confusion. Each word you learn in science is a new tool in your scientific toolkit!


This lesson covers vocabulary from 9 different science units — from how plants make food using sunlight, all the way to solar and lunar eclipses in space.

Worked Examples

Terms in Context

See how key vocabulary is used in real scientific sentences and reasoning.


🌿 Example 1: Photosynthesis Sentence

During photosynthesis, plants use energy from sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to produce glucose — a form of sugar. This process happens mainly in leaves, which have tiny openings called stomata that allow gases to enter and exit.

"Photo" comes from the Greek word for light, and "synthesis" means to make or put together. So photosynthesis literally means making something with light — plants use light to make food!

🌸 Example 2: Seed Dispersal

Dispersal is the movement of seeds from one place to another. Seeds can be dispersed by wind (like dandelions), water (coconuts), animals (berries), or explosion (touch-me-not plants). After dispersal, seeds may undergo germination if conditions are favourable — warm, damp soil with adequate sunlight.

"Damp" means slightly wet or moist — not completely soaked, but not dry either. Seeds need damp soil to absorb water and begin germinating.

⚡ Example 3: Electric Circuit

An electric circuit is a closed path through which current (flow of electric charges) can travel. A simple circuit includes a battery, wires, and a bulb. All these are components. Appliances like fans, televisions, and refrigerators are powered by the same principle of electricity flowing through circuits.

Electricity is the general form of energy. Current is specifically the flow of electric charges through a conductor. Think of electricity as the water in a pipe, and current as how fast that water is flowing!

🌑 Example 4: Solar Eclipse

A solar eclipse occurs when the Moon moves between Earth and the Sun, casting a shadow on Earth. If only part of the Sun is covered, it is a partial eclipse. If the Moon is farther away and appears smaller, a ring of sunlight remains visible — this is an annular eclipse. A lunar eclipse is opposite: Earth comes between the Sun and Moon.

"Solar" relates to the Sun (think: solar panel, solar system). "Lunar" relates to the Moon (think: lunar calendar, lunar module). In a solar eclipse, the Sun is blocked. In a lunar eclipse, the Moon is shadowed.

🦠 Example 5: Microorganisms

Microorganisms are the smallest living things, visible only under a microscope. Bacteria are single-celled organisms that can cause illness. Viruses are even smaller and can only reproduce inside a host cell. Mold is a type of fungi that grows on food in damp conditions. Doctors prescribe antibiotics to fight bacterial infections — but antibiotics do NOT work against viruses!

Antibiotics target specific parts of bacterial cells to stop them from growing. Viruses don't have the same cell structure — they're not even technically "alive" on their own. A microbiologist studies these tiny organisms in detail.

Did You Know? 🤔

🌿

Photosynthesis Fact

A single large tree can absorb up to 48 pounds of CO₂ per year through photosynthesis and release enough oxygen for 2 people to breathe!

🦠

Bacteria Fact

Click to reveal!

The human body contains more bacterial cells than human cells! Most bacteria in our gut are helpful, not harmful.

⚡

Electricity Fact

Click to reveal!

Lightning is a natural form of electricity. A single lightning bolt can reach temperatures of 30,000°C — 5 times hotter than the Sun's surface!

🌑

Eclipse Fact

Click to reveal!

A total solar eclipse happens somewhere on Earth roughly every 18 months, but any single location only experiences one every 375 years on average!

🔊

Sound Fact

Click to reveal!

Sound travels about 343 metres per second in air, but travels 4 times faster in water and 15 times faster in steel!

🐣

Hen Life Cycle Fact

Click to reveal!

A hen's egg takes about 21 days of incubation to hatch. The chick uses a special "egg tooth" on its beak to break through the shell!

Challenge Yourself! 🚀

🧠 Critical Thinking Challenges

  • If all decomposers disappeared from an ecosystem, what would eventually happen to the food chain? Use the terms producers, consumers, and nutrients in your answer.
  • Compare a solar eclipse and a lunar eclipse. Draw a diagram showing the position of Earth, Moon, and Sun in each case. Label partial and annular regions.
  • A plant is placed in a dark room for a week. Explain what happens to its glucose production and how this affects its stomata. What does this tell us about photosynthesis?
  • Design a simple experiment to show how damp conditions affect germination. Write a hypothesis, a method, and predict your results.
  • Why should we not overuse antibiotics? Research and explain how bacteria can become resistant — and what role a microbiologist plays in solving this problem.
