Background Knowledge

Output Devices & Computers

Computers process data, but to share results with users, they need output devices. Let's understand what they are!

🔍 What is an Output Device?

Output devices are hardware components that convey information from the computer to the user. They take processed data and present it in a form humans can understand — as text, images, sounds, or printed pages.

🖥️

Monitor

Displays visual information — text, images, and videos on screen in real time.

🔊

Speaker

Converts digital audio signals into sound waves that we can hear.

🖨️

Printer

Produces permanent output on paper — the focus of this unit!

Key Definition
Printer = Output Device that transforms digital information → physical hard copy on paper
Used for: text • images • graphics • documents

🔍 Inside a Dot Matrix Printer

Here's how a dot matrix printer creates characters step by step:

1
The computer sends character data to the printer's control electronics.
2
The print head (containing 9 or 24 pins arranged in a matrix) moves across the paper.
3
Selected pins fire forward, striking the ink ribbon against the paper to create tiny dots.
4
The pattern of dots forms the shape of each character. More pins = sharper output (24-pin is clearer than 9-pin).
🔲 → · · · → [A] character appears!
Even though dot matrix printers are older technology, they are still used because:
  • They can print on multi-part carbon copy forms (e.g., receipts with copies)
  • They are very durable and low-cost to operate
  • They work well in dusty or industrial environments
  • Commonly found in banks, billing counters, and invoice printing
Non-Impact Printers

⚡ Non-Impact Printer Types

Non-impact printers use modern technologies — no physical striking! They are quieter, faster, and produce higher quality output.

💧

Ink-Jet Printer

Non-Impact
  • Sprays patterns of ink through tiny nozzles onto paper
  • Produces high-quality images and text
  • Commonly used in homes and offices
  • Can print in full color
🔴

Laser Printer

Non-Impact
  • Uses a laser beam to create an image on a drum
  • Drum attracts toner, transferred to paper via heat & pressure
  • Known for speed & precision
  • Prints 4–20 pages per minute (ppm)

🔍 Inside a Laser Printer

The laser printing process explained step by step:

1
Charging: The drum is given a uniform negative electrostatic charge.
2
Exposing: A laser beam scans across the drum, removing charge from areas where toner should stick — creating an invisible image.
3
Developing: Toner (fine powder) is attracted to the discharged (exposed) areas on the drum.
4
Transferring: The toner image is transferred from the drum onto paper.
5
Fusing: Heat and pressure from the fuser unit permanently bonds the toner to the paper. Output is ready! 🖨️
Laser printers are preferred in offices because:
  • They print 4–20 pages per minute — much faster than ink-jet
  • Toner cartridges last much longer than ink cartridges
  • Output is precise and doesn't smudge when wet
  • Lower cost per page when printing large volumes
  • Quieter operation compared to impact printers

  • Sharp, precise output
  • Low cost per page

  • Higher upfront cost
  • Color laser is expensive
  • Larger & heavier

Q1: What is the primary purpose of a printer?
✅ To produce permanent output on paper — transforming digital information into a physical format (hard copy).
Q2: Which printer prints characters as a combination of dots?
✅ The Dot Matrix Printer. It uses a print head with 9 or 24 pins that strike a carbon ribbon to create dot patterns.
Q3: Name one key difference between impact and non-impact printers.
✅ Impact printers physically strike the paper using hammers/pins. Non-impact printers are quieter, faster, and use ink or laser technology without physical contact.
Q4: How does a laser printer transfer toner to paper?
✅ A laser beam creates an image on a drum, which attracts toner (powder). The toner is transferred to paper using heat and pressure.
Q5: Where is a Line Printer most commonly used?
✅ Line printers were commonly used in early digital computing and are still found in some industrial applications for bulk text printing.
Q6: What is the speed range of a laser printer?
✅ Laser printers print at speeds ranging from 4 to 20 pages per minute (ppm).

Challenge 1: Choosing the Right Printer

A small bakery wants to print receipts for customers. They need a printer that can print on multi-part carbon copy paper, is low-cost to run, and doesn't need high-quality graphics. Which printer should they choose?

Think about which printer type can print on multi-part forms and has a very low running cost. It might be noisy, but that's acceptable in a bakery!
Dot Matrix Printer! It can print on multi-part carbon copy forms, has very low operating costs, and is durable enough for a shop environment. The noise level is acceptable in a busy bakery.

Challenge 2: Office vs Home

A busy law firm prints hundreds of pages of black-and-white text documents every day. At home, a student occasionally prints colorful school projects. Which type of printer is best for each, and why?

The law firm needs speed and low cost per page for large volumes. The student needs color quality for occasional printing.
Law Firm → Laser Printer: Prints 4–20 ppm, very low cost per page, sharp text, perfect for high-volume B&W documents.

Student → Ink-Jet Printer: Affordable to buy, produces high-quality color images and text, perfect for occasional use at home.
