Core Concept

Using Slide Masters

A Slide Master allows you to set up a uniform style for your entire presentation. Changes made to the Slide Master will automatically apply to every slide, helping keep a consistent look.

🎨

What is a Slide Master?

A special "parent" slide that controls the fonts, colours, backgrounds, and layout for all slides in your presentation.

⚡

Why Use It?

Instead of formatting each slide individually, one change on the Slide Master updates every slide instantly — saving time and ensuring consistency.

🏗️

Custom Layouts

You can also create custom slide layouts within the Slide Master for different sections of your presentation.

Quick Path
View Tab → Slide Master → Edit → Close Master View
This is the fastest way to access and modify the Slide Master in PowerPoint.

Steps to Use the Slide Master

1

Open the Slide Master

Go to the View tab and click Slide Master. This view shows the master slide and all layouts underneath it.

2

Edit the Slide Master

Change fonts, colours, or backgrounds on the Slide Master and it will automatically apply to all slides.

  • Click on any text to change the font family and size
  • Use the Background Styles button to set a theme colour
  • Add a logo that will appear on every slide
3

Create Custom Layouts

Add new layouts under the Slide Master if you want different styles for specific slides (e.g., a title slide layout vs. a content layout).

4

Close the Slide Master

Click the Close Master View button once you're done. All slides in your presentation will now follow the styles you set!

  • Background colour or image
  • Font family (e.g., Arial, Calibri, Poppins)
  • Font sizes for headings and body text
  • Slide number position and style
  • Logo or watermark position
  • Colour scheme (theme colours)
Core Concept

Organizing Slides with Sections

In larger presentations, sections can help you manage and organize slides into logical parts — just like chapters in a book!

📂

Add a Section

Right-click between slides in the left slide panel, then select Add Section. Give it a meaningful name like "Introduction" or "Conclusion."

✏️

Manage Sections

Right-click on a section name to rename, expand, or collapse it. You can also move entire sections to reorder your presentation.

🧭

Benefits of Sections

Sections make it much easier to navigate and keep track of slides, especially in long presentations with 20+ slides.

How to Add a Section
Right-click between slides → Add Section → Type section name → Press Enter
Tip: Use names like "Introduction", "Main Content", "Body", "Conclusion"

🔍 Why Sections Matter

Imagine presenting a 30-slide project on "Climate Change." Without sections, finding "Slide 18 – Solutions" is tedious. With sections named "Causes", "Effects", and "Solutions", navigation is instant! You can even collapse sections you're not working on to reduce screen clutter.

Design Knowledge

Design Principles for Slides

Creating a professional look is about more than just applying themes. Here are the key principles for designing great slides.

⬜

1. Use of Whitespace

Leave some empty spaces around your content. This helps make your slides look less cluttered and allows viewers to focus on what matters. Whitespace is not "wasted" space — it's breathing room for your ideas!

🎨

2. Colour and Contrast

Choose colours that make text easy to read against the background. Use contrast to highlight key points. For example, dark text on a light background or white text on a dark background works best.

🔤

3. Font Selection

Choose simple, clean fonts like Arial or Calibri for readability. Avoid using too many font types or sizes in one presentation. Stick to 1–2 font families maximum.

Professional designers follow the "3-Font Rule": use a maximum of 3 different fonts in one presentation.

  • Font 1: For the main title (e.g., bold Poppins)
  • Font 2: For slide headings (e.g., semi-bold Calibri)
  • Font 3: For body text (e.g., regular Arial)

Good contrast combinations for slides:

  • Dark navy background + white text ✅
  • White background + dark grey text ✅
  • Yellow background + yellow text ❌ (hard to read!)
  • Light grey background + white text ❌ (low contrast!)

Discovery Zone

With vs Without Good Design

See the difference good design principles make to a presentation slide.

❌ Poor Design

• 8 different fonts used

• Yellow text on white background (no contrast!)

• Every inch crammed with text and images

• Inconsistent slide layouts throughout

• No whitespace — very cluttered

• No sections — hard to navigate

✅ Professional Design

• 1–2 clean fonts (Arial or Calibri)

• Dark text on light background (great contrast!)

• Generous whitespace around content

• Consistent layout from Slide Master

• Key points only — no text overload

• Sections named: Intro, Body, Conclusion

🧪 Lab Activities

Complete these practical activities to master professional presentations in PowerPoint.

Lab Activity 1

First Professional Presentation

Objective:

Practice all the skills from this chapter together.

Instructions:
  1. Create a new presentation with at least five slides.
  2. Use the Slide Master to set a background and font style for the entire presentation.
  3. Organize slides into two sections, naming them "Introduction" and "Main Content."
  4. Apply design principles learned in this chapter (whitespace, contrast, fonts).
Lab Activity 2

Consistent Slide Layout Using Slide Masters

Objective:

To create a uniform look across slides.

Instructions:
  1. Open a new PowerPoint file and go to the Slide Master view.
  2. Select a theme and customize by changing font style and background colour.
  3. Add a logo or watermark to the Slide Master.
  4. Close the view to apply changes to all slides.
  5. Save the presentation and present your layout to the class.
Lab Activity 3

Organizing Slides into Sections

Objective:

To learn how to manage and organize slides using sections.

Instructions:
  1. Create a presentation with at least ten slides.
  2. Divide the slides into three sections: Introduction, Body, and Conclusion.
  3. Name each section accordingly.
  4. Collapse and expand sections to see how organization improves slide navigation.
Lab Activity 4

Effective Colour and Font Choices

Objective:

To understand the impact of design choices on readability and aesthetics.

Instructions:
  1. Create a slide with a title, a paragraph of text, and an image.
  2. Apply a colour scheme with good contrast between text and background.
  3. Experiment with different fonts and sizes to find the most readable.
  4. Present your slide and explain your design choices.
Lab Activity 5

Whitespace and Alignment

Objective:

To learn how whitespace and alignment affect presentation design.

Instructions:
  1. Create two versions of a slide on any topic.
  2. Version 1: Fill the slide with text and images without any spacing.
  3. Version 2: Use whitespace effectively, align text and images properly, keep it uncluttered.
  4. Present both versions to the class and discuss the impact of whitespace on readability.
