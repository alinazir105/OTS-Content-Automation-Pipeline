📖 Introduction

What Are We Learning?

Factors and multiples help us understand how numbers relate to each other. Let's explore two powerful mathematical tools!

🔢

Factors

A factor is a number that divides another number exactly (with no remainder). For example, factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12.

✖️

Multiples

A multiple is the result of multiplying a number by an integer. For example, multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12, 16, 20...

🔍

Prime Numbers

Prime numbers are only divisible by 1 and themselves. Examples: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19... These are the building blocks!

🌟

Prime Factorization

Every number can be written as a product of prime numbers. This is called prime factorization — the key method for HCF and LCM!

🔎

Discovery Zone: The Connection

HCF and LCM are deeply connected. If you know the HCF and LCM of two numbers, you can find their product:

Key Relationship
HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b

For example: HCF(4, 6) = 2, LCM(4, 6) = 12. Check: 2 × 12 = 24 = 4 × 6 ✓

🔢 Core Concept

HCF — Highest Common Factor

The largest factor that two or more numbers share in common.

📚

Breaking Down the Name

  • Highest — meaning largest or greatest number
  • Common — meaning shared between two or more numbers
  • Factor — meaning an integer that a whole number can be divided by (a divisor)
💡

Also Known As

HCF is also called Greatest Common Divisor (GCD).

It is the BIGGEST number that divides all the given numbers without leaving a remainder.

HCF Method 1 — Prime Factorization
Step 1: Find prime factors of each number
Step 2: Identify COMMON prime factors
Step 3: Multiply the common prime factors → HCF
HCF Method 2 — Long Division
Step 1: Divide the larger number by the smaller
Step 2: Divide previous divisor by the remainder
Step 3: Repeat until remainder = 0 → Last divisor = HCF

✖️ Core Concept

LCM — Least Common Multiple

The smallest number that is a multiple of two or more given numbers.

📚

Breaking Down the Name

  • Lowest — meaning smallest number
  • Common — meaning shared between two or more numbers
  • Multiple — meaning the product of two numbers
💡

Key Property

The LCM of two or more numbers is the smallest number which can be exactly divided by each of the given numbers.

LCM is always greater than or equal to the largest number in the set.

LCM Method 1 — Prime Factorization
Step 1: Find prime factors of each number
Step 2: Identify COMMON and UNCOMMON prime factors
Step 3: LCM = Product of common × Product of uncommon
LCM Method 2 — Division Method (Ladder)
Step 1: Write all numbers in a row
Step 2: Divide by smallest prime that divides at least one number
Step 3: Repeat until all values become 1
Step 4: LCM = Product of all divisors used

Step-by-Step Solutions

🔢 HCF Examples

Example 1: HCF of 27 and 36 (Prime Factorization Method)

1
Find prime factors of 27: Divide 27 ÷ 3 = 9, then 9 ÷ 3 = 3, then 3 ÷ 3 = 1
∴ 27 = 3 × 3 × 3
2
Find prime factors of 36: 36 ÷ 2 = 18, 18 ÷ 2 = 9, 9 ÷ 3 = 3, 3 ÷ 3 = 1
∴ 36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3
3
Find common factors: Both have 3 × 3 in common
HCF(27, 36) = 3 × 3 = 9 ✓

Example 2: HCF of 66 and 88 (Long Division Method)

1
Divide larger by smaller: 88 ÷ 66 = 1 remainder 22
2
Now divide 66 by the remainder: 66 ÷ 22 = 3 remainder 0
3
Remainder is 0, so the last divisor is the HCF!
HCF(66, 88) = 22 ✓

Example 3: HCF of 36, 54 and 138 (Long Division Method)

1
Find HCF of first two: 36 and 54
54 ÷ 36 = 1 rem 18 → 36 ÷ 18 = 2 rem 0 → HCF(36, 54) = 18
2
Now find HCF of 18 and 138
138 ÷ 18 = 7 rem 12 → 18 ÷ 12 = 1 rem 6 → 12 ÷ 6 = 2 rem 0 → HCF = 6
3
HCF of all three numbers = HCF(HCF(36,54), 138) = HCF(18, 138)
HCF(36, 54, 138) = 6 ✓

✖️ LCM Examples

Example 1: LCM of 44, 66 and 99 (Prime Factorization Method)

1
44 = 2 × 2 × 11   |   66 = 2 × 3 × 11   |   99 = 3 × 3 × 11
2
Common factor to all three: 11
3
Uncommon factors: 2 × 2 (from 44) × 3 × 3 (from 99) = 36
4
LCM = Product of all unique prime factors with highest powers:
2² × 3² × 11 = 4 × 9 × 11
LCM(44, 66, 99) = 396 ✓

📋 Comparing Both Methods

Feature Prime Factorization Division / Long Division
⚡ Speed Good for small numbers Efficient for large numbers

For small numbers (under 100), prime factorization is intuitive. For large numbers, the division method is faster and less error-prone.
🎯 Accuracy Visual and clear Systematic step-by-step

Both methods give the same correct answer. Prime factorization helps you see the structure of numbers, while division is more algorithmic.

📝 Solve These Problems

HCF · Prime Factorization

Find HCF(8, 64)

Use prime factorization to find the HCF of 8 and 64.

Hint: 8 = 2 × 2 × 2 and 64 = 26. Find all common powers of 2. The answer is 8!
HCF · Division Method

Find HCF(48, 75)

Use the long division method to find the HCF.

Hint: 75 ÷ 48 = 1 rem 27 → 48 ÷ 27 = 1 rem 21 → 27 ÷ 21 = 1 rem 6 → 21 ÷ 6 = 3 rem 3 → 6 ÷ 3 = 2 rem 0. HCF = 3!
LCM · Prime Factorization

Find LCM(16, 24, 40)

Use prime factorization to find the LCM of these three numbers.

Hint: 16 = 2⁴, 24 = 2³ × 3, 40 = 2³ × 5. LCM = highest powers = 2⁴ × 3 × 5 = 16 × 3 × 5 = 240!
LCM · Division Method

Find LCM(36, 60, 120)

Use the division (ladder) method to find the LCM.

Hint: Start dividing by 2: 18, 30, 60 → 9, 15, 30 → divide by 3: 3, 5, 10 → 1, 5, 10 → divide by 5: 1, 1, 2 → 2. LCM = 2×2×3×3×5×2 = 360!
HCF · Three Numbers

Find HCF(32, 48, 96)

All three numbers are powers of 2 — what is their HCF?

Hint: 32 = 2⁵, 48 = 2⁴ × 3, 96 = 2⁵ × 3. The common prime factor is 2⁴. HCF = 16!
LCM · Word Problem

Find LCM(18, 27)

18 = 2 × 3² and 27 = 3³. Use this to find the LCM.

Hint: Take the highest power of each prime. LCM = 2¹ × 3³ = 2 × 27 = 54!

🌍 Real World

Where Do We Use HCF & LCM?

These concepts appear everywhere in daily life — from tiles to traffic lights!

🏗️

Construction & Tiling

Builders use HCF to find the largest square tile that can perfectly cover both a 48cm × 72cm floor without cutting. HCF(48, 72) = 24cm.

🚦

Traffic Light Synchronization

City planners use LCM to determine when multiple traffic lights will all be green at the same time — syncing traffic flow efficiently.

🏃

Race Tracks

If two runners complete laps every 3 and 4 minutes, they'll be at the starting line together every LCM(3, 4) = 12 minutes.

🎁

Dividing Equally

HCF helps you divide objects into equal groups. With 24 apples and 36 oranges, you can make HCF(24, 36) = 12 equal fruit baskets.

⚡

Power Schedules

Load shedding schedules use LCM to predict when different areas will simultaneously lose power, helping engineers plan maintenance.

🎵

Music & Rhythm

Musicians use LCM to find when different rhythmic patterns (like 3-beat and 4-beat) will align, creating harmonious compositions.
