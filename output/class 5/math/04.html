Core Concept

Place Value in Decimals

A decimal is a number that contains a fractional part. The decimal point separates the whole number part from the fractional part. The number of digits on the right side determines the decimal places.

How to Read Decimal Places

Consider 765.984. Each digit has a specific place value. The dot (.) is the decimal point.

Operation 1

Addition of Decimals

When adding decimals, always align the decimal points in a column. Write zero (0) where there is no digit to keep place values lined up.

Golden Rule for Addition
Align decimal points → add column by column → carry if needed
Always write 0 where there is no digit (e.g., 7.2 becomes 07.20)

Example: 20.25 + 7.52

  • 1Write 20.25 and below it 07.52 — align decimal points
  • 2Hundredths: 5 + 2 = 7
  • 3Tenths: 2 + 5 = 7
  • 4Ones: 0 + 7 = 7
  • 5Tens: 2 + 0 = 2
  • 6Answer: 27.77

2.68 + 3.64

  • 1Hundredths: 8+4=12, write 2, carry 1
  • 2Tenths: 6+6+1=13, write 3, carry 1
  • 3Ones: 2+3+1=6
  • ✓Answer: 6.32

68.7 + 53.8

  • 1Tenths: 7+8=15, write 5, carry 1
  • 2Ones: 8+3+1=12, write 2, carry 1
  • 3Tens: 6+5+1=12
  • ✓Answer: 122.5

When adding hundredths like 0.07 + 0.08: think of it as 7 + 8 = 15 hundredths = 0.15. Just add the digits and place the decimal correctly!

Operation 2

Subtraction of Decimals

Subtraction works just like addition — align decimal points, write zeros where needed, then subtract column by column, borrowing when necessary.

Golden Rule for Subtraction
Align decimal points → subtract right to left → borrow if digit above is smaller
17.5 → write as 17.50 when subtracting from a number with 2 decimal places

Example: 58.75 − 17.5

  • 1Write 17.5 as 17.50 (add trailing zero)
  • 2Hundredths: 5 − 0 = 5
  • 3Tenths: 7 − 5 = 2
  • 4Ones: 8 − 7 = 1
  • 5Tens: 5 − 1 = 4
  • ✓Answer: 41.25

8.5 − 2.1

  • 1Tenths: 5 − 1 = 4
  • 2Ones: 8 − 2 = 6
  • ✓Answer: 6.4

24.95 − 11.83

  • 1Hundredths: 5 − 3 = 2
  • 2Tenths: 9 − 8 = 1
  • 3Ones: 4 − 1 = 3
  • 4Tens: 2 − 1 = 1
  • ✓Answer: 13.12

Operation 3

Multiplication of Decimals

Multiply decimals like whole numbers, then count the total decimal places in both numbers and place the decimal point in the answer.

✖️ 10

× 10 Rule

Move decimal point 1 place to the right. The value increases 10 times.

2.23 × 10 = 22.3

✖️ 100

× 100 Rule

Move decimal point 2 places to the right. The value increases 100 times.

2.23 × 100 = 223

✖️ 1000

× 1000 Rule

Move decimal point 3 places to the right. The value increases 1000 times.

2.23 × 1000 = 2230

Multiplying by a Whole Number: 2.3 × 24

  • 1Ignore the decimal: multiply 23 × 24 = 552
  • 2Count decimal places in 2.3 → 1 decimal place
  • 3Place decimal 1 place from right: 55.2
  • ✓Answer: 55.2

Multiplying Decimal × Decimal: 0.25 × 0.056

  • 10.056 has 3 decimal places, 0.25 has 2 decimal places
  • 2Total decimal places = 3 + 2 = 5
  • 3Multiply 56 × 25 = 1400
  • 4Place decimal 5 places from right: 0.01400
  • ✓Answer: 0.014
Key Insight: Tenths & Hundredths
2.23 × 0.1 = 0.223  |  2.23 × 0.01 = 0.0223
Multiplying by 0.1 moves decimal LEFT 1 place. Multiplying by 0.01 moves it LEFT 2 places.

Operation 4

Division of Decimals

Division is the inverse of multiplication. When dividing by 10, 100, or 1000, move the decimal point to the LEFT. When dividing decimal by decimal, convert to a simpler form.

➗ 10

÷ 10 Rule

Move decimal point 1 place LEFT. Value decreases 10×.

15.34 ÷ 10 = 1.534

➗ 100

÷ 100 Rule

Move decimal point 2 places LEFT. Value decreases 100×.

15.34 ÷ 100 = 0.1534

➗ 1000

÷ 1000 Rule

Move decimal point 3 places LEFT. Value decreases 1000×.

15.34 ÷ 1000 = 0.01534

Example: 0.9 ÷ 3

  • 10.9 = 9 tenths
  • 29 tenths ÷ 3 = 3 tenths
  • ✓Answer: 0.3

Example: 0.84 ÷ 4

  • 1Split: (0.80 + 0.04) ÷ 4
  • 28 tenths ÷ 4 = 2 tenths
  • 34 hundredths ÷ 4 = 1 hundredth
  • 420 hundredths + 1 hundredth = 21 hundredths
  • ✓Answer: 0.21

Dividing Decimal by Decimal: 0.55 ÷ 0.05

  • 1Both have 2 decimal places → 0.55 ÷ 0.05 = 55 ÷ 5
  • 255 ÷ 5 = 11
  • ✓Answer: 11

Dividing: 0.125 ÷ 0.5

  • 10.125 has 3 dp, 0.5 has 1 dp → difference = 2 dp remain in answer
  • 2Multiply both by 10: 1.25 ÷ 5
  • 31.25 ÷ 5 = 0.25
  • ✓Answer: 0.25

Operation 5

Simplification with Brackets

When an expression has brackets, solve what's inside the brackets first (BODMAS rule). Then work left to right with the remaining operations.

BODMAS Order
Brackets → Orders → Division → Multiplication → Addition → Subtraction
Always solve the innermost brackets first!

Example 1: 24.24 − (5.6 + 20.25 − 4.45)

  • 1Inside bracket — addition first: 5.6 + 20.25 = 25.85
  • 2Inside bracket — subtraction: 25.85 − 4.45 = 21.40
  • 3Main expression: 24.24 − 21.40 = 2.84
  • ✓Answer: 2.84

Example 2: 52.05 + (28.22 − 22.6) + (13.15 − 6.56)

  • 1First bracket: 28.22 − 22.6 = 5.62
  • 2Second bracket: 13.15 − 6.56 = 6.59
  • 3Add: 52.05 + 5.62 = 57.67
  • 4Add: 57.67 + 6.59 = 64.26
  • ✓Answer: 64.26

Squares: (7.1)² − 6.7 × 3.1

  • 1(7.1)² = 7.1 × 7.1 = 50.41
  • 26.7 × 3.1 = 20.77
  • 350.41 − 20.77 = 29.64
  • ✓Answer: 29.64

Step 1: Solve bracket (5.6 − 4.6) = 1.0
Step 2: (9.5)² = 9.5 × 9.5 = 90.25
Step 3: 90.25 ÷ 1.0 = 90.25
Answer: 90.25

Rounding Off Decimals

Rounding makes numbers easier to work with. We can round to the nearest whole number, tenth, or hundredth.

🔵

Round to Whole Number

Look at the tenths digit:
• Less than 5 → keep whole number same
• 5 or more → add 1 to whole number

Example: 2.3 → 2 | 5.6 → 6

🟣

Round to One Decimal

Look at the hundredths digit:
• Less than 5 → keep tenths same
• 5 or more → add 1 to tenths

Example: 3.47 → 3.5 | 2.41 → 2.4

🟡

Round to Two Decimals

Look at the thousandths digit:
• Less than 5 → keep hundredths same
• 5 or more → add 1 to hundredths

Example: 32.38 → 32.4 (1dp) or 32.38 (2dp)

Conversion

Fractions ↔ Decimals

Fractions and decimals are two ways to represent the same value. Converting between them is a key skill!

Fraction → Decimal

  • 1Divide the numerator by denominator
  • 2Or: if denominator is 10 or 100, directly place decimal
  • 315/100 = 0.15 | 7/100 = 0.07 | 42/100 = 0.42

Decimal → Fraction

  • 1Count decimal places
  • 21 dp → denominator 10 | 2 dp → denominator 100
  • 30.1 = 1/10 | 0.11 = 11/100 | 0.5 = 5/10 = 1/2

Decimals and Percentages

The word "Percent" comes from Latin — "Per" (out of) + "Cent" (hundred). So percent always means out of 100, written with the symbol %.

Converting Decimal to Percentage
Decimal × 100 = Percentage

Real Life

Word Problems

Apply your decimal skills to real-world situations. Read carefully, identify what operation to use, then solve step by step.

1
Sabir bought two chickens of weight 1.450 kg and 1.685 kg respectively. Find the total weight of both chickens.
1.450 + 1.685 = 3.135 kg

The total weight of both chickens is 3.135 kg.

2
In a general store, 200.750 kg of flour and 98.500 kg of sugar were sold. How much more flour was sold than sugar?
200.750 − 98.500 = 102.250 kg

102.250 kg more flour was sold than sugar.

3
Sara bought a necklace of gold of mass 2.565 g and bangles of mass 8.875 g. What is the total mass of these two items?
2.565 + 8.875 = 11.440 g

The total mass of both items is 11.440 g.

4
Aftab saved Rs 3,206.75 from his monthly salary of Rs 35,200. Find his monthly expenditure.
35,200.00 − 3,206.75 = 31,993.25

Aftab's monthly expenditure is Rs 31,993.25.
