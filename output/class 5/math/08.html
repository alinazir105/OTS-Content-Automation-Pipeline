Introduction

What are Perimeter and Area?

Two of the most important measurements we use every day — from fencing a garden to tiling a floor!

📏

Perimeter

The total length of all sides (boundaries) of any closed figure. Think of it as the distance you walk around the outside of a shape.

Unit: mm, cm, m, km

🔲

Area

The measure of the region inside a closed figure. Think of it as counting how many unit squares fit inside a shape.

Unit: sq. mm, sq. cm, sq. m, sq. km

🏡

Real-World Uses

Farmers fence fields (perimeter), architects tile floors (area), and gardeners plant grass (area). These measurements are everywhere!

Mathematics in daily life

Discovery Zone

Key Insight: Perimeter and Area measure different things! Perimeter measures the boundary (a length), while Area measures the surface (a region). They have different units!

Perimeter unit = length unit (cm, m…)
Area unit = length² (sq. cm, sq. m…)
Background

Shapes We Will Study

Understanding the properties of squares, rectangles, and triangles is key to finding perimeter and area.

Rectangle

Has 2 pairs of equal sides. Length (longer side) and Breadth (shorter side). Opposite sides are equal.

Properties
4 sides, 4 right angles
Opposite sides are equal and parallel

Square

A special rectangle where all 4 sides are equal. It is a quadrilateral shape — all sides and all angles are equal.

Properties
4 equal sides, 4 right angles
All sides are equal

Triangle

Has 3 sides and 3 angles. Perimeter is the sum of all three sides. Sides may be measured in different units — convert first!

Key Rule
10 mm = 1 cm
Always use the same unit before adding

Core Concepts

Formulas for Perimeter & Area

Master these formulas and you can solve any perimeter or area problem!

PERIMETER

Square
P = 4 × side
All 4 sides are equal, so multiply one side by 4
Rectangle
P = 2 × (Length + Breadth)
Also: P = L + B + L + B = 2L + 2B
Triangle
P = side₁ + side₂ + side₃
Add all three sides (convert units first!)
Find Side of Rectangle
Breadth = P/2 − Length
Rearrange the formula to find a missing side
Find Side of Square
Side = Perimeter ÷ 4
Divide total perimeter by 4 equal sides

AREA

Square
Area = side × side
Multiply the side length by itself
Rectangle
Area = Length × Breadth
Multiply the two different side measurements
Units of Area
sq. cm, sq. m, sq. km
Area is always measured in "square" units
Key Rule

Always check units! If one side is in metres and another in centimetres, convert them to the same unit before calculating. 1 m = 100 cm 1 cm = 10 mm

Counting unit squares = Area:

5 × 2 = 10 unit squares = 10 sq. cm

Worked Examples

Step-by-Step Solutions

Follow along with these fully solved problems — from the textbook!

1 Rectangle — Perimeter & Area

Find the perimeter and area of a rectangle with sides of 5 cm and 6 cm.

  1. Identify: Length = 6 cm, Breadth = 5 cm
  2. Perimeter formula: P = B + L + B + L = 2 × (L + B)
  3. Calculate perimeter: P = 5 + 6 + 5 + 6 = 22 cm
  4. Area formula: Area = Length × Breadth
  5. Calculate area: Area = 6 × 5 = 30 sq. cm
✅ Perimeter = 22 cm  |  Area = 30 sq. cm
In the rectangle, count the unit squares: 6 columns × 5 rows = 30 squares. Each small square is 1 sq. cm, so the total area is 30 sq. cm.

2 Rectangular Field — Perimeter

Length of a rectangular field is 30 m and its breadth is 20 m. Find the perimeter.

  1. Given: Length = 30 m, Breadth = 20 m
  2. Formula: P = 2 × (Length + Breadth)
  3. Substitute: P = 2 × (30 + 20)
  4. Simplify: P = 2 × 50 = 100 m
✅ Perimeter of the rectangular field = 100 m
Remember: You only need the length and breadth for a rectangle. There are 2 lengths and 2 breadths, so multiply the sum by 2.

3 Square Ground — Distance Covered

A square ground has each side 36 m. What distance will a boy cover by cycling around the ground three times?

  1. Given: Each side of square = 36 m
  2. Perimeter of square: P = 4 × side = 4 × 36 = 144 m
  3. One round = 144 m
  4. Three rounds: 144 × 3 = 432 m
✅ Distance covered in 3 rounds = 432 m
First find the perimeter (one full round). Then multiply by the number of rounds. 144 × 3 = 432 m.

4 Rectangle — Finding Missing Side

The perimeter of a rectangular park is 320 m. Its length is 70 m. Find the breadth.

  1. Given: Perimeter = 320 m, Length = 70 m
  2. Formula: P = 2 × (Length + Breadth)
  3. Rearrange: Breadth = P/2 − Length
  4. Calculate: Breadth = (320 ÷ 2) − 70 = 160 − 70
  5. Answer: Breadth = 90 m
✅ Breadth of the rectangular park = 90 m
Divide the perimeter by 2 first to get (Length + Breadth). Then subtract the known length to get the breadth: 160 − 70 = 90 m.

5 Square — Finding Side from Perimeter

The perimeter of a square shape is 280 cm. Find the length of each side.

  1. Given: Perimeter = 280 cm
  2. Formula: Perimeter = 4 × side
  3. Rearrange: Side = Perimeter ÷ 4
  4. Calculate: Side = 280 ÷ 4 = 70 cm
✅ Each side of the square = 70 cm
A square has 4 equal sides. So the perimeter equals 4 times one side. To reverse this, divide the perimeter by 4.

6 Triangle — Mixed Units

Triangle ABC has: mAB = 30 mm, mBC = 50 mm, mAC = 40 mm. Find the perimeter in cm.

  1. Convert all to cm: mAB = 30 mm = 3 cm
  2. mBC = 50 mm = 5 cm (remember: 10 mm = 1 cm)
  3. mAC = 40 mm = 4 cm
  4. Perimeter: P = 3 + 5 + 4 = 12 cm
✅ Perimeter = (30 + 50 + 40) mm = 120 mm = 12 cm
Always convert to the same unit before adding! 10 mm = 1 cm. So divide mm by 10 to get cm.

Multi-Step Challenge

A rectangular playground is 150 m long and 100 m wide. A boy wants to cycle around it 5 times. What total distance will he cover?

Bonus Challenge

Think about this: Two rectangles have the same perimeter of 20 cm. One is 6 cm × 4 cm and another is 8 cm × 2 cm. Do they have the same area?

Rectangle 1: 6×4 = 24 sq. cm

Rectangle 2: 8×2 = 16 sq. cm

Conclusion: Same perimeter does NOT mean same area! The square-like shape always has a larger area for the same perimeter. This is a key mathematical insight! 🎯
