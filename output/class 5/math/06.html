🧠 Core Concept

What Is the Unitary Method?

A process by which we find the value of a single unit from the value of multiple units, and the value of multiple units from the value of a single unit.

🔍 The Big Idea

The unitary method works in two directions:

  • Many → One: Divide the total value by the number of items to find the value of ONE item.
  • One → Many: Multiply the value of one item by the number needed to find the total value.
📐 Key Formulas
Value of 1 unit = Total Value ÷ Number of Units Value of n units = Value of 1 unit × n
💡 Always start by finding the value of ONE unit, then scale to any number!

📚 The Three Cases

Types of Unitary Method Problems

The unitary method applies to three main types of problems. Master all three!

1

Finding Many from One

When given: The value of one object

To find: The value of many objects

Formula
Value of n units = Value of 1 unit × n

🎯 Example: Price of 1 box = Rs 20 → Price of 5 boxes = 20 × 5 = Rs 100

2

Finding One from Many

When given: The value of many objects

To find: The value of one object

Formula
Value of 1 unit = Total Value ÷ Number of units

🎯 Example: Price of 4 kg mangoes = Rs 400 → Price of 1 kg = 400 ÷ 4 = Rs 100

3

Finding Any Amount from a Given Amount

When given: The value of some objects

To find: The value of a different number of objects

Two-Step Formula
Step 1: Value of 1 unit = Given Total ÷ Given quantity Step 2: Value of required = Value of 1 unit × Required quantity

🎯 Example: 6 books cost Rs 180 → 1 book = Rs 30 → 14 books = 30 × 14 = Rs 420

⚖️ Ratio & Proportion

Direct and Inverse Proportion

Before solving proportion problems, let's understand ratio and the two types of proportion.

📐 Ratio

A way of comparing two or more quantities. The ratio of 8 to 2 is written as 8:2 (read as "8 is to 2").

Example: Ratio of Ibrahim's age to Ismail's age is 2:1 → Ibrahim is twice as old as Ismail.

⚖️ Proportion

An equation where two ratios are set equal to each other. Written as a:b = c:d or a:b :: c:d

Example: 4:5 = 8:10 is a proportion (4:5 :: 8:10)

Type What Happens Real Example Symbol
Direct ↑↑ One increases → Other increases More books → Pay more money ∝
💡 Direct proportion: More temperature = more heat. More energy = more work done. If you buy more, you pay more!
Direct ↓↓ One decreases → Other decreases Less apples → Pay less money ∝
💡 Both go DOWN together. Fewer items purchased means a smaller total bill.
Inverse ↑↓ One increases → Other decreases More workers → Less time to finish ∝ 1/x
💡 Inverse proportion: More speed = less travel time. More taps = less time to fill a tank. More workers = finish faster!
Inverse ↓↑ One decreases → Other increases Fewer labourers → More time to build ∝ 1/x
💡 Fewer workers means the same job takes longer. More buses on the road = less space remaining.

Direct Proportion

A man buys 4 kg of mangoes for Rs 360. How much for 9 kg?

🥭 Given: 4 kg = Rs 360 (Direct proportion) | Find: 9 kg = ?

Kg Rs Kg Rs
4 360 9 ?
1

For 4 kg of mangoes, he spends = Rs 360

2

For 1 kg of mangoes = 360 ÷ 4 = Rs 90

3

For 9 kg = 90 × 9 = Rs 810

✅ He will pay Rs 810 for 9 kg of mangoes.

Inverse Proportion

6 labourers can build a house in 10 hours. How long will 12 labourers take?

👷 Given: 6 labourers = 10 hours (Inverse proportion) | Find: 12 labourers = ?

Labourers Hours Labourers Hours
6 10 12 ?
1

6 labourers can build in = 10 hours

2

1 labourer would take = 6 × 10 = 60 hours (more time when fewer workers)

3

12 labourers take = 60 ÷ 12 = 5 hours (less time with more workers)

✅ 12 labourers will build the same house in 5 hours.
