Exploring Geometry

Discover angles, triangles, and quadrilaterals — the building blocks of the shapes around us. Learn to identify, measure, and construct geometric figures with confidence!

What is Geometry?

Geometry is the branch of mathematics that deals with shapes, sizes, and properties of figures and spaces.

📐

Angles

An angle is formed when two rays meet at a common endpoint called the vertex. The symbol for an angle is ∠.

📏

Triangles

A triangle is a closed plane figure with 3 sides, 3 vertices, and 3 angles. The sum of all angles = 180°.

🔷

Quadrilaterals

A quadrilateral is a closed figure with 4 sides and 4 angles. The sum of all angles = 360°.

Types of Angles

Angles are classified by their measure in degrees. Let's explore each type!

What is an Angle?

An angle is formed when two straight lines or rays meet at a common endpoint. The point is called the vertex. The two lines are called arms. It is written as ∠BAC and read as "angle BAC".

Acute Angle

Less than 90°

An angle whose measure is less than 90°. Example: 45°, 30°, 60°

Right Angle

Exactly 90°

An angle whose measure is exactly 90°. Indicated by a small square symbol.

Obtuse Angle

90° to 180°

An angle whose measure is greater than 90° but less than 180°. Example: 130°

Straight Angle

Exactly 180°

An angle whose measure is exactly 180°. It forms a straight line.

Reflex Angle

180° to 360°

An angle whose measure is greater than 180° but less than 360°. Example: 290°, 315°

Constructing Angles

Use a protractor to construct right angles, straight angles, and reflex angles.

🔧 Construct a Right Angle (90°)

Goal: Draw ∠AOB = 90°
  1. Draw ray OA along a horizontal line.
  2. Place the centre point of the protractor at point O along OA.
  3. Read the protractor from the side where its zero mark lies on OA. Mark point B at 90°.
  4. Draw ray OB. Thus m∠AOB = 90°.

🔧 Construct a Straight Angle (180°)

Goal: Draw ∠MON = 180°
  1. Draw ray OM along a line.
  2. Place the centre of the protractor at point O along OM.
  3. Read from the side where zero mark lies on OM. Mark point N at 180°.
  4. Draw ray ON. Thus m∠MON = 180°.

🔧 Construct a Reflex Angle (e.g., 300°)

Goal: Draw reflex ∠AOC = 300°
  1. Subtract from 360°: 360° − 300° = 60°
  2. Draw the acute angle of 60° first.
  3. The reflex angle on the other side = 300°.
  4. Label it as reflex ∠AOC = 300°.
Key Formula
Reflex angle = 360° − (its corresponding acute/obtuse angle)

Reading a Protractor:

  • Place the centre hole at the vertex of the angle.
  • Align the base line (0° line) with one arm of the angle.
  • Read the number where the other arm crosses the scale.
  • Always check which scale (inner or outer) you're reading!

Angle Pairs

Learn about adjacent, complementary, and supplementary angles.

📌

Adjacent Angles

Two angles are adjacent if they share a common vertex and a common arm, with their other arms on opposite sides of the common arm.

Example
∠ABD and ∠DBC share vertex B and arm BD.
🔵

Complementary Angles

Two angles are complementary if their sum is 90°. Each is the complement of the other.

Formula
Complement = 90° − given angle
e.g., Complement of 65° = 90° − 65° = 25°
🟡

Supplementary Angles

Two angles are supplementary if their sum is 180°. Each is the supplement of the other.

Formula
Supplement = 180° − given angle
e.g., Supplement of 140° = 180° − 140° = 40°

Constructing Triangles

Use compasses, ruler, and protractor to draw accurate triangles.

📏 Equilateral Triangle (SSS)

Example: Construct △PQR with each side = 3.8 cm
  1. Draw line segment PQ = 3.8 cm with a ruler.
  2. Set compass to 3.8 cm. Draw arc from P.
  3. Draw another arc of 3.8 cm from Q. Mark intersection as R.
  4. Draw PR and QR to complete the triangle.
Result
Each angle = 60°, all sides = 3.8 cm ✓

📐 Isosceles Triangle (SSS)

Example: △DEF where DE = DF = 4.5 cm, EF = 3.5 cm
  1. Draw EF = 3.5 cm.
  2. Set compass to 4.5 cm. Draw arc from E.
  3. Draw arc of 4.5 cm from F. Mark intersection as D.
  4. Draw DE and DF to complete the triangle.
Result
DE = DF = 4.5 cm (equal sides), ∠E = ∠F ✓

📐 Scalene Triangle (SSS)

Example: △ABC where AB = 5cm, AC = 4cm, BC = 3cm
  1. Draw AB = 5 cm.
  2. Set compass to 4 cm. Draw arc from A.
  3. Set compass to 3 cm. Draw arc from B to meet previous arc at C.
  4. Draw AC and BC to complete the triangle.
Result
All sides different — scalene triangle ✓

Using Protractor (ASA/AAS)

Right-Angled Triangle

Example: △ABC where ∠BAC=50°, ∠ABC=40°, AB = 3.5cm
  1. Draw AB = 3.5 cm.
  2. At A, draw angle BAC = 50° using protractor.
  3. At B, draw angle ABC = 40° using protractor.
  4. The arms meet at C, forming a right angle at C.
Note
50° + 40° + 90° = 180° ✓ (angle sum property)

Acute-Angled Triangle

Example: △ABC where ∠ABC=75°, AB=4.2cm, BC=3.8cm
  1. Draw AB = 4.2 cm.
  2. Using protractor, make ∠ABD = 75°.
  3. From B, draw arc of 3.8 cm to cut arm BD at C.
  4. Draw AC to complete the triangle.

Obtuse-Angled Triangle

Example: △DEF where ∠DEF=110°, DE=4.5cm, EF=4cm
  1. Draw DE = 4.5 cm.
  2. At E, draw angle DEG = 110° with protractor.
  3. With E as centre, draw arc of 4 cm to cut line EG at F.
  4. Draw DF to complete the triangle.

Hypotenuse is the longest side in a right-angled triangle. It is always opposite the right angle (90°).

Key Fact
In △ABC with ∠ABC = 90°, the hypotenuse is AC (opposite to the right angle).

Quadrilaterals

A closed figure with four sides, four angles, and four vertices. Sum of angles = 360°

Square

All 4 sides equal, opposite sides parallel, each angle = 90°

Rectangle

Opposite sides equal and parallel, each angle = 90°

Parallelogram

Opposite sides equal & parallel, opposite angles equal, no 90° angles

Rhombus

All 4 sides equal, opposite sides parallel, opposite angles equal

Trapezium

Only one pair of opposite parallel sides

Kite

Two distinct pairs of adjacent sides equal in length

Constructing a Rectangle

📐 Rectangle 5.5cm × 3.3cm (using ruler & set square)

Given: Length = 5.5 cm, Breadth = 3.3 cm
  1. Draw AB = 5.5 cm using ruler.
  2. Place both set squares on AB, perpendicular sides parallel to each other.
  3. Construct perpendicular at A, cut 3.3 cm, mark as D.
  4. Construct perpendicular at B, cut 3.3 cm, mark as C.
  5. Join CD with ruler. ABCD is the required rectangle.

Constructing a Square

📐 Square with side = 3.4 cm

Given: Side = 3.4 cm
  1. Draw PQ = 3.4 cm.
  2. At P, draw perpendicular of 3.4 cm, mark as S.
  3. At Q, draw perpendicular of 3.4 cm, mark as R.
  4. Join RS = 3.4 cm. PQRS is the required square.

🏆 Triangle Angle Sum Challenge

In △ABC, ∠A = 2x°, ∠B = (x + 20)°, and ∠C = (3x − 10)°. Find the value of x and all three angles.

Using angle sum property:

2x + (x + 20) + (3x − 10) = 180°

6x + 10 = 180°

6x = 170°

x = 28.33° (approx)

∠A = 56.67°, ∠B = 48.33°, ∠C = 75° — Acute-angled triangle

🔷 Quadrilateral Angle Challenge

In a quadrilateral ABCD, ∠A = 85°, ∠B = 110°, ∠C = 90°. Find ∠D.

Using angle sum of quadrilateral = 360°:

85° + 110° + 90° + ∠D = 360°

285° + ∠D = 360°

∠D = 75°

📐 Reflex Angle Construction

How would you construct a reflex angle of 250°? Describe the steps using the formula: Reflex = 360° − x

360° − 250° = 110° (the obtuse angle to draw first)

1. Draw a ray OA.

2. At O, draw an obtuse angle of 110° using protractor. Mark as OB.

3. The angle on the other side = 360° − 110° = 250° (reflex angle).

4. Label it as reflex ∠AOB = 250°.
