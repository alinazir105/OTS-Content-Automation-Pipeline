Core Concept

What is a Fraction?

A fraction is a part of a whole. It is written as a quotient where the numerator is divided by the denominator.

Discover: Anatomy of a Fraction

2
5

🟦 Numerator (2) — The number on top. Shows how many parts we have.

📏 Fraction Bar — Means "divided by".

🟧 Denominator (5) — The number below. Shows the total equal parts.

2 out of 5 parts = ²⁄₅

📐

Proper Fraction

The numerator is less than the denominator.
Example: ³⁄₅, ¹⁄₄, ²⁄₇

📊

Improper Fraction

The numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator.
Example: ⁷⁄₄, ⁹⁄₅

🔢

Mixed Number

A whole number combined with a proper fraction.
Example: 2²⁄₃, 3¹⁄₄, 1⁵⁄₈

⚖️

Equivalent Fractions

Fractions that have the same value but different numerators and denominators.
Example: ¹⁄₂ = ²⁄₄ = ⁴⁄₈

Operation 1

Adding Fractions

To add fractions with different denominators, we first find the LCM (Lowest Common Multiple) of the denominators, then create equivalent fractions.

Addition Formula
a/b + c/d = (a×d + c×b) / (b×d)
Find LCM of denominators → Convert → Add numerators

📘 Example 1: Add ²⁄₅ and ¹⁄₄

1
Find LCM of 5 and 4.
5 = 5 × 1   |   4 = 2 × 2
LCM = 2 × 2 × 5 = 20
2
Convert fractions to equivalent fractions with denominator 20:
²⁄₅ = 8/20    ¹⁄₄ = 5/20
3
Apply the formula:
²⁄₅ + ¹⁄₄ = (2×4) + (1×5) / 20 = (8 + 5) / 20
²⁄₅ + ¹⁄₄ = 13/20 ✓

📘 Example 2: Add ⁵⁄₆ + ⁴⁄₅

1
LCM of 6 and 5 = 30
2
⁵⁄₆ + ⁴⁄₅ = (5×5 + 4×6) / 30 = (25 + 24) / 30 = 49/30
⁵⁄₆ + ⁴⁄₅ = 49/30 = 1 19/30 ✓

📘 Example 3: Add fractions with same denominator — ⁷⁄₉ + ²⁄₉ + ⁵⁄₉

1
Same denominator: just add the numerators!
2
⁷⁄₉ + ²⁄₉ + ⁵⁄₉ = (7 + 2 + 5) / 9 = 14/9
= 14/9 = 1⁵⁄₉ ✓

Operation 2

Subtracting Fractions

Subtraction follows the same LCM process as addition — find a common denominator, then subtract the numerators.

Subtraction Formula
a/b − c/d = (a×d − c×b) / (b×d)
Find LCM → Convert → Subtract numerators

📘 Example 1: Subtract ²⁄₇ from ¹⁄₃

1
Find LCM of 3 and 7: LCM = 3 × 7 = 21
2
¹⁄₃ − ²⁄₇ = (1×7) − (2×3) / 21 = (7 − 6) / 21
¹⁄₃ − ²⁄₇ = 1/21 ✓

📘 Example 2: Subtract fractions with same denominator — ⁶⁄₇ − ⁵⁄₇

1
Same denominator — just subtract numerators!
⁶⁄₇ − ⁵⁄₇ = (6 − 5) / 7 = 1/7
⁶⁄₇ − ⁵⁄₇ = ¹⁄₇ ✓

📘 Example 3: Chain Subtraction — ²³⁄₂₄ − ¹⁄₄ − ²⁄₁₂

1
LCM of 24, 4, 12 = 24
2
²³⁄₂₄ − ¹⁄₄ − ²⁄₁₂ = 23/24 − 6/24 − 4/24 = (23 − 6 − 4)/24 = 13/24
= 13/24 ✓
Operation 3

Multiplying Fractions

Multiplication of fractions is the simplest operation — multiply numerators together and denominators together, then simplify!

Multiplication Formula
a/b × c/d = (a×c) / (b×d)
No LCM needed! Multiply straight across, then simplify.

📘 Example 1: ¹⁄₂ × ⁴⁄₅

1
¹⁄₂ × ⁴⁄₅ = (1×4) / (2×5) = 4/10
2
Simplify: 4/10 = 2/5 (divide both by 2)
= ²⁄₅ ✓

📘 Example 2: 2²⁄₃ × ⁹⁄₄

1
Convert mixed number: 2²⁄₃ = 8/3
2
⁸⁄₃ × ⁹⁄₄ = (8×9)/(3×4) = 72/12 = 6
= 6 ✓

📘 Example 3: Three Fractions — ⁶⁄₅ × ²⁵⁄₈ × ¹⁄₃

1
Multiply all numerators: 6 × 25 × 1 = 150
Multiply all denominators: 5 × 8 × 3 = 120
2
Cancel common factors: 6/5 × 25/8 × 1/3
= (6÷6 × 25÷5 × 1) / (5÷5 × 8÷2 × 3÷3) = (1 × 5 × 1)/(1 × 4 × 1) = 5/4
= 5/4 = 1¼ ✓

📘 Example 4: Fraction × Whole Number — ³⁄₄ × 4

1
Write whole number as fraction: 4 = 4/1
³⁄₄ × 4/1 = (3×4)/(4×1) = 12/4 = 3
³⁄₄ × 4 = 3 ✓
Operation 4

Dividing Fractions

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal (flip the second fraction upside down)!

Division Rule — Keep, Change, Flip!
a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c
KEEP the first fraction → CHANGE ÷ to × → FLIP the second fraction

BODMAS Rule with Fractions

When fractions appear in complex expressions, follow the BODMAS order of operations to solve correctly.

B
Brackets
Solve ( ), then { }, then [ ] first
O
Of / Orders
Powers and roots
D
Division
Left to right
M
Multiplication
Left to right
A
Addition
After × and ÷
S
Subtraction
Last in sequence

📘 BODMAS Example: ⁵⁄₃ × (1¹⁄₃ − ¹⁄₂) ÷ ⁵⁄₂

B
Solve the bracket first: 1¹⁄₃ − ¹⁄₂ = ⁴⁄₃ − ¹⁄₂
LCM of 3 and 2 = 6 → (8−3)/6 = 5/6
×
Multiply: ⁵⁄₃ × ⁵⁄₆
= (5×5)/(3×6) = 25/18
÷
Divide: 25/18 ÷ 5/2 = 25/18 × 2/5
= (25×2)/(18×5) = 50/90 = 5/9
Final Answer = 5/9 ✓

📘 Nested Brackets: ((³⁄₄ − ³⁄₈) × ⁷⁄₉) ÷ (⁵⁄₆ + ¹⁄₃ + ¹⁄₂)

1
Inner bracket: ³⁄₄ − ³⁄₈ = ⁶⁄₈ − ³⁄₈ = ³⁄₈
2
Multiply: ³⁄₈ × ⁷⁄₉ = 21/72 = 7/24
3
Second bracket: ⁵⁄₆ + ¹⁄₃ + ¹⁄₂, LCM=6 → 5/6 + 2/6 + 3/6 = 10/6 = 5/3
4
Divide: 7/24 ÷ 5/3 = 7/24 × 3/5 = 21/120 = 7/40
= 7/40 ✓
Real World

Word Problems

Apply fraction operations to solve real-life problems. Click "Show Solution" to reveal the working.

1

🚪 Door Height Problem

The height of a door is 2⁵⁄₄ metres. Out of this, ¹⁄₈ of the part was trimmed off. How much was trimmed off?

Height of door = 2⁵⁄₄ = 13/4 metres
Part trimmed = ¹⁄₈ of 13/4
= ¹⁄₈ × 13/4
= 13/32 metres
∴ 13/32 metres was trimmed off.
2

🏫 Students on Leave

On Monday, one-tenth of the total number of students were on leave. If the total number of students enrolled is 50, how many students were present?

Total students = 50
On leave = ¹⁄₁₀ × 50 = 5 students
Students present = 50 − 5 = 45
∴ 45 students were present.
3

🏥 Hospital Drips

A surgeon advised an operating room technician to arrange 12 drips of dextrose solution. The hospital has only ⁵⁄₄ of the required dextrose. How many drips are available?

Required drips = 12
Available = ⁵⁄₄ × 12
= (5 × 12) / 4
= 60/4 = 15 drips
∴ 15 drips are available in the hospital.
4

🔌 Wire Length

A piece of wire is 8¹⁄₃ metres long. Find the total length of wire if there are 13¹⁄₅ such small pieces.

Length of one piece = 8¹⁄₃ = 25/3 metres
Number of pieces = 13¹⁄₅ = 66/5
Total length = 25/3 × 66/5
= (25 × 66) / (3 × 5)
= 1650/15
= 110 metres
∴ Total length = 110 metres.
5

🍅 Cost of Tomatoes

The cost of 1 kg of tomatoes is Rs 45. Find the cost of 13⁵⁄₄ kg of tomatoes.

Cost per kg = Rs 45
Weight = 13⁵⁄₄ = 57/4 kg
Total cost = 45 × 57/4
= 2565/4
= Rs 641.25
∴ Cost of 13⁵⁄₄ kg tomatoes = Rs 641.25

📋 Fraction Operations Quick Reference Table

Operation Rule Example Answer Key Point
Addition (+) Find LCM, convert, add numerators ²⁄₅ + ¹⁄₄ 13/20 Need common denominator
💡 Always find the LCM of the denominators before adding. Then multiply each numerator by the factor needed to reach the LCM.
Subtraction (−) Find LCM, convert, subtract numerators ¹⁄₃ − ²⁄₇ 1/21 Same as addition process
💡 The process is identical to addition, but you subtract the numerators at the end instead of adding.
Multiplication (×) Multiply numerators × numerators, denominators × denominators ¹⁄₂ × ⁴⁄₅ 2/5 No LCM needed!
💡 You can cross-cancel before multiplying to keep numbers smaller. Simplify the final answer.
Division (÷) Multiply by reciprocal of divisor ⁹⁄₁₆ ÷ ³⁄₄ 3/4 Keep, Change, Flip!
💡 The reciprocal of ³⁄₄ is ⁴⁄₃. Then you simply multiply as normal.
Mixed Numbers Convert to improper fraction first 2²⁄₃ = 8/3 (2×3+2)/3 Whole × denom + numer
💡 Formula: a b/c = (a×c + b)/c. Always convert mixed numbers before any operation.
BODMAS Brackets → Division → Multiplication → Addition → Subtraction ⁵⁄₃ × (1¹⁄₃ − ¹⁄₂) ÷ ⁵⁄₂ 5/9 Always solve brackets first
💡 BODMAS applies to fractions just like whole numbers. Work from left to right after handling brackets.

🔥 Challenge 3: Create Your Own!

A baker uses ³⁄₄ cup of sugar for one cake. How many cups does she need to bake 2⁴⁄₅ dozen cakes (where 1 dozen = 12)?

Number of cakes = 2⁴⁄₅ × 12 = 14/5 × 12 = 168/5
Sugar needed = 3/4 × 168/5 = 504/20 = 126/5 = 25.2 cups
∴ 126/5 = 25¹⁄₅ cups of sugar needed.
