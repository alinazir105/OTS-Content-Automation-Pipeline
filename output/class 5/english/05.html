Background

What is an Adjective?

Definition

An adjective is a word that describes or modifies a noun or pronoun. It tells us about qualities, size, colour, shape, quantity, or condition.

Spot the Adjectives!

In the sentence "The tall, blue-eyed boy carried a heavy red bag," the words tall, blue, heavy, and red are all adjectives describing the nouns.

Type What It Tells Us Example Sentence Adjective
Quality The nature or feature of something The cake is delicious. delicious
Size How big or small something is She has a small bag. small
Colour The colour of something He wore a blue shirt. blue
Quantity How many or how much There are three chairs here. three
Shape The form or outline of something The table is round. round
Condition The state or condition of something The car is broken. broken

Core Concept

Degrees of Adjectives

Adjectives have three degrees that allow us to compare people, things, and ideas.

🌸

Positive Degree

The base form of the adjective. No comparison is made.

"This flower is pretty."
⚖️

Comparative Degree

Used to compare two things or people.

"This flower is prettier than that one."
🏆

Superlative Degree

Used to compare three or more things or people.

"This is the prettiest flower in the garden."

📌 Key Rule

Use comparative when comparing exactly two things. Use superlative when comparing three or more things — or to say something is the most extreme in its group.


🔸 "My brother is taller than me." (two people → comparative)

🔸 "She is the tallest in the class." (whole group → superlative)

Forming Degrees

How to Form Comparative & Superlative

There are four main patterns depending on the type of adjective.

Quick Reference Formula
Short adjectives → add -er (comparative) | -est (superlative) Adjectives ending in -y → change y to -ier | -iest Long adjectives → use more ... (comparative) | most ... (superlative) Irregular adjectives → completely new word form (bad → worse → worst)
Short Adjectives

Add -er / -est

fast → faster → fastest
tall → taller → tallest
big → bigger → biggest
  • This car is fast.
  • That car is faster than mine.
  • The red car is the fastest of all.
Adjectives ending in -y

Change -y to -ier / -iest

easy → easier → easiest
happy → happier → happiest
kind → kinder → kindest
  • This test is easy.
  • That test was easier than the previous one.
  • The last test was the easiest of all.
Long Adjectives

Use more / most

expensive → more expensive
→ most expensive
interesting → more interesting
  • This watch is expensive.
  • That one is more expensive than mine.
  • This is the most expensive watch in the store.
Irregular Adjectives

Completely Change Form

good → better → best
bad → worse → worst
little → less → least
  • His handwriting is bad.
  • His handwriting is worse than mine.
  • This is the worst handwriting I've ever seen.
Worked Examples

Exercise 1: Worked Examples

Let's walk through the exercises from the textbook step by step. Click the hint button for guidance before revealing the answer.

1 The book is the ___ in the library.
(interesting / more interesting / most interesting)
We're talking about the book among ALL books in the library — comparing 3 or more → use superlative with a long adjective → "most interesting"
2 My brother is ___ than me.
(tall / taller / tallest)
The sentence compares two people (brother and me) and uses "than" → comparative → "taller"
3 This is the ___ game I've ever played.
(exciting / more exciting / most exciting)
"I've ever played" implies comparing to all games ever → superlative. "Exciting" is a long adjective → use "most"
4 She is ___ than her sister.
(kind / kinder / kindest)
Two people compared + "than" → comparative. "Kind" is short → add -er → "kinder"
5 The elephant is ___ than the horse.
(big / bigger / biggest)
Two animals compared + "than" → comparative. "Big" ends in a consonant-vowel-consonant → double the g → "bigger"
6 He is the ___ student in class.
(intelligent / more intelligent / most intelligent)
Comparing to the whole class (many students) → superlative. "Intelligent" is long → use "most"
7 My car is ___ than yours.
(fast / faster / fastest)
Two cars compared + "than" → comparative. "Fast" is short → add -er → "faster"
8 The story is ___ than the movie.
(boring / more boring / most boring)
Two things compared + "than" → comparative. "Boring" is a longer adjective → use "more"
9 This puzzle is ___ to solve.
(easy / easier / easiest)
No comparison is being made here — just describing the puzzle → positive degree → "easy"
10 She feels ___ today than yesterday.
(happy / happier / happiest)
Comparing two time periods + "than" → comparative. "Happy" ends in -y → change to -ier → "happier"
