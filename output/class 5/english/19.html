Introduction

What is a Poem?

A poem is a special type of writing that uses beautiful words, rhythm, and sometimes rhyme to express feelings, ideas, and images. Unlike stories, poems are often short but full of meaning.

🎯 In this unit you will study 3 poems:
  • Wind on the Hill by A.A. Milne
  • The Rainbow by Christina Rossetti
  • The Swing by Robert Louis Stevenson

Key Poetry Terms

TermMeaning
StanzaA group of lines in a poem (like a paragraph)
RhymeWords that end with the same sound
RhythmThe musical beat or pattern of syllables
PoetA person who writes poems
ImageryWord pictures that help you imagine

Poem 1

Wind on the Hill

📝
About the Poet

A.A. Milne (Alan Alexander Milne)

A.A. Milne was a famous English poet and author, born on January 18, 1882, in London, England. He is best known for writing the Winnie-the-Pooh stories, but he was also a poet who wrote beautiful and simple poems for children.

Wind on the Hill

by A.A. Milne
No one can tell me, Nobody knows, Where the wind comes from, Where the wind goes.
It's flying from somewhere As fast as it can, I couldn't keep up with it, Not if I ran.
But if I stopped holding The string of my kite, It would blow with the wind For a day and a night.

📖 Poem Analysis

Theme: The mystery and power of the wind — it is so fast and free that nobody can say where it comes from or where it goes.

Stanza 1: The poet says the wind's origin is a complete mystery — nobody knows where it comes from or where it is going.

Stanza 2: The wind moves so fast that even if the poet ran as quickly as possible, he could not keep up.

Stanza 3: If the poet let go of his kite's string, it would be carried away by the wind for a whole day and night.

🎵 Rhyme Scheme

LineWords That Rhyme
Stanza 1knows / goes
Stanza 2can / ran
Stanza 3kite / night
The kite in the poem is a symbol. When the poet says he is "holding the string," it means he is keeping the kite (and himself) grounded. Letting go means allowing something to fly freely with the wind — just like the wind itself is free!

📝 Short-Answer Questions – Wind on the Hill

Q1. What will happen if the poet stops holding the string of his kite?
If the poet stops holding the string of his kite, it will blow with the wind for a day and a night — the kite will fly away freely, carried by the wind.
Q2. Can anyone tell the poet from where the wind comes?
No, nobody can tell the poet where the wind comes from. The poem says "No one can tell me, Nobody knows" — the wind's origin is a complete mystery.

Poem 2

The Rainbow

🌸
About the Poet

Christina Rossetti (1830–1894)

Christina Rossetti was a famous English poet who lived in the 19th century. She is known for her beautiful poems, many of which are about love, faith, and nature. She was part of a family that loved art and poetry, and her brother, Dante Gabriel Rossetti, was also a famous poet and artist.

The Rainbow

by Christina Rossetti
Boats sail on the rivers, And ships sail on the seas; But clouds that sail across the sky Are prettier far than these.
There are bridges on the rivers, As pretty as you please; But the bow that bridges heaven, And overtops the trees, And builds a road from earth to sky, Is prettier far than these.

📖 Poem Analysis

Theme: The beauty of nature — specifically how the rainbow (called "the bow") is more beautiful than anything humans have made, like boats, ships, or bridges.

Stanza 1: Boats sail on rivers, ships on seas — but the clouds in the sky are even prettier than both of these.

Stanza 2: There are bridges on rivers that are pretty, but the rainbow ("the bow") that stretches from earth to sky is the most beautiful of all.

🌈 Comparing Man-Made vs Nature

Man-MadeNatural (More Beautiful!)
Boats on riversClouds in the sky
Ships on seasRainbow ("the bow")
Bridges on riversRainbow bridges heaven
"The bow" in the poem refers to the rainbow. Rossetti describes it as a bridge that connects earth to sky — a beautiful arch of colors stretching across heaven, more gorgeous than any bridge built by people!

📝 Short-Answer Questions – The Rainbow

Q1. What according to the poet is prettier than boats and ships?
According to the poet, clouds that sail across the sky are prettier than boats and ships. And ultimately, the rainbow is the most beautiful of all.
Q2. What is the bridge that connects Earth with sky?
The rainbow (called "the bow" in the poem) is the bridge that connects Earth with sky. It "builds a road from earth to sky" and "bridges heaven."
Q3. What according to the poet sails in the sky?
Clouds sail in the sky according to the poet. She says "clouds that sail across the sky are prettier far than these" — comparing them to boats and ships.

1) Christina Rossetti was a Pakistani poet.
2) Rainbow is prettier than bridges on the rivers.
3) Ships sail on the seas.
Poem 3

The Swing

⛵
About the Poet

Robert Louis Stevenson (1850–1894)

Robert Louis Stevenson was a famous Scottish writer, born on November 13, 1850, in Edinburgh, Scotland. He wrote exciting adventure stories like "Treasure Island" (about pirates) and "Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde" (about a man with two personalities). Despite being sick for much of his life, he kept writing until he passed away in 1894.

The Swing

by Robert Louis Stevenson
How do you like to go up in a swing, Up in the air so blue? Oh, I do think it the pleasantest thing Ever a child can do!
Up in the air and over the wall, Till I can see so wide, Rivers and trees and cattle and all Over the countryside—
Till I look down on the garden green, Down on the roof so brown— Up in the air I go flying again, Up in the air and down!

📖 Poem Analysis

Theme: The joy and freedom of childhood — swinging feels like flying and lets you see the whole world from up high.

Stanza 1: The child thinks swinging is the most pleasurable thing a child can do — it feels like being up in a beautiful blue sky.

Stanza 2: As the child swings high — over the wall — they can see rivers, trees, cattle, and the whole countryside stretching below.

Stanza 3: Coming back down, the child sees the green garden and the brown roof — then goes flying up again!

👁️ What the Child Sees

DirectionWhat is seen
Looking farRivers, trees, cattle, countryside
Looking downGarden (green), roof (brown)
Looking upAir (blue sky)
"Pleasantest" means most pleasant — most enjoyable. The poet uses this word to emphasize that for a child, nothing compares to the joy of swinging. The feeling of going up and down, with the wind rushing past, is absolutely delightful!

📝 Short-Answer Questions – The Swing

Q1. What is the main activity described in the poem?
The main activity described in the poem is swinging on a swing. The child goes up in the air and comes back down, feeling the joy of flying.
Q2. How does the child feel about going up in the swing?
The child feels very happy and excited. The poet calls it "the pleasantest thing ever a child can do" — meaning it is the most enjoyable activity possible!
Q3. What things can the child see when he goes high in the air?
When high up, the child can see rivers, trees, cattle, and the whole countryside stretching out below.
Q4. What does the child see when he looks down from the swing?
When the child looks down, he sees the garden (green) and the roof (brown) of the house below.
Q5. How does the child describe the garden and the roof from the swing?
The child describes the garden as green and the roof as brown — these colors are seen from high up on the swing.
Q6. What do you think the child likes about swinging in the poem?
The child loves the feeling of freedom and flying — going high up in the air and seeing the beautiful wide world below. It makes the child feel as though they are truly soaring through the sky!
Q7. How does the poet describe the swing in the poem?
The poet describes the swing as something magical — it takes the child up in the air, over the wall, letting the child see rivers and countryside, and then bringing them back down again in a beautiful, rhythmic motion.
