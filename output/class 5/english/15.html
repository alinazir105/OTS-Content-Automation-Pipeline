Background

What Are Punctuation Marks?

Punctuation marks help us organize our writing and make it easier to understand. They guide the reader on where to pause, stop, or emphasize certain parts of a sentence.

Why Do Punctuation Marks Matter?

Consider these two sentences — the punctuation completely changes the meaning!

Version A

"Let's eat, Grandma!"

An invitation to Grandma to eat

Version B

"Let's eat Grandma!"

Something much more alarming! 😱

Core Concepts

The Six Key Punctuation Marks

Let's explore each punctuation mark in detail with its use and examples.

.
1. Full Stop (.)
📌 Use: To end a sentence.
  • I like reading books.
  • The cat is sleeping.
  • She is coming tomorrow.
,
2. Comma (,)
📌 Use: To separate items in a list or clauses in a sentence.
  • I bought apples, bananas, oranges, and grapes.
  • If it rains, we will stay inside.
?
3. Question Mark (?)
📌 Use: To ask a question.
  • What is your name?
  • Can you help me?
  • Where is the library?
!
4. Exclamation Mark (!)
📌 Use: To show strong feelings like excitement, surprise, or anger.
  • Wow! That's amazing!
  • Be careful!
  • What a beautiful day!
'
5. Apostrophe (')
📌 Use: To show possession or a contraction.
  • Possession: This is Sarah's book.
  • Contraction: It's (it is) raining outside.
""
6. Inverted Commas ("")
📌 Use: To show a direct quote or to emphasize something.
  • Quote: She said, "How are you?"
  • Emphasis: The politician said his opponent was "corrupt".

Worked Examples

Step-by-Step Solutions

Let's work through the exercises from the textbook together, understanding why each punctuation mark is used.

Step 1: Read the sentence aloud. Does it end in a natural falling tone? → Full Stop
Step 2: Does your voice rise at the end? → Question Mark
Step 3: Is there strong emotion or urgency? → Exclamation Mark
Step 4: Do you pause naturally in the middle of a list? → Comma
Step 5: Is there ownership (belonging)? → Apostrophe + s
Step 6: Is it a shortened word? → Apostrophe (contraction)

1

What is your name___

This sentence is asking a question. The voice rises at the end.
Answer: What is your name? → Question Mark
2

I love reading books___

This is a statement — a fact being shared. No question, no strong emotion.
Answer: I love reading books. → Full Stop
3

Wow___ That's an amazing idea___

Both parts express excitement and strong feelings. "Wow" is an exclamation word.
Answer: Wow! That's an amazing idea! → Exclamation Marks
4

Please bring apples___ bananas___ and oranges from the market___

This is a list of three items. Commas separate each item, and the sentence ends with a full stop.
Answer: Please bring apples, bananas, and oranges from the market.
5

This is Sarah_s jacket___

The jacket belongs to Sarah (possession). The blank between "Sarah" and "s" needs an apostrophe.
Answer: This is Sarah's jacket. → Apostrophe (possession) + Full Stop
8

This is Ali_s notebook and it_s new___

Two apostrophes here: "Ali's" = possession, "it's" = contraction of "it is".
Answer: This is Ali's notebook and it's new.
9

The children went to the park and played football___ cricket___ and hide and seek___

Three activities in a list. Commas after each item except the last, then a full stop.
Answer: ...played football, cricket, and hide and seek.

✅ Model Answer:

My name is Sana. I have a pet cat. Her name is Fluffy. She likes to play with a ball. She eats fish, milk, and bread. Do you have a pet? Wow! Pets are so cute. One day Fluffy ran out of the house. "Where is she?" I shouted.

📋 Punctuation used:
• Full stops — to end each statement sentence
• Commas — to separate items in the list (fish, milk, and bread)
• Question mark — for "Do you have a pet?" and "Where is she?"
• Exclamation mark — for "Wow!"
• Inverted commas — for the spoken dialogue
• Capital letters — at the start of each sentence and for names (Sana, Fluffy)
