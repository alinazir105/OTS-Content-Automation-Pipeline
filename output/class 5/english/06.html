Core Concept

What is an Adverb?

An adverb is a word that describes or modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb. It gives us more information about how, when, where, how often, or to what degree something happens.

Discovery Zone

Look at these two sentences. Which one gives you more detail?


🔵 "The girl ran."

🟢 "The girl ran quickly."


The word quickly is an adverb — it tells us how the girl ran. Adverbs make our writing more vivid and precise!

DEFINITION
Adverb → modifies [Verb | Adjective | Another Adverb]
Tells → HOW | WHEN | WHERE | HOW OFTEN | TO WHAT DEGREE

Fun Facts

Did You Know?

Hover or tap the cards to flip them and discover amazing facts about adverbs!

🤔

Adverb Origins

Tap to reveal!

The word "adverb" comes from Latin adverbium, meaning "added to the verb". The prefix ad- means "to" or "towards"!

💡

-ly Ending

Tap to reveal!

Many adverbs are formed by adding -ly to adjectives! Slow → slowly, Happy → happily, Loud → loudly.

🎯

Adverbs vs Adjectives

Tap to reveal!

Adjectives describe nouns. Adverbs describe verbs, adjectives, or adverbs. "She is a quick runner" vs "She runs quickly."

📝

Tricky Words

Tap to reveal!

Some words like fast, hard, and late can be both adjectives AND adverbs: "He is a fast runner" / "He runs fast."

Exercise 1

Complete the Sentences

Fill in the blanks with a suitable adverb of the given type. Then click "Check Answers" to see how you did!

1
The baby cried because it was hungry.
(Adverb of Manner)
Think of how a baby cries — is it loud or soft? Look for a manner adverb like "loudly".
2
They will leave for the airport .
(Adverb of Time)
This tells WHEN they will leave. Try: today, tomorrow, soon, now, later.
3
She studies before her exams.
(Adverb of Frequency)
This tells HOW OFTEN she studies. Try: always, often, sometimes, never, usually.
4
He spoke during the presentation.
(Adverb of Manner)
How did he speak during the presentation? Try: confidently, politely, clearly, loudly, slowly.
5
The children played in the garden.
(Adverb of Place)
This tells WHERE they played. The sentence already says "in the garden" so think of: there, here, outside, nearby.
6
We are excited for the trip tomorrow.
(Adverb of Degree)
This tells TO WHAT EXTENT we are excited. Try: very, extremely, quite, really, so.
7
The cat walked across the room.
(Adverb of Manner)
How did the cat walk? Try: softly, quietly, gracefully, slowly, carefully.
8
She will arrive for the meeting.
(Adverb of Time)
When will she arrive? Try: soon, early, late, tomorrow, now.
9
He is tired to continue.
(Adverb of Degree)
This tells the extent of his tiredness. The sentence structure "_____ tired to continue" suggests: too.
10
She visits her grandparents on weekends.
(Adverb of Frequency)
How often does she visit? Try: always, often, sometimes, regularly, frequently.
