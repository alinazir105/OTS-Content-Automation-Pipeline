Introduction

What Are Modal Verbs?

Modal verbs are special helper verbs that add meaning to our sentences. They tell us about ability, permission, obligation, and advice.

🌟 Key Definition

A modal is a special verb we use to express ability, permission, obligation (=duty), and similar expressions. We can also call them "Defective verbs" as they don't have their third forms.

Important Rule:
Subject + Modal Verb + Base Verb (without "to")
❌ She can to swim.   ✅ She can swim.
⚠️ Two modal verbs CANNOT be used together!
❌ I will can go tomorrow.   ✅ I can go tomorrow.

CAN

Present ability & informal permission

COULD

Past ability & polite requests

SHALL

Future suggestions & offers

SHOULD

Advice & mild obligation

MAY

Formal permission & possibility

MIGHT

Hesitant permission & possibility

WOULD

Polite requests & conditionals

MUST

Strong obligation & deduction

Did You Know?

Modal verbs NEVER change form! We don't say "cans", "coulded", or "shoulded" — they stay the same for all subjects!

🤔

Did You Know?

Click to reveal!

Modal verbs are called "defective verbs" because they don't have all the forms normal verbs have — no infinitive, no -ing form, no past participle!

📚

Did You Know?

Click to reveal!

"Could" is the past form of "can", and "might" is the past form of "may" — they are called past tense modals but often used in the present for politeness!

Core Concept

Can & Could — Expressing Ability

💪

CAN — Present / Future Ability

Used to express ability right now or in general. Something someone is able to do at the present moment or regularly.

⏪

COULD — Past Ability

"Could" is the past form of "can." Used to express ability in the past. Also used for polite requests in the present.

Modal Time Use Example
Can Present / Future Present ability "I can swim."
Can Future Future ability "I can finish by tomorrow."
Could Past Past ability "I could run faster when young."
Could Present Polite request "Could you pass the salt?"
Worked Examples

Can & Could in Action

✅ Present Ability

I can speak three languages.

✅ Future Ability

I can finish the project by tomorrow.

✅ Past Ability

When I was younger, I could run faster.

✅ Polite Request

Could you please pass me the salt?

✅ Past Ability (Age)

I could swim when I was 10 years old.

Use CAN when: talking about what you are able to do now or regularly. Example: "She can paint beautiful pictures."

Use COULD when: talking about what you were able to do in the past OR making a very polite request. Example: "He could ride a bicycle at age 5." / "Could you help me, please?"

⚠️ Remember: After a modal verb, NEVER use "to" before the main verb. Say "I can swim" NOT "I can to swim."

Core Concept

Modal Verbs for Permission

Modal verbs are commonly used to ask for, give, and refuse permission in both the present and past.

🙋 Asking Permission

  • CAN (informal): Can I go to the restroom?
  • MAY (formal): May I leave early today?
  • COULD (polite): Could you lend me your pen?
  • MIGHT (hesitant): Might I borrow your car?

✅ Giving Permission

  • CAN (informal): You can take the day off.
  • MAY (formal): You may leave now.
  • COULD (past): You could use my laptop.
  • WAS/WERE ALLOWED TO (past occasion): They were allowed to attend.

🚫 Refusing Permission

  • CAN'T (informal): You can't go to the party.
  • MAY NOT (formal): You may not leave early.
  • COULDN'T (past): You couldn't take the car last night.
  • WASN'T ALLOWED TO: I wasn't allowed to leave early.
Function Present (Informal) Present (Formal) Past
Ask Can I...? May I...? Could I...? / Might I...?
Give You can... You may... You could... / Was allowed to
Refuse You can't... You may not... You couldn't... / Wasn't allowed to
🔍

General vs. Specific Past Permission

COULD — General Past Permission

Things permitted at any time in the past:

"When I was a child, I could visit the park anytime."

WAS/WERE ALLOWED TO — One Occasion

Permission for one specific occasion:

"Yesterday, I was allowed to leave school early."

Core Concept

Should — Obligation & Advice

📋 SHOULD

Should is used for giving advice and suggesting the best action to take. It expresses a mild obligation — something that is a good idea to do.

💊

Advice

You should drink water every day.
He should study for the test to do well.

🥦

Healthy Recommendations

We should eat healthy food.
You should exercise regularly.

🚫

SHOULDN'T — Negative Advice

You shouldn't stay up too late.
He shouldn't play in the rain.

Structure with SHOULD:
✅ Positive: Subject + should + base verb
   → "You should sleep early."

✅ Negative: Subject + shouldn't + base verb
   → "You shouldn't eat too much sugar."

✅ Question: Should + subject + base verb?
   → "Should I go to the doctor?"

❌ WRONG: You should to study. / Should I going?

Exercise 3: Correct the Sentences

Find the mistake in each sentence and write the correct version in the box below.

1) I can run a marathon when I was 18.

2) Could you fixing the computer?

3) She can play the violin when she was younger.

4) I could speak English fluently when I was a child.

1) ❌ Wrong modal → ✅ "I could run a marathon when I was 18." (past time = could)

2) ❌ "fixing" wrong → ✅ "Could you fix the computer?" (modal + base verb)

4) ✅ CORRECT — no mistake!

5) ❌ "to drive" wrong → ✅ "He can drive very well." (no "to" after modal)

Exercise 4: Should — Find & Correct Mistakes

1) You should to study for your test.

2) We should forget to bring our books tomorrow.

3) Should I going to the party tonight?

4) He shouldn't playing in the rain.

5) You should watched TV after homework.

1) Remove "to" → ✅ "You should study for your test."

2) Wrong meaning → ✅ "We should not forget (shouldn't forget) to bring our books."

3) "going" wrong → ✅ "Should I go to the party tonight?"

4) "playing" wrong → ✅ "He shouldn't play in the rain."

5) "watched" wrong → ✅ "You should watch TV after homework."
