📖 Background

What Are Conjunctions?

A conjunction is a linking word that connects words, phrases, or sentences together. Conjunctions make sentences smoother and easier to understand.

Why Do We Need Conjunctions?

Without conjunctions, every idea would need its own short sentence. That would sound choppy and boring! Conjunctions let us join ideas naturally, just like bridges connect two places.

Definition
A conjunction = a LINKING word that CONNECTS words / phrases / sentences

Sentence A + [CONJUNCTION] + Sentence B → One smooth sentence
💡

Did You Know?

Click to reveal!

The word "conjunction" comes from the Latin conjungere, meaning "to join together." Even the word itself tells you what it does!

🔗

Fun Fact

Click to reveal!

Coordinating conjunctions like "and," "but," and "or" are some of the most used words in the English language — they appear in almost every paragraph!

✍️

Writing Tip

Click to reveal!

Good writers vary their conjunctions! Using only "and" becomes repetitive. Try "although," "unless," or "while" for richer, more interesting sentences.

📋 Core Concept

Types & Uses of Conjunctions

There are 10 key conjunctions to master. Each has a specific use that changes the meaning of a sentence. Study the table below — click any row to see more details!

Conjunction Use / Function Example Sentence
and Adds similar ideas or combines things I like apples and oranges.
but Shows contrast or difference It was sunny but it was cold.
or Offers a choice between options Would you like tea or coffee?
because Explains the reason for something I stayed home because it was raining.
so Shows the result of something It was raining so I stayed home.
when Refers to time or a specific moment Call me when you reach school.
while Two actions happening at the same time She sang while he played guitar.
although Shows contrast despite a condition Although it was late, I stayed awake.
unless Indicates a condition (if not) You cannot go out unless you finish homework.
yet Adds contrast similar to "but" It was late, yet he kept working.

💡 Worked Examples

Conjunctions in Action

Let's look at how each conjunction changes the meaning and relationship between ideas.

I like apples and oranges.
🔗 AND — Joins two similar ideas (both are fruits you like). Both parts are equally true.
It was sunny but it was cold.
⚡ BUT — Shows contrast. We expect sunny weather to be warm — the "but" surprises us with "cold."
Would you like tea or coffee?
🔀 OR — Offers a choice. You can pick one option OR the other, but not necessarily both.
I stayed home because it was raining.
❓→ BECAUSE — Explains the reason. The rain is WHY I stayed home. It answers "Why did you stay home?"
It was raining, so I stayed home.
➡️ SO — Shows the result. The rain caused me to stay home. Notice: same facts, but "so" shows the outcome!
Call me when you reach school.
⏰ WHEN — Connects a time point. The calling should happen at the moment of arriving at school.
She was singing while he was playing the guitar.
⏱️ WHILE — Two things happening at the same time simultaneously. Both actions overlap.
Although it was late, I stayed awake.
🌙 ALTHOUGH — Despite a condition. Being late would normally mean sleeping, but I surprised everyone!
You cannot go out unless you finish homework.
🚫 UNLESS — Sets a condition meaning "if not." Homework must be done FIRST; otherwise, no going out.
It was late, yet he kept working.
💪 YET — Similar to "but," slightly more formal. Shows something admirable/surprising happening despite circumstances.

⚠️ Because vs. So — What's the Difference?

These two are often confused! Here's the key:

BECAUSE → explains the reason
"I stayed home because it was raining."
(The rain = reason for staying home)
SO → shows the result
"It was raining, so I stayed home."
(Staying home = result of rain)

📝

Grammar Tip

When "although" starts a sentence, don't put "but" in the second part. ✅ "Although it was cold, we went out." ❌ "Although it was cold, but we went out."

🎯

Common Mistake

"Because" should not start a standalone sentence fragment. ❌ "Because I was tired." ✅ "I slept early because I was tired."

💡

Upgrade Your Writing

Replace simple "and" and "but" with more precise conjunctions. Instead of "I was tired but I worked" → "Although I was tired, I worked."
