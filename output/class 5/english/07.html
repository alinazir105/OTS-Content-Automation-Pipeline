Introduction

What are Tenses?

Tenses tell us when something happens — in the past, present, or future. Every sentence uses a tense to place an action in time.

🌟 The Three Time Zones

Think of time as a timeline with three zones: Past (already happened), Present (happening now), and Future (will happen). Each zone has four aspects: Simple, Continuous, Perfect, and Perfect Continuous — giving us 12 tenses total!

Did You Know?

English has 12 tenses in total — 4 in past, 4 in present, and 4 in future forms.

🔑

Key Insight

The word "tense" comes from the Latin tempus, meaning time. Tenses show the relationship between an action and time.

⚡

Quick Tip

Look for time words (yesterday, now, tomorrow) to help identify which tense to use in a sentence.

Overview

Tense Structures at a Glance

All 12 tenses with their structures, positive, negative, interrogative, and negative question forms.

Tense Type Structure Positive Example Negative Example Interrogative
Simple Present Sub + V1 + Obj She writes a letter. She does not write a letter. Does she write a letter?
Negative Question: Doesn't she write a letter? / Don't they play football?
Use: Habits, routines, general truths.
Simple Past Sub + V2 + Obj He painted a picture. He did not paint a picture. Did he paint a picture?
Negative Question: Didn't he paint a picture?
Use: Completed actions in the past.
Simple Future Sub + will + V1 + Obj They will visit the museum. They will not visit the museum. Will they visit the museum?
Negative Question: Won't they visit the museum?
Use: Future intentions and predictions.
Present Continuous Sub + is/am/are + V-ing + Obj She is washing the dishes. She is not washing the dishes. Is she washing the dishes?
Negative Question: Isn't she washing the dishes?
Use: Actions happening right now or around the present time.
Past Continuous Sub + was/were + V-ing + Obj He was driving a car. He was not driving a car. Was he driving a car?
Negative Question: Wasn't he driving a car?
Use: Actions that were in progress at a specific time in the past.
Future Continuous Sub + will be + V-ing + Obj I will be studying at 5 PM. I will not be studying at 5 PM. Will I be studying at 5 PM?
Negative Question: Won't I be studying at 5 PM?
Use: Actions that will be in progress at a future time.
Present Perfect Sub + has/have + V3 + Obj They have completed the project. They have not completed the project. Have they completed the project?
Negative Question: Haven't they completed the project?
Use: Actions completed at an unspecified time, with results relevant to present.
Past Perfect Sub + had + V3 + Obj He had finished his work before lunch. He had not finished his work before lunch. Had he finished his work before lunch?
Negative Question: Hadn't he finished his work before lunch?
Use: Actions completed before another past action.
Future Perfect Sub + will have + V3 + Obj She will have submitted her report by tomorrow. She will not have submitted her report by tomorrow. Will she have submitted her report by tomorrow?
Negative Question: Won't she have submitted her report by tomorrow?
Use: Actions that will be completed before a specific future time.
Present Perfect Continuous Sub + has/have + been + V-ing + Obj + since/for I have been practising guitar for two hours. I have not been practising guitar for two hours. Have I been practising guitar for two hours?
Negative Question: Haven't I been practising guitar for two hours?
Use: Actions that started in the past and are still continuing.
Past Perfect Continuous Sub + had + been + V-ing + Obj + since/for They had been playing football for an hour. They had not been playing football for an hour. Had they been playing football for an hour?
Negative Question: Hadn't they been playing football for an hour?
Use: Actions continuing before another past action — shows cause/reason.
Future Perfect Continuous Sub + will have been + V-ing + Obj + since/for She will have been exercising for an hour by 7 PM. She will not have been exercising for an hour by 7 PM. Will she have been exercising for an hour by 7 PM?
Negative Question: Won't she have been exercising for an hour by 7 PM?
Use: Actions continuing up to a point in the future — emphasis on duration.

  • V1 (Base form) — the dictionary form: write, play, go, eat
  • V2 (Past simple) — the past form: wrote, played, went, ate
  • V3 (Past participle) — the 3rd form: written, played, gone, eaten

Core Concept

Simple Tenses

Simple tenses express actions without indicating whether they are complete or ongoing.

🕐 Simple Present

Structure: Subject + V1 (base form) + Object
For he/she/it: add -s or -es
Examples

✅ She writes a letter. / They play football.

❌ She does not write a letter.

❓ Does she write a letter?

Uses: Habits, routines, general truths, scheduled events.

🕙 Simple Past

Structure: Subject + V2 (past form) + Object
Neg/Q: did + V1 (base form)
Examples

✅ He painted a picture. / We cooked dinner.

❌ He did not paint a picture.

❓ Did he paint a picture?

Uses: Completed actions at a specific past time.

🕐 Simple Future

Structure: Subject + will + V1 + Object
Neg: will not / won't + V1
Examples

✅ They will visit the museum. / I will call you.

❌ They will not visit the museum.

❓ Will they visit the museum?

Uses: Future intentions, predictions, spontaneous decisions.

Core Concept

Continuous Tenses

Continuous (progressive) tenses describe actions that are in progress at a particular time.

🔄 Present Continuous

Structure: Sub + is/am/are + V-ing + Obj
Examples

✅ She is washing the dishes. / They are running.

❌ She is not washing the dishes.

❓ Is she washing the dishes?

🔄 Past Continuous

Structure: Sub + was/were + V-ing + Obj
Examples

✅ He was driving a car. / We were watching a movie.

❌ He was not driving a car.

❓ Was he driving a car?

🔄 Future Continuous

Structure: Sub + will be + V-ing + Obj
Examples

✅ I will be studying at 5 PM. / She will be dancing.

❌ I will not be studying at 5 PM.

❓ Will I be studying at 5 PM?

  • Most verbs: just add -ing → play → playing, read → reading
  • Ends in 'e': drop 'e' then add -ing → write → writing, make → making
  • Short vowel + consonant: double consonant → run → running, sit → sitting, swim → swimming
  • Ends in 'ie': change 'ie' to 'y' → die → dying, tie → tying

Core Concept

Perfect Tenses

Perfect tenses show the relationship between two time periods — a completed action and its relevance to another time.

✅ Present Perfect

Structure: Sub + has/have + V3 + Obj
he/she/it: has | I/we/you/they: have
Examples

✅ They have completed the project.

✅ She has written an email.

❌ They have not completed the project.

❓ Have they completed the project?

✅ Past Perfect

Structure: Sub + had + V3 + Obj
All subjects use: had
Examples

✅ He had finished his work before lunch.

✅ We had left before the rain.

❌ He had not finished his work before lunch.

❓ Had he finished his work before lunch?

✅ Future Perfect

Structure: Sub + will have + V3 + Obj
Examples

✅ She will have submitted her report by tomorrow.

✅ They will have finished by 8 PM.

❌ She will not have submitted her report by tomorrow.

❓ Will she have submitted her report by tomorrow?

📌

Present Perfect Use

Use present perfect for actions with unspecified time or actions that started in the past and are relevant now: "I have eaten lunch."

📌

Past Perfect Use

Use past perfect for the action that happened first when two past actions are mentioned: "She had studied before the exam started."

📌

Future Perfect Use

Use future perfect for actions that will be completed before a specific future time: "By 5 PM, she will have finished the task."

Core Concept

Perfect Continuous Tenses

Perfect continuous tenses emphasize the duration of an action that connects two different time points.

⏱ Present Perfect Continuous

Structure: Sub + has/have + been + V-ing + Obj + since/for
Examples

✅ I have been practising guitar for two hours.

✅ She has been running since morning.

❌ I have not been practising guitar for two hours.

❓ Have I been practising guitar for two hours?

Uses: Actions started in the past and still continuing; temporary actions; emphasizing duration.

⏱ Past Perfect Continuous

Structure: Sub + had + been + V-ing + Obj + since/for
Examples

✅ They had been playing football for an hour.

✅ He had been working on a project for months.

❌ They had not been playing football for an hour.

❓ Had they been playing football for an hour?

Uses: Actions continuing before another past action; showing cause or reason in the past.

⏱ Future Perfect Continuous

Structure: Sub + will have been + V-ing + Obj + since/for
Examples

✅ She will have been exercising for an hour by 7 PM.

✅ We will have been travelling for five hours.

❌ She will not have been exercising for an hour by 7 PM.

❓ Will she have been exercising for an hour by 7 PM?

Uses: Actions continuing up to a future point; emphasis on future duration.

🔍 "Since" vs "For"

SINCE — a point in time

She has been studying since Monday.
He had been waiting since morning.

FOR — a duration

She has been studying for two hours.
He had been waiting for 30 minutes.

Special Topic

States & Possessions in Tenses

Certain verbs describe states (to be, get, become, remain) and possessions (have, get). These have special patterns across tenses.

🎭 States — Present

Verbs: to be, get, become, remain

✅ She is intelligent. / He gets tired easily. / She remains calm. / He becomes anxious.

❌ She is not intelligent. / He does not get tired easily.

❓ Is she intelligent? / Does he get tired easily?

📦 Possessions — Present

Verbs: has/have, get

✅ He has a bicycle. / She gets a new phone every year.

❌ He does not have a bicycle. / She does not get a new phone every year.

❓ Does he have a bicycle? / Does she get a new phone every year?

Past Simple — States

✅ She was intelligent. / He got tired easily.

❌ She was not intelligent. / He did not get tired easily.

❓ Was she intelligent? / Did he get tired easily?

Past Simple — Possession

✅ He had a bicycle. / She got a new phone last year.

❌ He did not have a bicycle.

❓ Did he have a bicycle?

Future Simple — States

✅ She will be intelligent. / He will get tired easily. / She will remain calm. / He will become anxious.

❌ She will not be intelligent.

❓ Will she be intelligent? / Will he get tired easily?

Future Simple — Possession

✅ He will have a bicycle. / She will get a new phone next year.

❌ He will not have a bicycle.

❓ Will he have a bicycle?
