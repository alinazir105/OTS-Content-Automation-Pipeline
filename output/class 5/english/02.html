🔍 What Are Pronouns?

Why Do We Use Pronouns?

Pronouns are words that replace nouns in sentences. They help us avoid repetition and make our writing and speaking flow more naturally.

Discovery Zone: Spot the Difference!

❌ Without Pronouns (Repetitive):

"Ali went to school. Ali took Ali's bag. Ali met Ali's friends and Ali played with Ali's friends."

✅ With Pronouns (Natural):

"Ali went to school. He took his bag. He met his friends and he played with them."

📌 Key Rule
Pronoun = A word that replaces a noun to avoid repetition and add variety to language.
📚 Core Concept

Types of Pronouns

There are five main types of pronouns covered in this unit. Each type has a specific role in a sentence.

👤

1. Subject Pronouns

Come at the place of the subject in a sentence — the person or thing doing the action.

I we you he she it they

Ali plays football. → He plays football.

My mother cooks food. → She cooks food.

Sarah and Sofia speak. → They speak.

🎯

2. Object Pronouns

Come at the place of the object — the person or thing receiving the action.

me us you him her it them

I saw Sarah. → I saw her.

She cooks biryani. → She cooks it.

They called Ali. → They called him.

🔑

3. Possessive Pronouns

Show relationship and ownership — indicate who something belongs to.

mine ours yours his hers its theirs

This book is Ali's. → This book is his.

That car is Maria's. → That car is hers.

These toys are the children's. → These toys are theirs.

🔄 Type 4

Reflexive Pronouns

When the subject and the object of a sentence refer to the same person or thing, we use a reflexive pronoun. These pronouns end in -self (singular) or -selves (plural).

Person Pronoun Type Example Sentence
First Person (singular) myself Singular I taught myself how to play the piano.
💡 "Myself" refers back to "I" — same person doing and receiving the action.
Second Person (singular) yourself Singular Enjoy yourself at the party!
💡 "Yourself" is used with certain verbs: enjoy yourself, behave yourself, prepare yourself.
Third Person (male) himself Singular He looked at himself in the mirror.
💡 The boy dressed himself — showing the subject did the action alone.
Third Person (female) herself Singular She blamed herself for the mistake.
💡 "Herself" = subject (she) and object (herself) are the same person.
Third Person (thing) itself Singular The cat licked itself clean.
💡 Used for animals or objects when subject and object are the same.
First Person (plural) ourselves Plural We enjoyed ourselves at the party.
💡 Plural reflexive — "we" refers back to "ourselves."
Second Person (plural) yourselves Plural Please behave yourselves!
💡 Used when speaking to multiple people.
Third Person (plural) themselves Plural They took care of themselves during the trip.
💡 They refers back to themselves — the same group doing and receiving the action.

Three Uses of Reflexive Pronouns

1️⃣

Refer Back to Subject

"I taught myself how to play the piano."

2️⃣

Subject Did It Alone

"The boy dressed himself." (without help)

3️⃣

With Certain Verbs

enjoy yourself, prepare yourself, behave yourself

⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid!

Reflexive pronouns should NOT be used in place of subject or object pronouns.

❌ Incorrect
"Myself will handle the work."
✅ Correct
"I will handle the work myself."
💪 Type 5

Intensive Pronouns

When a reflexive pronoun is placed soon after the subject, it is called an intensive (or emphatic) pronoun. It emphasizes the subject — showing that the subject did it personally.

🔑 Formula
Subject + Intensive Pronoun + Verb + Object

Example: "He himself admitted the mistake."
💬

I myself fixed the broken chair.

💬

He himself admitted the mistake.

💬

They themselves organized the event.

💬

The president herself signed the document.

Reflexive vs. Intensive — Side by Side Comparison

🔄 Reflexive Pronoun 💪 Intensive Pronoun
He fixed the car himself.
(object — car was fixed by him)		 He himself fixed the car.
(emphasis — HE personally did it)
She prepared herself for the exam.
(reflexive — she got herself ready)		 She herself prepared for the exam.
(emphasis — SHE personally prepared)
They hurt themselves playing.
(object — they received the hurt)		 They themselves organized the trip.
(emphasis — THEY did the organizing)

The word "I" in English

"I" is always capitalized in English! It's the only single-letter pronoun that must be uppercase, no matter where it appears in a sentence.

🏛️

Click to discover!

Ancient pronouns

English pronouns have roots going back over 1,000 years! Old English used "hē" for "he" and "hīe" for "they" — very similar to modern English.

🎭

Click to discover!

Singular "they"

"They" can refer to a single person! We often say "Someone left their bag — can they collect it?" using "they" for one unknown person. This has been used in English since the 1300s!

📖

Click to discover!

Most used pronoun

"I" is one of the most frequently used words in the English language! Subject pronouns as a group account for about 35% of all pronoun usage in everyday writing.
