📚 Background

What Are Voices in Grammar?

In English grammar, "voice" tells us the relationship between the subject, verb, and object of a sentence. There are two main voices: Active and Passive.

🔍 Discovery Zone: The Core Difference

🟣 Active Voice
"The cat chases the mouse."

The subject performs the action.

Subject: The cat
Verb: chases
Object: the mouse
🔵 Passive Voice
"The mouse is chased by the cat."

The subject receives the action.

Subject: The mouse
Verb: is chased
Agent: by the cat

🧩 Core Concept

Understanding the Transformation

When we change from active to passive, the object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence. The original subject becomes the "agent" (introduced with "by").

ACTIVE SUBJECT
The children
→
BECOMES AGENT
by the children
|
ACTIVE OBJECT
the cookies
→
BECOMES SUBJECT
The cookies

📐 Passive Voice Formula

🟢 PRESENT SIMPLE
Subject + is/am/are + Verb (3rd form) + by + Agent
Subject + is/am/are + NOT + Verb (3rd form) + by + Agent
Is/Am/Are + Subject + Verb (3rd form) + by + Agent?
🟡 PAST SIMPLE
Subject + was/were + Verb (3rd form) + by + Agent
Subject + was/were + NOT + Verb (3rd form) + by + Agent
Was/Were + Subject + Verb (3rd form) + by + Agent?
🔵 FUTURE SIMPLE
Subject + will be + Verb (3rd form) + by + Agent
Subject + will NOT be + Verb (3rd form) + by + Agent
Will + Subject + be + Verb (3rd form) + by + Agent?

💡 Did You Know?

🔄

Object Becomes Subject

Click to reveal!

When converting to passive, the object of the active sentence moves to the subject position. "The cookies" is the object → becomes subject in passive!

🚫

No Passive Possible!

Click to reveal!

Sentences with intransitive verbs (go, sleep, run, laugh, cry...) or state verbs (have, is, are) CANNOT be converted to passive voice!

📰

Passive in Real Life

Click to reveal!

Newspapers and scientific reports often use passive voice to focus on WHAT happened rather than WHO did it. "The bridge was built in 2005."

⏰

4 Tenses, No Passive!

Click to reveal!

Four tenses CANNOT form passive: Future Continuous, Present Perfect Continuous, Past Perfect Continuous, and Future Perfect Continuous!

📋 Rules

Rules for Converting Active to Passive

Not every sentence can be made passive. Learn these important rules before you start converting!

1

No State Verbs

We cannot make a passive with state (non-action) verbs like is, am, are, have, has.

❌ I have a car. → A car is had by me.
(This is incorrect!)
2

Needs an Object

We cannot make a passive if there is no object in the sentence. An object is required.

❌ "You teach." — No object, so no passive form is possible.
3

No Intransitive Verbs

Intransitive verbs affect only the subject, not an object. They cannot be made passive.

❌ Verbs: go, arrive, sleep, laugh, run, cry, talk, wait, swim, fall, smile, sit, dance, jump
4

Focus on the Action

Use passive when we want to focus on what happened, not who did it. The doer becomes less important.

✅ "The road was built." (Focus on the road, not the workers.)

🚫 Intransitive Verbs — No Passive Possible!

Go No Passive
She goes to school every day.
No receiver of the action.
Arrive No Passive
The train arrived late.
No object, no passive form.
Sleep No Passive
The baby sleeps peacefully.
No one receives the sleeping.
Laugh No Passive
They laughed at the joke.
Doesn't act on anyone.
Run No Passive
The children run in the park.
No object to receive action.
Cry No Passive
The baby cried loudly.
No object to be acted upon.
Talk No Passive
He talks to his friends daily.
No passive construction.
Wait No Passive
We waited for the bus.
Waiting isn't done to anyone.
Swim No Passive
She swims in the pool.
No receiver of swimming.

The leaves fall in autumn.
No one is "fallen" upon.
Smile No Passive
He smiled at her.
No direct object despite "at".
Dance No Passive
She dances at the party.
No object to act upon.
⏱️ Tenses

Active vs Passive Across Tenses

See how the sentence changes across Present, Past, and Future tenses in both Active and Passive Voice.

🟣 Active Voice — Affirmative, Negative, Interrogative

TenseAffirmative ✅Negative ❌Interrogative ❓Neg. Question
Present The children eat the cookies. The children don't eat the cookies. Do the children eat the cookies? Don't the children eat the cookies?
Past The children ate the cookies. The children didn't eat the cookies. Did the children eat the cookies? Didn't the children eat the cookies?
Future The children will eat the cookies. The children won't eat the cookies. Will the children eat the cookies? Won't the children eat the cookies?

🔵 Passive Voice — Affirmative, Negative, Interrogative

TenseAffirmative ✅Negative ❌Interrogative ❓Neg. Question
Present The cookies are eaten by the children. The cookies aren't eaten by the children. Are the cookies eaten by the children? Aren't the cookies eaten by the children?
Past The cookies were eaten by the children. The cookies weren't eaten by the children. Were the cookies eaten by the children? Weren't the cookies eaten by the children?
Future The cookies will be eaten by the children. The cookies won't be eaten by the children. Will the cookies be eaten by the children? Won't the cookies be eaten by the children?

📌 Key Patterns to Remember

Present: is/am/are + V3 Past: was/were + V3 Future: will be + V3

Always use the 3rd form (past participle) of the verb in passive constructions. Add "by + agent" to show who performed the action.

📝 Convert the Sentences

Formula: Subject + is/am/are + Verb (3rd form) + by + Agent
Singular subjects use is. Plural subjects use are.
Example: "He writes a letter." → "A letter is written by him."
Formula: Subject + was/were + Verb (3rd form) + by + Agent
Singular: was. Plural: were.
Example: "He wrote a letter." → "A letter was written by him."
Formula: Subject + will be + Verb (3rd form) + by + Agent
Example: "He will write a letter." → "A letter will be written by him."

🎯 Advanced Challenge Questions

1. Convert to passive: "The workers built the road." (Past Simple)
2. Can "The baby cried loudly." be made passive? Why or why not?
3. Convert to active: "The movie is watched by the audience."
4. Convert to passive (Future Simple): "She will not teach English next week."
5. Identify the error: "I have a book. → A book is had by me." What rule is broken?
Answers:
1. The road was built by the workers.
2. No, because "cry" is an intransitive verb — it has no object to receive the action.
3. The audience watches the movie.
4. English will not be taught by her next week.
5. Rule 1 is broken — "have" is a state verb and cannot be made passive.
