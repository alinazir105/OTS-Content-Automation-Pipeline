📖 Key Concepts

Core Definitions

🌿

Biology

Biology is the branch of science which deals with the study of living things. From Greek: Bio (Life) + Logos (Study).

🔬

Cell

Cell is the basic structural and functional unit of a living organism. All life activities depend on the cell.

💧

Protoplasm

It is the combination of organic and specific inorganic substances — the physical and chemical basis of life.

🔭 What is Biology?

Biology: The Science of Life

The word Biology is composed of two Greek words: "Bio" meaning "Life" and "Logos" meaning "Discourse, thought or reasoning" — or in simple terms, study. It is the branch of natural science that deals with organisms and studies different phenomena of life.

"Biology is a multidimensional subject that links with other sciences — it is truly an interdisciplinary science."

— Introduction to Biology, Unit 1
📐 Divisions

Three Main Divisions of Biology

I

Zoology

From Greek: Zoon (Animals) + Logos (Study). Deals with the study of animals.

II

Botany

From Greek: Butane (Plants) + Logos (Study). Deals with the study of plants.

III

Microbiology

Deals with the study of microscopic organisms such as bacteria, viruses, etc., visible only with a microscope.

Key Term
Bio + Logos

Greek roots: Bio = Life, Logos = Study. Together: "Study of Life."

Remember
3 Divisions

Definition
Protoplasm

Organic + Inorganic substances forming the physical basis of life inside a cell.

🌐 Branches

15 Important Branches of Biology

1

Morphology

The study of external form and structure of organisms.

2

Anatomy

The study of internal parts of the body of living organisms by cutting them open.

3

Cell Biology

The study of cells and their organelles.

4

Histology

The study of the structure of tissues of plants and animals.

5

Physiology

The study about functions of living organisms.

6

Taxonomy

The study of rules, principles, grouping and naming of living organisms.

7

Genetics

The study of heredity — transferring of characters from parents to offspring.

8

Developmental Biology

The study of formation and development of embryo (Embryology).

9

Environmental Biology

The study of relationship between living organisms and non-living factors of the environment.

10

Paleontology

The study of remote past organic life with the help of fossils.

11

Biotechnology

Techniques for manipulation of genes to achieve desirable characters in organisms.

12

Socio-biology

The study of social behavior of living organisms and their interactions.

13

Parasitology

The study of parasites.

14

Pharmacology

The study about the action of drugs on living organisms.

15

Molecular Biology

The study of organic molecules that constitute cells and organelles.

🔗 Interdisciplinary

Relationship of Biology with Other Sciences

Biology is considered an interdisciplinary science, which is related with other sciences. Some of these relationships are:

Biophysics

Laws and techniques of physics applied to explain the processes of life.

Biometry

The branch of mathematics which collects and analyzes data of living organisms. Plays a vital role in research.

Biochemistry

Deals with molecules forming living organisms/cells; explains synthesis of biomolecules and their functions.

Biogeography

Deals with distribution of living organisms in different geographical regions of the world.

Bio-Economics

Deals with economically important organisms — calculating cost and profit values in production (e.g., meat production).

📿 Quranic Perspective

What Does the Quran Describe About Life?

The Holy Quran mentions the origin and diversity of life in remarkable ways that align with biological understanding. Three important references are presented below:

أَوَلَمْ يَرَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا أَنَّ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ كَانَتَا رَتْقًا فَفَتَقْنَاهُمَا وَجَعَلْنَا مِنَ الْمَاءِ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ حَيٍّ أَفَلَا يُؤْمِنُونَ
"We made everything of water. Will they not then believe?"
Surah Al-Anbiya, Ayah 30

🔬 Biological Relevance: Water is the primary constituent of all living cells (60–90% of cell mass), essential for all metabolic processes.

وَاللَّهُ خَلَقَ كُلَّ دَابَّةٍ مِنْ مَاءٍ فَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَمْشِي عَلَى بَطْنِهِ وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَمْشِي عَلَى رِجْلَيْنِ وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَمْشِي عَلَى أَرْبَعٍ يَخْلُقُ اللَّهُ مَا يَشَاءُ إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ
"And Allah has created every animal from water. Of them there are some that creep on their bellies, some that walk on two legs; and some that walk on four. Allah creates what He will. Lo! Allah is able to do all."
Surah Al-Nur, Ayah 45

🔬 Biological Relevance: This verse classifies animals by their mode of locomotion — a fundamental concept in Zoology.

وَفِي الْأَرْضِ قِطَعٌ مُتَجَاوِرَاتٌ وَجَنَّاتٌ مِنْ أَعْنَابٍ وَزَرْعٌ وَنَخِيلٌ صِنْوَانٌ وَغَيْرُ صِنْوَانٍ يُسْقَى بِمَاءٍ وَاحِدٍ وَنُفَضِّلُ بَعْضَهَا عَلَى بَعْضٍ فِي الْأُكُلِ إِنَّ فِي ذَلِكَ لَآيَاتٍ لِقَوْمٍ يَعْقِلُونَ
"And in the earth are neighboring tracts, vineyards and ploughed lands, and datepalms, like and unlike, which are watered with one water. And We have made some of them excel others in fruits. Lo! Herein verily are portents for people who have sense."
Surah Al-Ra'd, Ayah 4

🔬 Biological Relevance: This ayah highlights botanical diversity — plants watered by the same source yet producing different fruits, a core concept in plant genetics and physiology.

Quranic Insight
Water — Origin of Life

Al-Anbiya 30 states everything living is made from water — confirmed by modern biology: cells are ~70% water.

Classification
Animal Locomotion

Al-Nur 45 classifies animals by movement: belly, 2 legs, 4 legs — Zoological classification!

Botany Link
Plant Diversity

Al-Ra'd 4 discusses plants of different types growing from the same water — genetic diversity in Botany.

🔭 Organization

Levels of Biological Organization

The levels of organization in the living world are based on a chemical foundation. All living organisms are made up of cells and the protoplasm of the cell is the physical as well as chemical basis of life.

⚛️ Atomic Level of Organization

▼

All matter is made up of elements, which are composed of atoms. Each atom is made up of sub-atomic particles: electrons, protons, and neutrons.

In nature, there are more than 100 kinds of elements. Among these, 16 elements are called bio-elements, which are vital for life.

Only six elements — C, H, O, N, S, and P — are called the basic elements of life.

🧪 Molecular Level of Organization

▼

Molecules are formed by the binding of atoms. Organic molecules of cells are called bio-molecules, constructed in great variety and complexity.

They are classified as:

  • Micro-molecules: Glucose, amino acids, fatty acids
  • Macro-molecules: Carbohydrates, proteins, lipids

Units of micro-molecules combine to form macro-molecules.

🔬 Cellular Level of Organization

▼

When bio-molecules work together in suspension, it is called Protoplasm. When protoplasm works as a unit, it is called a Cell.

From cell to organism:

  • Cells → Tissues (similar cells grouped together)
  • Tissues → Organs (different tissues arranged together)
  • Organs → Organ Systems (organs working in coordination)
  • Organ Systems → Organism (multicellular body)

🏷️ Taxonomic Level

▼

Species is the smallest unit of taxonomic level. It includes morphologically similar living organisms that inter-breed and produce fertile offspring.

👥 Population Level

▼

All the members of a species living in a specific habitat are called a Population.

Example: A group of parrots living on a tree is called a parrot population.

🌳 Community Level

▼

Members of different species living in a specific habitat are called a Community.

Example: A group of different kinds of birds living on a tree is called a bird community.

🌍 Ecological System (Ecosystem)

▼

Communities always depend upon their non-living environment in a reciprocal interaction for survival. For example, organisms obtain oxygen from the environment for respiration and give out CO₂ in return.

This interaction is called an Ecosystem (Ecological System).

🌐 Biosphere Level

▼

The part of Earth where life exists is called the Biosphere. It consists of different kinds of ecosystems.

🔬 Cell Organization

Types of Cell Organization

In living organisms, cells organize in three ways to make bodies of organisms:

1

Unicellular Organization

Only one cell makes the life. All life activities are carried out by this single cell.

🦠 Examples:

Amoeba: Found in shallow ponds and stagnant water. Size ~0.25mm. Moves by pseudopodia (false feet). Has ectoplasm (gel) and endoplasm (sol).

Paramecium and Euglena are also unicellular organisms.

2

Colonial Organization

Many unicellular organisms live together but without division of labor. Each cell lives its own life independently.

🌿 Example: Volvox

Volvox: A green alga found in water. Hundreds of Volvox cells form a colony. Each cell has two flagella for movement. Contains chlorophyll — makes food by photosynthesis. Not harmful to humans.

3

Multicellular Organization

Cells organize into tissues, organs and organ systems. Each has a specific role — division of labor.

🌱 Example: Mustard Plant

Mustard (Brassica campestris): Sown in winter. Plant body used as vegetable; seeds for extracting oil. Has vegetative parts (roots, branches, leaves) and reproductive parts (flowers, fruits, seeds).

📋 Key Steps in Cellular Organization

1

Atoms combine to form molecules (bio-molecules like glucose, amino acids).

2

Bio-molecules work together in suspension forming Protoplasm.

3

Protoplasm enclosed in a unit boundary becomes a Cell — basic unit of life.

4

Similar cells grouped together form a Tissue (e.g., muscle tissue).

5

Different tissues arranged together to perform a function form an Organ (e.g., heart, leaf).

6

Organs working in coordination form an Organ System (e.g., circulatory system).

7

All organ systems together form a complete Multicellular Organism.
