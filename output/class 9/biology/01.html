Core Definitions

Key Terms

Biology
Biology is the branch of science which deals with the study of living things. From Greek: Bio (life) + Logos (study).
Cell
Cell is the basic structural and functional unit of living organisms. All life is composed of one or more cells.
Protoplasm
It is the combination of organic and specific inorganic substances. It is the physical and chemical basis of life.
What is Biology?

The Science of Life

The word Biology is composed of two Greek words: "Bio" meaning "Life" and "Logos" meaning "Discourse", thought, reasoning, or simply "study." It is the branch of natural science that deals with organisms and studies different phenomena of life.

Divisions of Biology

Three Main Divisions

Biology is broadly divided into three main branches that form the foundation of the entire discipline:

🦁
Zoology
From Greek Zoon (animals) + Logos (study). Deals with the study of animals.
🌱
Botany
From Greek Butane (plants) + Logos (study). Deals with the study of plants.
🦠
Microbiology
Deals with the study of microscopic organisms such as bacteria, viruses etc., visible only with a microscope.
Etymology
Bio = Life (Greek)
Logos = Study (Greek)
Biology = Bio + Logos
Remember
3 Main Divisions: Zoology, Botany, Microbiology
Key Fact
Biology is an interdisciplinary science linking with physics, chemistry, math and more.
Detailed Study

15 Branches of Biology

Each branch specializes in a particular aspect of life science, collectively covering the full spectrum of biological knowledge.

1. Morphology
The study of external form and structure of organisms.
2. Anatomy
The study of internal parts of body of living organisms by cutting them open.
3. Cell Biology
The study of cells and their organelles.
4. Histology
The study of structure of tissues of plants and animals.
5. Physiology
The study about functions of living organisms.
6. Taxonomy
The study of rules, principles, grouping and naming of living organisms.
7. Genetics
The study of heredity — transferring of characters from parents to offspring.
8. Embryology
The study of formation and development of embryo. (Developmental Biology)
9. Environmental Biology
The study of relationship between living organisms and non-living environmental factors.
10. Paleontology
The study of remote past organic life with the help of fossils.
11. Biotechnology
The study of techniques for manipulation of genes to bring desirable changes in organisms.
12. Socio-Biology
The study of social behavior of living organisms — interaction between themselves.
13. Parasitology
The study of parasites and their relationship with host organisms.
14. Pharmacology
The study about the action of drugs on living organisms.
15. Molecular Biology
The study of organic molecules which constitute the cell and its organelles.
📋 Detailed Notes on Key Branches ▼
1
Morphology — Studies the external appearance. Helps classify organisms by their shapes and structures.
2
Genetics — Heredity is the transfer of genetic information (traits) from parents to offspring via DNA. Studied at molecular and cellular levels.
3
Biotechnology — Manipulates gene structure and location to produce organisms with desirable characteristics (e.g., insulin-producing bacteria).
4
Taxonomy — The science of classification. Groups organisms based on shared characteristics. Species is the basic unit.
5
Paleontology — Studies fossils to understand prehistoric life forms and the history of evolution on Earth.
Interdisciplinary Science

Biology & Other Sciences

Biology is an interdisciplinary science that overlaps and connects with many other scientific fields, creating hybrid disciplines of great importance.

Biophysics
Laws and techniques of physics are applied to explain the processes of life (e.g., nerve impulses, muscle mechanics).
Biometry (Biomathematics)
The branch of mathematics that collects and analyzes data of living organisms. Plays a vital role in biological research.
Biochemistry
Deals with molecules that form living organisms. Explains synthesis of biomolecules and their functions in the body.
Biogeography
Deals with the distribution of different living organisms across different geographical regions of the world.
Bio-Economics
Deals with economically important organisms in production. Cost values and profit values are calculated (e.g., meat, milk production).
🔗 Why is Biology Called a Multidimensional Subject? ▼
1
Multiple Dimensions — Biology links with physics (Biophysics), chemistry (Biochemistry), mathematics (Biometry), geography (Biogeography), and economics (Bio-Economics).
2
Interdisciplinary Nature — No single science can explain life completely. Biology borrows tools, methods, and theories from all other sciences.
3
Practical Applications — Biotechnology, medicine, agriculture, and environmental science all require biological knowledge combined with other disciplines.
Hybrid Fields
Physics + Biology = Biophysics
Math + Biology = Biometry
Chemistry + Biology = Biochemistry
Exam Tip

Historical Contributions

Muslim Scientists in Biology

Muslim scholars made foundational contributions to biology centuries before the Renaissance, laying the groundwork for modern life science.

Jabir Bin Hayan
721–815 A.D.
Al-Nabatat · Al-Haywan
  • Born in Iran, practiced in Iraq
  • Introduced experimental investigation in chemistry
  • Wrote extensively on plants (Al-Nabatat) and animals (Al-Haywan)
Abdul Malik Asmai
740–828 A.D.
Al-Ibil · Al-Kheil · Al-Wahoosh · Khalaqal-Insan
  • First Muslim scientist to study animals in detail
  • Al-Ibil: study of camel
  • Al-Kheil: study of horse
  • Al-Wahoosh: study of wild animals
Bu Ali Sina (Avicenna)
980 A.D.
Al-Qanoon Fil Tib · Al-Shafa · Khalaqal-Insan
  • Greatest physician-philosopher of Islamic Golden Age
  • Al-Qanoon Fil Tib: Canon of Medicine (used in Europe for centuries)
  • Al-Shafa: encyclopedic philosophical treatise
  • Described human anatomy and physiology

Structural Hierarchy

Levels of Organization

Living world is organized in a hierarchical manner from the simplest atoms to the entire biosphere. Each level emerges from and depends upon the one below it.

🏗️ Hierarchy of Biological Organization

graph LR A[⚛️ Atoms] --> B[🔬 Molecules] B --> C[🧬 Bio-Molecules] C --> D[🔵 Protoplasm] D --> E[🔴 Cell] E --> F[🧱 Tissue] F --> G[🫀 Organ] G --> H[⚙️ Organ System] H --> I[🧍 Organism] I --> J[👥 Population] J --> K[🌳 Community] K --> L[🌐 Ecosystem] L --> M[🌍 Biosphere]
⚛️ Atomic Level of Organization ▼
1
All matter is made of elements, which are composed of atoms.
2
Each atom is made up of sub-atomic particles: electrons, protons, and neutrons.
3
In nature, there are more than 100 kinds of elements. Among these, 16 elements are called bio-elements — vital for life.
4
Only six elements — C, H, O, N, S and P — are called the basic elements of life.
🔬 Molecular Level of Organization ▼
1
Molecules are formed by the binding of atoms. Organic molecules of cells are called bio-molecules.
2
Bio-molecules are classified as: Micro-molecules (glucose, amino acids, fatty acids) and Macro-molecules (carbohydrates, proteins, lipids).
3
Units of micro-molecules combine together to form macro-molecules.
🔴 Cellular Level of Organization ▼
1
Protoplasm: When bio-molecules work together in suspension form, it is called Protoplasm. It is the physical and chemical basis of life.
2
Cell: When protoplasm works as a unit, it forms a cell. Cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
3
Tissue: Similar types of cells organized in a group are called tissues.
4
Organ: Different types of tissues arranged together to work in a particular manner.
5
Organ System: Organs of different types working in a coordinated manner to perform a function.
6
Organism: When different organ systems function in co-ordination as a unit, they form a body or multicellular organism.
👥 Population, Community & Ecosystem Levels ▼
1
Species (Taxonomic Level): The smallest unit of taxonomic organization. Morphologically similar organisms that interbreed and produce fertile offspring.
2
Population: All members of a species living in a specific habitat. e.g., A group of parrots living on a tree.
3
Community: Members of different species living in a specific habitat. e.g., Different kinds of birds living in the same tree.
4
Ecosystem: Communities interacting with their non-living environment. e.g., Organisms obtain oxygen from environment and release CO₂ in return.
5
Biosphere: The part of Earth where life exists. It consists of all different ecosystems combined.
6 Basic Elements
C, H, O, N, S, P are the 6 basic elements of life.

Cellular Biology

Cell Organization

In living organisms, cells organize in three fundamental ways to form the bodies of organisms: unicellular, colonial, and multicellular.

Type 1

🔵 Unicellular Organization

Only one cell makes the life. All life activities are carried out by this single cell — digestion, movement, reproduction, and response all in one unit.

Examples: Amoeba · Paramecium · Euglena
🔍 Amoeba — Detailed Structure ▼
1
Found in muddy, shallow ponds, pools, and stagnant water
2
Size approximately 0.25 millimeters
3
Cell membrane helps in movement of molecules and protects cytoplasm
4
Outer cytoplasm = clear ectoplasm (gel); Inner cytoplasm = endoplasm (sol)
5
Contains nucleus, food vacuoles, mitochondria, contractile vacuole
6
Moves by false feet called pseudopodia
Type 2

🟢 Colonial Organization

Many unicellular organisms live together but without division of labour. Each cell in the colony lives its own life and does not depend on other cells for vital requirements.

Example: Volvox (green alga found in water)
🔍 Volvox — Detailed Structure ▼
1
A green alga; hundreds of Volvox cells make a single colony
2
Each Volvox cell has two flagella
3
Flagella beat together to roll the body in water
4
Cells contain chlorophyll and make their own food by photosynthesis
5
Not harmful to humans — produces no toxic substances
Type 3

🌿 Multicellular Organization

Cells organize into tissues, organs, and organ systems with division of labour. Each type of cell specializes in a particular function for the benefit of the whole organism.

Example: Mustard Plant (Brassica campestris)
🔍 Mustard Plant — Details ▼
1
Scientific Name: Brassica campestris
2
Sowing: Sown in winter; produces seeds at end of winter
3
Importance: Plant body used as vegetable; seeds used for extracting oil
4
Vegetative Parts: Roots, branches, leaves — do NOT take part in sexual reproduction

Scientific Reasoning

Key Reasoning Questions

Q: Why is Biology called multidimensional?
Biology links with physics (Biophysics), chemistry (Biochemistry), mathematics (Biometry), and economics (Bio-Economics). It is an interdisciplinary science.
Q: Why is Species the smallest taxonomic unit?
Because it includes morphologically similar living organisms that interbreed and produce fertile offspring — no smaller group can be classified.
Q: How does farming help mankind?
Farming deals with animal farms (protein/milk), poultry farms (chicken/eggs), and fruit farms (different fruit-yielding plants). Animal breeding technologies improve food production.
Q: How is Population different from Community?
Population = same species in specific habitat (e.g., group of parrots). Community = different species in specific habitat (e.g., different birds on one tree).
