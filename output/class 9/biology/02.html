Definitions

Key Terms

Before exploring the biological method, understand these essential vocabulary terms used throughout the unit.

Biological Method
The scientific method in which biological problems are solved. Any organised and systematic approach used to resolve a specific biological problem.
Theory
A set of consistent and experimentally supported scientific assumptions. A hypothesis which is proved experimentally and stands the test of time.
Data
The information such as names, dates or values made from observation and experimentation. A single piece of collected information.
Mathematical Biology
A field of research that examines mathematical representation of biological systems using models, equations, and statistical tools.
Mathematical Models
A description of a system using mathematical concepts and languages. One key role of mathematics in biology is the creation of such models.
Hypothesis
An intelligent guess made by a scientist in the form of a statement, based on observations. A key component of the scientific process.
Core Concept

The Biological Method

The biological method is a structured approach scientists use to solve problems in biology. It involves a series of logical steps from observation to forming a law or theory.

The Six Steps in Detail

1

Observation

The first step is to identify the reason of the problem, followed by formulating a question about what has been observed. In 1880, a French physician, Laveran, studied the blood samples of malaria patients under a microscope and observed tiny creatures, which he named Plasmodium.

2

Hypothesis

Hypothesis is a key component of the scientific process. It is defined as "the intelligent guess made by a scientist in the form of a statement." For example, in the malaria case, the hypothesis was: "Plasmodium is the cause of malaria."

3

Deduction

The logical consequences of a hypothesis are called deductions. For this purpose, a hypothesis is taken as true and expected results are drawn. "If a particular hypothesis is true, then one should expect a certain result."

4

Experimentation

Experiments are designed to test the deduction. Biologists perform experiments to see if the hypothesis is true or not. Controlled experiments compare groups carefully to isolate variables.

5

Results

The results are where you record what happened in the experiment — detailing all observations and data made during experimentation. Results either verify or challenge the hypothesis.

6

Law or Theory

After repeated experimentation and elimination of personal biases, the hypothesis becomes a theory — a general principle that is universally accepted. "A theory is a statement (hypothesis) which is proved experimentally."

Case Study

Malaria — A Biological Problem Solved

The Mystery of Malaria

A Historical Investigation Using the Biological Method

In ancient times, physicians were familiar with malaria — a fever with recurring attacks. They observed that the disease was more common among people living in low marshy areas.

💡 The word "malaria" literally comes from Italian: mala aria = "bad air" — an early incorrect hypothesis!

Major Observations About Malaria

Observation 1
Malaria and marshy areas have some relation — the disease was more common near swamps.
Observation 2
Quinine is an effective drug for treating malaria, suggesting a biological cause.
Observation 3
Drinking the water of marshes does not cause malaria — ruling out waterborne transmission.
Observation 4
Plasmodium was seen in the blood of malarial patients under microscope by Laveran in 1880.

🧪 Step-by-Step Malaria Experiment

+
H
Hypothesis: "Plasmodium is the cause of malaria."
D
Deduction: "If Plasmodium is the cause of malaria, then ALL persons ill with malaria should have Plasmodium in their blood."
E1
Experiment Group 1: Blood of 100 malarial patients was examined under microscope.
E2
Control Group: Blood of 100 healthy persons was also examined under microscope for comparison.
R
Result: Almost all malaria patients had Plasmodium in their blood. Only 7 out of 100 healthy persons had Plasmodium — these were in the incubation period (period between entry of parasite in host and appearance of symptoms).
✓
Conclusion: Results confirmed the hypothesis. "Plasmodium is the cause of Malaria" was verified.
Data & Analysis

Data Organisation & Analysis

What is Data?

Data can be defined as "a single piece of information such as names, dates or values made from observations and experiments." Data organisation and analysis are critical steps in the biological method.

Data Organisation

In order to formulate and then test the hypothesis, scientists collect and organise data. Prior to conducting an experiment, it is important to describe collection methods because it ensures the quality of experiments.

Data is organised in different formats:

📊 Graphics
Bar charts, pie charts, line graphs to visualise trends and comparisons.
📋 Tables
Organised rows and columns for systematic comparison of values.
🗺️ Maps
Geographic distribution of diseases, species, or biological phenomena.
📐 Diagrams
Flowcharts, structural diagrams, and concept maps for relationships.

Data Analysis

Data analysis is necessary to prove or disprove a hypothesis by experimentation. It is done through the application of statistical methods. The acceptance or rejection of a hypothesis is entirely based on the mathematical analysis of recorded data.

Mathematical Biology

Ratio, Proportion & Mathematical Models

Ratio
When a relation between two numbers a and b is expressed in terms of quotient a/b, it is called the ratio of one number to the other.
Proportion
Proportion means to join two equal ratios by the sign of equality (=). If a:b = c:d, then a, b, c, d are in proportion.

📐 Worked Example: Proportion in Malaria Research

A biologist allowed infected mosquitoes to bite sparrows. In one experiment, when mosquitoes bit 20 sparrows, 14 got malaria. How many out of 100 would get malaria?

1st Ratio: 14 : 20  (14 out of 20)
2nd Ratio: X : 100  (? out of 100)
Proportion: 14 : 20 :: X : 100
Cross multiply: X × 20 = 100 × 14
Solve: X = (100 / 20) × 14 = 5 × 14

✅ X = 70  →  70 out of 100 sparrows get malaria

This proportion method allows biologists to scale small experimental results to larger populations.

Mathematical Biology & Models

Mathematical biology is a field of research that examines mathematical representations of biological systems. Mathematical models describe systems using mathematical concepts and languages — they are a key tool for prediction and analysis in biology.

Comparisons

Key Comparisons

Qualitative vs. Quantitative Observations

Feature Qualitative Observations Quantitative Observations
Accuracy Considered Less Accurate Considered More Accurate
Measurability Variable and less measurable Invariable and measurable
Numbers Cannot be recorded in numbers Can be recorded in numbers
Example 1 The freezing point of water is colder than its boiling point The freezing point is 0°C and boiling point is 100°C
Example 2 A litre of water is heavier than a litre of ethanol A litre of water weighs 1000g; ethanol weighs 789g

Theory vs. Law

Feature Theory Scientific Law
Definition A hypothesis that stands the test of time, often tested and never rejected A uniform, constant fact of nature — an irrefutable theory
Challenge A theory may be challenged A law cannot be challenged
Alteration Can be altered in case of new evidence Already an established, definite entity — cannot be altered
Testing Always subjected to new testing Not subjected to further testing
Example Darwin's Theory of Evolution Hardy-Weinberg Law

Inductive vs. Deductive Reasoning

Feature Inductive Reasoning Deductive Reasoning
Direction Moves from specific → general Moves from general → specific
Example Shark is a fish. All fishes have scales. Therefore sharks also have scales. If Plasmodium is the cause of malaria, then all malaria patients should have Plasmodium in their blood.

Scientific Reasoning — Q&A

Q1. Why is theory a highly reliable scientific explanation?

Scientific theories are well tested and highly reliable scientific explanations of natural phenomena that unify many repeated observations and data collected from lots of experiments with authentic resources.

Q2. Why is a table or graph necessary for data organisation?

Data organisation requires the formation of a table or graph for the purpose of mathematical analysis, as the acceptance or rejection of a hypothesis is entirely based on the mathematical analysis of recorded data.

Q3. Why is experiment necessary for theory?

Experiments play an important role in order to testify theories and to provide a basis for scientific knowledge. Without experimental evidence, no hypothesis can become an accepted theory.
