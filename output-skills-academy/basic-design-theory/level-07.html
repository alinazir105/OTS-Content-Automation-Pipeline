Hierarchy:
Who's in Charge?

"Design without hierarchy is noise."

👁

When you look at a design for the first time — what do you see first?

Was that an accident? Or did a designer put it there on purpose? Every design has a pecking order. Someone decided what you see first, second, and last. That's visual hierarchy — and it's one of the most powerful tools in design.

Look Around You

Before we start — observe.

In the next 60 seconds, look around you — your phone screen, a book cover, a food packet, a poster on the wall. Pick one thing and ask these three questions.

1️⃣

What did you see first?

Your eye landed somewhere immediately. That element has the highest hierarchy. Why? Is it bigger? Brighter? Bolder?

2️⃣

What did you see second?

After the first hit, where did your eye travel? The designer planned this path — a visual journey through the information.

3️⃣

What did you almost miss?

That element has the lowest hierarchy. It's probably small, light, or tucked away. Was that the right decision?

🎯

Was there a clear leader?

Some designs have a strong star and supporting actors. Others try to make everything equal — and everything disappears.

The Concept

Visual Hierarchy — The 6 Tools

Hierarchy is not one thing — it's a toolkit. Designers use these six tools to tell your eye where to go, in what order, and why.

HEADLINE — BIG
Subheading — Medium
body text — small

Size & Scale

The bigger it is, the more important it appears. This is the most intuitive hierarchy tool — use it first, always.

H1 H2 Body Caption
BOLD draws the eye first.
Medium weight supports.
Light weight recedes into the background.

Weight & Style

Heavy (bold, black) weights shout. Light weights whisper. Mix them in the same font family to create clear levels without changing size.

Black 900 Bold 700 Light 300
Accent colour → PRIORITY
White — secondary importance
Muted grey — low priority

Colour & Contrast

A pop of your brand colour on one element creates instant hierarchy. The eye moves to the most contrasting element automatically.

Accent Primary Muted
Title Up Here
Small detail
Body content sits below the title with enough breathing room to feel secondary.

Position & Space

Top-left is prime real estate. Elements at the top get seen first. Generous white space around an element elevates its importance.

Top = First Space = Status

How Eyes Read: Z-Pattern & F-Pattern

Designers don't just use hierarchy tools in isolation — they guide the path the eye takes across the whole page. Two patterns dominate western reading.

Z-PATTERN

Used in advertising, posters, landing pages. Place the most important elements at the four points of the Z.

F-PATTERN

Used in articles, blogs, feeds. Readers read the top fully, then skim left edges. Put key info top-left and in first sentences.

In the Wild

Hierarchy in the Real World

Great hierarchy is everywhere — once you learn to see it. Here's where the principle lives in things you encounter every day.

Newspaper Front Page

The headline is enormous. The deck is medium. The byline is tiny. No one needed to tell you what to read first.

Web Landing Pages

Hero headline → value statement → primary CTA button → secondary info. Your eye follows the hierarchy plan perfectly.

Karachi Rickshaw Art

The owner's name or blessing dominates the centre. Decorative borders frame without competing. Local design with global hierarchy rules.

Restaurant Menus

Dish name → description → price. The best menus make the dish name the star, not the price. Hierarchy guides appetite.
